🧠 PART 3 · LA GRAMÁTICA DE UN VISTAZO 11 min

The whole module on two tables and one note

First, the yo‑zco verbs. The trick is that they are regular in every form except yo, where the c becomes ‑zco:

Subjectconocer (to know / be familiar with)conducir (to drive)ofrecer (to offer)
yoconozcoconduzcoofrezco
túconocesconducesofreces
usted / él / ellaconoceconduceofrece
nosotros/asconocemosconducimosofrecemos
ustedes / ellos / ellasconocenconducenofrecen

Then the three everyday irregulars. Each has its own yo form; the rest are easy. Keep this as your quick-reference:

Subjectir (to go)dar (to give)ver (to see / watch)
yovoydoyveo
túvasdasves
usted / él / ellavadave
nosotros/asvamosdamosvemos
ustedes / ellos / ellasvandanven

Remember ir a + infinitive for what's coming up: Voy a jugar al fútbol. Vamos a ver el partido.

💡 conocer vs. saber (+ personal a)

saber = to know a fact, information, or how to do something (saber + infinitive): Sé nadar. ¿Sabes quién ganó?  conocer = to be familiar with a person, place, or thing: Conozco Panamá. Conocemos a la entrenadora. When the thing you know is a specific person, add the personal a: Conozco a Mariano. Veo a mis amigos en la cancha.

🌎 CULTURA · PANAMÁ
El béisbol, la pasión de un país puente

Panamá is a country built as a crossroads — the Canal de Panamá links two oceans and carries a large share of world trade past its capital every day. That crossroads spirit shows up in its favorite sport: while much of Latin America lives for fútbol, Panamá's national passion is béisbol, a game that arrived through the Canal Zone and never left. The country has sent a remarkable number of players to the U.S. major leagues for its size — among them Rod Carew, one of the greatest hitters in the game's history, and Mariano Rivera, widely considered the greatest relief pitcher ever. When you hear a Panamanian teen like Anaís say conozco a los grandes peloteros, she means players her whole country claims as family. (In the full course this pairs with a short clip of Panamanian sports radio in the Vocabulary Check.)

★ LA VIRTUD · PERSEVERANCE

Mariano Rivera (Panamá, b. 1969) grew up in the fishing village of Puerto Caimito, the son of a fisherman, playing ball with gloves made from cardboard and milk cartons. He signed for a tiny bonus and spent years in the minor leagues before becoming the most dominant closer baseball has ever seen — and in 2019 the first player ever voted into the Hall of Fame unanimously. He got there not through one gift but through perseverancia: the same pitch, thrown with the same care, thousands upon thousands of times. Language works the same way. The yo‑zco forms and voy / doy / veo that felt strange on Day 3 feel automatic now because you kept showing up. Bring that steadiness into the quiz — and into Module 4.
