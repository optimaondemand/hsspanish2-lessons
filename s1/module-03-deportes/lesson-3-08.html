★ Mi deporte favorito — en mi propia voz
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Everything you built this module now comes together — out loud, in your voice.
Today is a production day: no new grammar and no new words. You already own the sports-and-pastimes vocabulary and the module's verbs — the -zco group (conozco, conduzco, ofrezco) plus voy, doy, and veo. Now you'll put them together into a short, first-person presentation about your favorite sport or pastime and someone you know in it, rehearse it, and record it — that recording is your graded Tarea oral. We scaffold it step by step so that when you hit “record,” you already know exactly what you want to say.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…

I will be able to draft, rehearse, self-check, and record my Tarea oral to the presentational rubric — then reflect on my delivery.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

Warm up your voice before you record

A recording sounds far more confident when you've said the words out loud first. Read this line aloud twice, a little smoother each time: —Mi deporte favorito es el fútbol; los sábados voy a la cancha y veo los partidos. That one sentence already uses two of today's verbs. Now think about your own life before we build.

🎤 FONÉTICA · play before you record · the -zco sound
The trickiest sound in your verbs is the zc in the yo forms. In Latin American Spanish it's simply “s + k” — not one letter, but two clean sounds run together: co·NOS·co, con·DUS·co, o·FRES·co. Say each one back slowly, then at a natural pace — feel the little “k” land before the final -co.
🧠 PART 3 · TUS VERBOS DE HOY 7 min

The yo-forms your presentation leans on

These are the module's verbs — nothing new. Since your Tarea oral is all about you, the yo form is exactly what you need. The top three add a surprise -zco; the bottom three are the short, high-frequency voy, doy, veo. Click each one and say it aloud.

Verbyo formHear itUse it to say…
conocer (to know a person/place)conozcoConozco a mi entrenadora.
ofrecer (to offer)ofrezcoLe ofrezco ayuda a mi equipo.
conducir (to drive)conduzcoTodavía no conduzco al partido.
ir (to go)voyVoy a la cancha los sábados.
dar (to give)doyDoy un paseo en bicicleta.
ver (to see / watch)veoVeo los partidos con mi familia.

Two reminders for your recording: after conocer a person, add the “personal a” — conozco a mi entrenadora. And voy a + an activity means “I'm going to…”: voy a jugar, voy a ver el partido.

🧠 PART 4 · ARMA TU PRESENTACIÓN 12 min

Assemble the right sentence, then build your own lines

First, three quick checks so the verbs feel automatic. Each one asks you to pick the piece that makes the sentence correct. Then you'll draft your own presentation with the frames below.

✅ TRY IT · assemble the sentence
“Yo ___ a mi entrenador de fútbol; es una buena persona.” (I know my soccer coach…)
✅ TRY IT · assemble the sentence
“Este sábado ___ jugar un partido importante con mi equipo.” (This Saturday I'm going to play…)
✅ TRY IT · assemble the sentence
“Todos los domingos ___ los partidos de béisbol en la tele.” (Every Sunday I watch…)
✎ SENTENCE FRAMES · fill each one for yourself, then say it aloud
1. Mi deporte/pasatiempo favorito es _______, y normalmente voy a _______. (your sport + where you go)
2. Conozco a _______, que _______; los fines de semana veo _______. (someone you know + what you watch)
3. Este fin de semana voy a _______. (what you're going to do — voy a + activity)

Now say your whole presentation out loud — all three frames in a row, as one smooth paragraph. When you're happy with it, reveal a full model to compare.

Me llamo Daniela y mi deporte favorito es el fútbol. Normalmente voy a la cancha del parque y practico con mi equipo. Conozco a mi entrenadora, la señora Ríos, que siempre nos ofrece buenos consejos. Los fines de semana veo los partidos en la tele con mi familia. Este sábado voy a jugar un partido importante. — Yours will use your own sport, person, and plans; the frame is what matters.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 6 min

A student's model — read it, then borrow its shape

Mateo tiene dieciséis años y vive en la Ciudad de Panamá. Él presenta su pasatiempo así:

«Me llamo Mateo. Mi deporte favorito es el béisbol. Todos los sábados voy al parque con mi equipo y practicamos toda la mañana. Conozco a mi entrenador, el señor Justavino, que juega desde niño y siempre nos ofrece buenos consejos. Cuando no juego, veo los partidos de las Grandes Ligas en la tele, porque muchos jugadores panameños famosos empezaron como yo. Este domingo voy a jugar la final — ¡quiero ganar!»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find Mateo's voy, conozco, ofrece, and veo. Notice how short his sentences are — you do not need long, complicated Spanish to score well. Where does he use the personal a, and why? (Because he knows a person: conozco a mi entrenador.)
🎤 GRADED · TAREA ORAL (20 pts) · presentational speaking
Record: “Mi deporte favorito y alguien que conozco”

Record a 45–75 second spoken presentation about your favorite sport or pastime and someone you know in it. Speak in the first person and use, correctly and at least once each, conozco (a…), voy a…, and veo… Submit the recording in the Tarea oral — Módulo 3 assignment in Canvas.

Before you record:
• Fill in the three frames from Part 4 with your real life.
• Say the whole thing aloud twice — once slowly, once at a natural pace.
• Record in a quiet spot. It's fine to glance at your notes, but don't read word-for-word — speak.
Run through this list out loud before your final take:
• Did I say my favorite sport/pastime in the first sentence?
• Did I use conozco a + a person (with the personal a)?
• Did I use voy a + an activity (something I'm going to do)?
• Did I use veo + something I watch or follow?
• Is the -zco sound clean (“s + k”) in conozco / ofrezco?
• Am I between 45 and 75 seconds, speaking clearly — not rushing?

🌎 CULTURA · PANAMÁ
El béisbol — la pasión nacional

In Panamá, the favorite pastime isn't fútbol — it's béisbol. Kids play béisbol callejero (street baseball) in neighborhoods and coastal towns, sometimes with a broomstick and a taped-up ball, and the country of just over four million people has sent dozens of players to the Major Leagues. The legend Rod Carew and the great Mariano Rivera both grew up here, and every child who says «voy a la cancha» is stepping into that tradition. When you talk about your own sport, you're joining a conversation Panamanians have every single day. (In the full course this pairs with a short clip of a street-baseball game in Panamá.)

★ LA VIRTUD · PERSEVERANCE

Mariano Rivera (Panamá, b. 1969) grew up in the small fishing village of Puerto Caimito, playing baseball with a glove made from a milk carton. He signed for just a few thousand dollars, spent years working his way up, and reinvented his whole game around a single pitch when his career nearly stalled. He became the greatest closer in baseball history — and in 2019 the first player ever voted into the Hall of Fame unanimously. Él nunca se rinde. His story is perseverancia in one word: showing up, day after day, and improving one small thing at a time — exactly what re-recording your Tarea oral until it's right asks of you.
