★ Los pasatiempos — What You Do in Your Free Time
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you named the sports. Today we open up everything else people do to relax.
In Lesson 3.01 you built Vocab Set A — the sports (el fútbol, el básquetbol, jugar, practicar). Today you add Vocab Set B: the quieter, everyday pasatiempos — chess, drawing, cooking, shopping, playing an instrument. There is no new grammar here. Instead, you'll slot these new words into frames you already own from Spanish 1 — especially me gusta + infinitive, and the verbs jugar and practicar — so that by the end you can say what you like to do, and how often.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name at least eight leisure pastimes in Spanish (el ajedrez, dibujar, cocinar, ir de compras…).
2
I will be able to say what I like to do in my free time using the known frame me gusta + infinitive.
3
I will be able to say how often I do an activity using a menudo, a veces, and nunca.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

Not everything is a sport

Think about a rainy Saturday — no game, no practice. What do you actually do? Maybe you draw, cook, play a video game, or go to the mall. Those are pasatiempos (pastimes / hobbies), and Spanish has warm, everyday words for all of them. You already know the frame to talk about them: me gusta… Read this aloud: En mi tiempo libre, me gusta cocinar. You just made a full sentence with a brand-new word.

On a free afternoon with nothing scheduled, which of these sounds most like you?

⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Go back through the list and sort the pastimes into two piles out loud: divertido (fun, for you) or aburrido (boring, for you). Say each as a mini-sentence: El ajedrez es divertido. Ir de compras es aburrido. You're already reacting in Spanish.
🧠 PART 3 · FRAMES YOU ALREADY OWN 8 min

New words, familiar structures

Nothing new to learn here — just three frames you already carry from Spanish 1 and Lesson 3.01. Drop today's pastimes straight into them:

Known frameUse it forExample with Vocab B
me gusta + infinitivesaying what you like to doMe gusta dibujar. / Me gusta cocinar.
jugar a + gamegames you playJuego al ajedrez los fines de semana.
tocar + instrumentplaying an instrumentToco la guitarra en mi tiempo libre.

Careful with two English “plays”: you juego a game (jugar) but you toco an instrument (tocar). And jugar a + el becomes al → jugar al ajedrez.

Saying how often

Add a frequency word to tell your reader how regular the pastime is. A menudo and a veces sit comfortably at the start of the sentence; nunca goes right before the verb.

How oftenExample
a menudo (often)A menudo cocino los fines de semana.
a veces (sometimes)A veces juego al ajedrez con mi abuelo.
nunca (never)Nunca dibujo, pero me gusta la música.

Slot the new words into the frames

✅ TRY IT · how often?
Which sentence means “I never cook”?

Your turn: say “On weekends I like to go shopping and cook.” Say it out loud first — using a frame from Part 3 — then reveal a model.

Los fines de semana me gusta ir de compras y cocinar. The frame is me gusta + infinitive, and you can chain two infinitives with y. Los fines de semana at the front tells when.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

«Hola, me llamo Valeria y vivo en la Ciudad de Panamá. En mi tiempo libre tengo muchos pasatiempos. A menudo dibujo en mi cuaderno, y los fines de semana me gusta cocinar con mi mamá. Mi hermano y yo jugamos al ajedrez — ¡es muy divertido! A veces vamos de compras al centro. Nunca toco un instrumento, pero me gusta escuchar música típica.»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Which pastime does Valeria do a menudo, which on the fines de semana, and which does she nunca do? Notice she reuses the same frames you just practiced — me gusta + infinitive and jugar al.
🌎 CULTURA · PANAMÁ
El dominó: el pasatiempo de la esquina

In Panamá, one of the most beloved pasatiempos isn't a screen or a stadium — it's a small table on the sidewalk and a set of dominoes (el dominó). On weekends, in neighborhoods across Panama City and the countryside, you'll hear the sharp click of tiles as friends and neighbors play for hours, laughing and talking. Like chess, it's a game of patience and strategy, and it turns free time into community time. When a Panamanian says los fines de semana me gusta jugar, this steady, sociable rhythm is often what they picture. (In the full course this pairs with a short video of a neighborhood dominó game.)

★ LA VIRTUD · PERSEVERANCE

Mariano Rivera (Panamá, b. 1969) grew up in the fishing village of Puerto Caimito, playing baseball with a glove made from a milk carton. He turned one pitch — the cutter — into the finest closing career in the sport's history through years of patient, daily repetition, and in 2019 became the first player ever elected to the Hall of Fame unanimously. Whether a pastime is a sport or a game of ajedrez, the lesson is the same: skill is built by the person who keeps showing up. La perseverancia — steady effort over time — is exactly what turns a hobby into mastery.
