★ Tres verbos pequeños, un solo secreto: voy, doy, veo
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Three tiny verbs do enormous work — and they all hide their irregularity in exactly the same place.
Yesterday you met the yo -zco verbs (conozco, conduzco, ofrezco) — verbs that are perfectly regular except in the yo form. Today's three verbs, ir (to go), dar (to give), and ver (to see/watch), follow the same instinct: the yo form is where all the surprise lives — voy, doy, veo. We'll stay entirely inside the sports and pastimes vocabulary you already own, and end with a sneak peek at ir a + infinitive, your key to talking about what you're going to do.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to conjugate ir, dar, and ver in the present tense, including their irregular yo forms (voy, doy, veo).
2
I will be able to explain how the yo-form pattern of these verbs mirrors the -zco verbs (conozco, conduzco, ofrezco) from yesterday.
3
I will be able to preview a near-future plan with ir a + infinitive using pastime verbs (voy a jugar, vamos a nadar).
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

You already know the pattern — you just met it under another name

Think back to yesterday. A verb like conocer behaves like any normal -er verb (conoces, conoce, conocemos, conocen) — until you reach yo, where it swerves: conozco. That single-form swerve is the most common shape of irregularity in Spanish. Today's verbs do the very same thing, just with different endings. Before you see the tables, make a prediction.

The pastime phrases that are ir, dar, and ver

No new words today — these are all Deportes y pasatiempos expressions you already met this module. But notice: three of your favorite pastimes are literally built on today's verbs. Click the 🔊 and say each one aloud.

💡 THE THROUGH-LINE WITH YESTERDAY

Look at the yo forms in gold — voy, doy, veo — and set them beside yesterday's conozco, conduzco, ofrezco. Different endings, same lesson: Spanish loads its irregularity into the yo form and lets the other people stay predictable. Learn the yo form on purpose, and the rest of the verb almost builds itself. (Two small notes: ver simply keeps its full e everywhere — veo, ves, ve — and ir is the wild one, borrowing its shapes from an old cousin: voy, vas, va.)

👀 VISTAZO · A PREVIEW YOU'LL USE ALL YEAR

Here's the bonus. Take any form of ir, add a, then a verb in its plain -ar / -er / -ir (infinitive) form, and you've just said what someone is going to do. It's the easiest way to talk about the near future — no new tense required.

ir + a + infinitive:
• Voy a jugar al fútbol. → I'm going to play soccer.
• Vamos a nadar este fin de semana. → We're going to swim this weekend.
• ¿Vas a ver el partido? → Are you going to watch the game?

Notice you conjugate only ir; the pastime verb stays untouched in the infinitive. We'll practice this frame properly later this week.

📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Un fin de semana en la Ciudad de Panamá

Me llamo Daniela y vivo en la Ciudad de Panamá. Los sábados yo voy al parque con mi familia y doy un paseo por la Cinta Costera, junto al mar. Por la tarde mis hermanos ven un partido de béisbol en la televisión — aquí el béisbol es muy popular. Yo prefiero nadar, así que voy a la piscina. El domingo mi amiga Sofía y yo vamos a montar en bicicleta y después vamos a ver una película en su casa. ¡Me encanta el fin de semana!

🌎 CULTURA · PANAMÁ
El béisbol: el pasatiempo nacional de Panamá

In much of Latin America soccer is king, but in Panamá the national pastime is el béisbol. Panamanians pack stadiums for the amateur national league, and the country has sent an outsized number of players to the U.S. major leagues — most famously Mariano Rivera, born in the fishing village of Puerto Caimito, who became the greatest relief pitcher in baseball history and, in 2019, the first player ever elected to the Hall of Fame by a unanimous vote. When Daniela's brothers ven un partido on Saturday, they're taking part in a shared national passion. (In the full course this pairs with a short clip of a Panamanian béisbol broadcast for listening.)

★ LA VIRTUD · PERSEVERANCE

Mariano Rivera (Panamá, b. 1969) grew up in the fishing village of Puerto Caimito with a milk-carton glove and no real coaching, yet he returned to the mound day after day until one pitch became unhittable. His story is la perseverancia in motion: talent opens the door, but it's the willingness to repeat the small thing correctly, over and over, that carries you through it. The same is true of these three little verbs — say voy, doy, veo out loud enough times and they become yours for good.
