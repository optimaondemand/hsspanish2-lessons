★ ¿A quién conoces? — conocer y la a personal
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you met conozco. Today you point it at real people and places — and learn the tiny word that comes before a person.
Nothing brand-new today — no new grammar point and no new vocabulary. We take conocer (the -zco verb from Lesson 3.03) and put it to work with the sports and pastime words you built in Lessons 3.01–3.02. Two ideas do all the work: (1) when what you know is a specific person, Spanish slips a little «a» in front of it — the a personal — but a place or a thing takes no a; and (2) conocer (to be familiar with a person or place) is a different kind of “know” from saber (to know a fact, or to know how to do something). Then you'll read a Panamanian teen describe who and what he knows in the world of sports.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…

2

3
I will be able to read a short paragraph by a Panamanian teen and find conocer, saber, and the personal a inside it.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Two ways to “know”

English uses one word, know, for three very different things: knowing a person or place (I know her; I know Panama City), knowing a fact (I know the score), and knowing how to do something (I know how to swim). Spanish splits that job between two verbs — conocer and saber — and you already met both in Spanish 1. Today we sharpen the line between them, in the world of sports.

🧠 PART 3 · CONOCER + LA A PERSONAL 9 min

The verb you know — now with a person in front

First, a quick look back at conocer from Lesson 3.03. Only the yo form is unusual — the -zco ending — and the rest are regular -er endings.

SubjectconocerEjemplo (deportes)
yoconozcoYo conozco a un jugador de fútbol.
túconoces¿Conoces a la entrenadora?
usted / él / ellaconoceElla conoce el estadio muy bien.

📌 LA REGLA · THE PERSONAL A

When the direct object — the person or thing you know, see, or visit — is a specific person, Spanish puts a small «a» right before it. This a has no English translation; you just have to remember it's there.

✅ Person → a: Conozco a Mariano. / Conozco a mi entrenador. / Veo a los jugadores.
❌ Place or thing → no a: Conozco Panamá. / Conozco el estadio. / Conozco las reglas.

Second, keep conocer apart from its cousin saber. They both become “know” in English, but they don't do the same job:

VerboSe usa para… (use it for…)Ejemplo (deportes)
conocerbeing familiar with a person or a placeConozco a la campeona. / Conozco el estadio.
saberknowing a fact, or knowing how (saber + infinitive)Sé las reglas. / Sé jugar al fútbol.

Quick test: if you could swap in “be familiar with” (a person/place), use conocer; if you could swap in “know a fact” or “know how to,” use saber. And remember: the personal a rides with conocer only when a person follows — never before las reglas, el estadio, or Panamá.

Conozco a mi entrenadora de voleibol y sé jugar al voleibol. Notice the two different “knows”: conozco a mi entrenadora (a person — so the personal a appears) and sé jugar (know how to — saber + infinitive, no a). Yours just needs your real coach, teammate, or player and something you can do.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 6 min

Mateo y el béisbol

«¡Hola! Me llamo Mateo y soy de la Ciudad de Panamá. Mi pasatiempo favorito es el béisbol. Conozco a muchos jugadores del equipo de mi barrio, y también conozco a un entrenador muy bueno que me ayuda cada semana. No conozco a Mariano Rivera en persona, pero conozco su historia muy bien: sé que él es de Panamá y sé lanzar la pelota gracias a sus videos. Los sábados ofrezco mi ayuda en el estadio; reparto el agua y limpio el campo. Mi tío conduce la camioneta del equipo a cada partido. Conozco bien el estadio y conozco casi toda la ciudad, pero todavía no conozco todo mi país. ¡Algún día quiero conocer las islas de Panamá también!»

✅ COMPRENSIÓN · ¿a quién conoce?
According to the text, whom does Mateo know personally?
✅ COMPRENSIÓN · la a personal
Mateo writes “conozco a un entrenador” but “conozco el estadio” — no a. Why the difference?
⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Hunt for the -zco verbs Mateo uses: conozco (several times), ofrezco, and his uncle's conduce (from conducir). Circle every personal a — is there always a person right after it? And find his two sé forms: one is a fact, one is a know-how. Which is which?
🌎 CULTURA · PANAMÁ
El béisbol, la pasión nacional

While much of Latin America lives for fútbol, in Panamá the deepest sporting love is béisbol. The game arrived with workers building the Panama Canal over a century ago and became the country's national pastime, played in dusty barrio fields and packed stadiums alike. Panama has sent stars to the U.S. major leagues for generations — most famously Mariano Rivera, from the fishing town of Puerto Caimito, widely considered the greatest relief pitcher in baseball history and the first player ever elected to the Hall of Fame by a unanimous vote. When Mateo says he conoce the story of Mariano and sabe how to throw thanks to his videos, he speaks for a whole country that treats its ballplayers as national heroes. (In the full course this pairs with a short clip of Panamanian béisbol radio commentary.)

★ LA VIRTUD · PERSEVERANCE

Mariano Rivera (Panamá, b. 1969) grew up in the fishing village of Puerto Caimito with almost no equipment, yet he built an entire Hall-of-Fame career on one pitch, thrown with the same motion thousands upon thousands of times until it was automatic. That is exactly the shape of conocer today: one small irregularity — the yo form, conozco — repeated deliberately until it stops feeling strange. La perseverancia is not a single dramatic breakthrough; it is Rivera's cutter and your -zco ending, practiced the same patient way.
