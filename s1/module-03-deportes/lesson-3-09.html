🧠 PART 3 · DOS REGLAS, UN VISTAZO 8 min

The whole module on one screen

Before you read, take a slow look at the two grammar points the reading leans on. Nothing here is new — it's a refresher you can glance back at while you read Part 5.

1 · the -zco yo-verbs — only yo is the surprise

Verbs ending in -cer / -cir after a vowel add a z only in the yo form: -zco. Every other form is regular.

Subjectconocerconducirofrecer
yoconozcoconduzcoofrezco
túconocesconducesofreces
usted / él / ellaconoceconduceofrece
nosotros/asconocemosconducimosofrecemos
ustedes / ellos / ellasconocenconducenofrecen

2 · three everyday irregulars — ir, dar, ver

Subjectirdarver
yovoydoyveo
túvasdasves
usted / él / ellavadave
nosotros/asvamosdamosvemos
ustedes / ellos / ellasvandanven

Quick trick for the reading: whenever you see a yo form, ask “is this a -zco verb or ir/dar/ver?” If it means go, give, or see, it's voy / doy / veo; if it ends in -zco, only the yo changed — the rest of the verb is regular.

📖 PART 5 · A LEER · EL SALTO DE COLÓN AL PODIO 8 min

Read it once for the gist, then again for the details. Every verb is present tense, and the bold words are your -zco verbs and ir / dar / ver doing real work — each time you hit one, name the verb and the subject. Harder words are glossed in parentheses.

¿Conoces a Irving Saladino? En Panamá casi todos conocen su nombre: es el primer atleta panameño que gana una medalla de oro olímpica. Nace en Colón, una ciudad del Caribe, en 1983. De niño practica muchos deportes, pero su especialidad es el salto largo (long jump).

El camino no es fácil. Saladino entrena en pistas (tracks) muy pobres y a veces no tiene buen equipo. Un día, un entrenador brasileño ve su talento y le ofrece entrenar en Brasil. Saladino acepta: deja (leaves) su país, su familia y sus amigos para perseguir (to chase) un sueño (dream).

En una entrevista (interview), Saladino explica su rutina: «Cada mañana voy al gimnasio y doy lo mejor de mí. Conozco mis límites, pero también los empujo (I push them). No conduzco un carro de lujo (luxury car) ni busco fama; ofrezco trabajo, disciplina y paciencia, día tras día.»

En 2008, en los Juegos Olímpicos de Pekín, Saladino salta 8,34 metros y gana la medalla de oro. Es un momento histórico: por primera vez, Panamá ve a uno de sus hijos en lo más alto del podio (podium). Hoy muchos jóvenes panameños van a las pistas con el mismo sueño, porque Saladino demuestra (shows) que la perseverancia da frutos (bears fruit).

🌎 CULTURA · PANAMÁ
Un puente entre dos mares — y entre dos mundos

Panamá is a slender isthmus (istmo) — the narrow neck of land that joins North and South America — and its famous Canal lets ships cross between the Atlantic and Pacific without sailing around an entire continent. That crossroads spirit runs through Panamanian culture: it is a meeting point of Indigenous, African, and Spanish traditions. On the Caribbean coast, the Guna people are renowned for the mola — a hand-stitched panel made by layering several colors of cloth and cutting them away (reverse appliqué) to reveal birds, fish, and bold geometric designs. A single mola can take weeks of patient stitching — the same perseverance an athlete pours into training.

— UNA MOLA GUNA —
Capas (layers): varias telas de colores cosidas a mano, una sobre otra
Motivos (motifs): pájaros, peces, tortugas y figuras geométricas
Uso: panel frontal de la blusa tradicional de la mujer guna

Panama also wears its athletes with pride — from baseball great Mariano Rivera to boxing legend Roberto Durán — but Irving Saladino's 2008 gold was the country's very first, a leap the whole isthmus felt.

★ LA VIRTUD · PERSEVERANCE

Mariano Rivera (Panamá, b. 1969) embodies la perseverancia — the virtue of continuing steadily even when progress is slow and no one is watching. There was no shortcut from a milk-carton glove in Puerto Caimito to Cooperstown; there was only the next pitch, thrown well, thousands of times over nineteen seasons. Notice how the same virtue shows up in this week's reading and culture review: perseverance isn't a burst of effort the night before the quiz — it's the quiet, repeated work of reviewing a little each day, redoing the reps you miss, and showing up again tomorrow. That habit, more than any single quiz, is what carries you forward.
