★ ¿Qué deporte eres tú?
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Everyone belongs to some team, court, track, or pool — today you name yours in Spanish.
Welcome to Módulo 3: Deportes y pasatiempos. This week is about how the Spanish-speaking world plays. Today there's no new grammar — we reactivate what you already own (the present tense and gustar) and load in a fresh set of words: the core deportes. You'll listen, say each one back, and start talking about which sports you like and play. Sport is also our doorway into a country that punches far above its size — Panamá — and into this module's virtue, perseverance.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to recognize and say the core sports vocabulary (el fútbol, el béisbol, el baloncesto, la natación, el ciclismo…).
2
I will be able to reuse gustar and the present tense of jugar (a) and practicar to say which sports I like and do.
3
I will be able to explain why Panamanians are so passionate about sport — and how a sport trains perseverance.

Switch on what you already have

You don't start this module from zero. You already know how to say what you like — me gusta — and how to say what you do in the present tense. Read this aloud: Me gusta el deporte. Practico un deporte y juego con amigos. You understood almost all of it, right? Today we just aim that toolkit at a new topic.

🔄 PART 3 · REPASO · DECIR QUÉ TE GUSTA 8 min

Old tools, new words

Nothing new here — just a reminder of the two frames you already own, now pointed at sports. Use gustar to say what you like, and the present-tense verbs jugar (a) and practicar to say what you do. (Full -zco verbs and ir/dar/ver arrive later this module — not today.)

Frame you already knowWith a sportEnglish
me gusta + singularMe gusta el fútbol.I like soccer.
me gusta + infinitiveMe gusta nadar.I like to swim.
jugar a (u→ue, from M2)Yo juego al baloncesto.I play basketball.
practicar (regular -ar)Yo practico la natación.I do / practice swimming.

Quick reminder: jugar pairs with a + the sport, and a + el contracts to al — juego al fútbol. Practicar takes the sport directly — practico el ciclismo. Both are review from earlier modules.

📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

«Hola, me llamo Valeria y soy de la Ciudad de Panamá. En mi familia todos somos fanáticos del deporte. A mi papá le gusta el béisbol — aquí es casi una religión. Mi hermano juega al fútbol en un equipo de la escuela y nunca se pierde un partido. Yo prefiero la natación: practico tres veces por semana. A veces mi equipo gana y a veces pierde, pero eso no importa — lo importante es seguir entrenando.»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Which sport does each person like? Find the two verbs that go with a ball game (jugar a) versus the one Valeria says with practicar. And notice her last line — win or lose, seguir entrenando (keep training). That's the module virtue in one sentence.
🌎 CULTURA · PANAMÁ
Un país pequeño, una potencia deportiva

Panamá is a narrow country of about 4.4 million people — famous for the Canal de Panamá, where ships cross between two oceans — yet it produces world-class athletes far beyond its size. El béisbol is the national passion: Panamá has sent dozens of players to the U.S. Major Leagues, including the legendary pitcher Mariano Rivera and Hall-of-Famer Rod Carew. And in 2008, long-jumper Irving Saladino won Panamá's first-ever Olympic gold medal. For many Panamanians, saying me gusta el béisbol isn't a hobby — it's part of who they are. (In the full course this pairs with a short clip of a Panamanian league game and a map of the Canal.)

★ LA VIRTUD · PERSEVERANCE

Mariano Rivera (Panamá, b. 1969) grew up in a poor fishing village, Puerto Caimito, playing baseball with a glove made from a milk carton. He was signed for a tiny sum, spent years in the minor leagues, and even tore a knee ligament at age 42 — then came back to pitch again. In 2019 he became the first player ever voted into the Baseball Hall of Fame unanimously. His story is perseverancia in one word: showing up, training, and getting back up after every loss. A sport teaches exactly that — a veces ganas, a veces pierdes, but you keep training.
