🧠 PART 3 · LOS DOS GRUPOS DE VERBOS, EN FOCO 8 min

Two verb families to lock in

Nothing new — just the two habits that make these verbs sound right. Family one: the -zco verbs. Verbs ending in -cer or -cir change only in the yo form, which grows a z: conocer → conozco. Every other form is completely regular. Here is conocer (“to know / be familiar with”):

SubjectconocerExample
yoconozco Yo conozco a los jugadores del equipo.
túconoces (regular)¿Conoces un buen club de ajedrez?
usted / él / ellaconoce (regular)Ella conoce el estadio.
nosotros/asconocemos (regular)Nosotros conocemos la cancha.
ustedes / ellos / ellasconocen (regular)Ellos conocen a la campeona.

The other two work exactly the same way — only the yo form is special:

  • conducir → conduzcoto drive — Yo conduzco al partido.
  • ofrecer → ofrezcoto offer — Yo ofrezco llevar el balón.

Family two: ir, dar, and ver. No rule to derive — you simply memorize them. Notice the three yo forms all end in a way you can hear: voy, doy, veo.

Subjectir (to go)dar (to give)ver (to see / watch)
yovoy doy veo
túvasdasves
usted / él / ellavadave
nosotros/asvamosdamosvemos
ustedes / ellos / ellasvandanven

Rule of thumb for this module: for conocer / conducir / ofrecer, only the yo form is odd (-zco) — everything else is regular. For ir / dar / ver, learn all five forms as a set. And remember the personal a: when you know a person, use conocer a — Conozco a Mariano.

The graded task asks for 4–5 connected sentences about your sports and pastimes, using at least one form of conocer and the verbs ir, dar, and ver. Draft yours out loud or on paper first — then reveal a model to compare.

En mi tiempo libre conozco muchos deportes, pero mi favorito es el béisbol. Los sábados voy al parque con mis amigos y jugamos un partido en la cancha. Después damos un paseo y comemos algo. Por la noche veo los partidos profesionales en la tele. También conozco a varios jugadores famosos de Panamá.

Notice the pattern the graders look for: conocer used correctly (conozco, with the personal a before people), the three little irregulars in action (voy, damos, veo), sports and pastime vocabulary woven in (el béisbol, el partido, la cancha, dar un paseo), and connectors holding it together (después, también, por la noche). 🌎 CULTURA · PANAMÁ
El béisbol, el deporte del istmo

Ask a Panamanian what sport the country loves and the answer often comes back the same: el béisbol. Brought to the isthmus more than a century ago along the railroad and the Canal zone, baseball became a national passion, and tiny Panamá — barely four million people — has sent an astonishing number of players to the U.S. major leagues. The most famous is Mariano Rivera, from the fishing village of Puerto Caimito, considered the greatest relief pitcher in history and the first player ever elected to the Hall of Fame by a unanimous vote. Panamanians also cheer boxing champions like Roberto Durán and, on any given afternoon, gather to ver un partido in the plaza or dar un paseo along the Cinta Costera, the seaside walkway in the capital that looks out over the ships waiting to cross the Canal. When you say conozco a un jugador panameño, you're naming a country that punches far above its size. (In the full course this pairs with a short clip of a Panamanian baseball broadcast inside the Vocabulary Check.)

★ LA VIRTUD · PERSEVERANCE

Mariano Rivera (Panamá, b. 1969) grew up in Puerto Caimito, a fishing village where his father captained a boat. There was no money for equipment, so young Mariano made gloves from cardboard and milk cartons and used a stick for a bat. He was signed as an unremarkable prospect and, early on, threw a pitch so ordinary that scouts nearly gave up on him — until he discovered, almost by accident, the cut fastball that would make him unhittable. He then threw essentially that one pitch, refined over and over for nineteen seasons, until he retired as the most dominant closer the game has ever seen. His story is la perseverancia in its purest form: not a single dramatic breakthrough, but the patient, daily repetition of one thing done well. The same is true of language — today you didn't learn anything new; you practiced what you already had, one more time, a little sharper. That is exactly how mastery is built.
