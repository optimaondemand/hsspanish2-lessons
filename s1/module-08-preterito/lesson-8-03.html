★ El pretérito regular: verbos en -ar
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Every language needs a way to close a door on the present — Spanish just closed it with an accent mark.
All year you've lived in the present tense — what you do, what's happening, what's true. Today that changes. You're getting the pretérito, the tense Spanish uses to report a finished action — something that happened and is over. We start with the easiest, most common family: regular -ar verbs. Once you can say hablé, tomé, and bailé, you can already say most of what you did yesterday.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to conjugate regular -ar verbs in the pretérito (yo, tú, usted/él/ella, nosotros, ustedes/ellos/ellas).
2
I will be able to use time expressions like ayer, anoche, and la semana pasada to anchor an action firmly in the past.
3
I will be able to describe at least three things I did yesterday or last week, using regular -ar pretérito verbs.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

You already narrate — just not yet in Spanish

Think about the last thing you told a friend about your day. You didn't say “I eat breakfast” — you said “I ate breakfast.” English quietly changes the verb (eat → ate) to show the action is done. Spanish does the same job, but it changes the ending instead — and often adds an accent mark. That small mark is doing real work: it tells your listener this already happened.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Before we start: what's one thing you did yesterday that you could imagine describing in Spanish by the end of today?
📚 PART 2 · REPASO · MARCADORES DE TIEMPO 10 min

The words that signal “this is finished”

You met these time markers earlier in this module. Click 🔊 and say each one back — they'll appear constantly once you start using the pretérito, because they're what tells everyone when the finished action happened.

  • ayeryesterday
  • anochelast night
  • esta mañanathis morning (already past)
  • la semana pasadalast week
  • el fin de semana pasadolast weekend
  • el mes pasadolast month
  • hace dos díastwo days ago
  • yaalready
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Say three of these markers in a row, oldest to most recent: el mes pasado → la semana pasada → ayer. Notice how each one gets closer to right now — that ordering is exactly how you'll organize a story later in this module.
🧠 PART 3 · EL PRETÉRITO REGULAR · VERBOS EN -AR 9 min

Drop the -ar, add the pretérito ending

Take any regular -ar verb you already know — hablar works well — drop the -ar, and add one of these five endings:

Subjecthablar → pretéritoExample
yohabléAyer hablé con mi abuela.
túhablaste¿Con quién hablaste anoche?
usted / él / ellahablóElla habló por una hora.
nosotros/ashablamosNosotros hablamos la semana pasada.
ustedes / ellos / ellashablaronEllos hablaron por teléfono.

Two things to watch: the accent marks on hablé and habló are not decoration — without them, hablo (I speak, right now) and habló (he/she spoke, finished) would look identical in writing. And the nosotros/as form, hablamos, is spelled exactly like the present tense — a time marker like ayer or la semana pasada is what tells your listener you mean the past. The same endings work for every regular -ar verb: estudiar → estudié, trabajar → trabajé, escuchar → escuché, mirar → miré, viajar → viajé, comprar → compré, bailar → bailé, tomar → tomé.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 9 min
✅ TRY IT · choose the yo form
“Anoche yo ___ con mi abuela por dos horas.” (Last night I talked with my grandmother for two hours.)
✅ TRY IT · choose the tú form
“Marta, ¿tú ___ la tarea anoche?” (Marta, did you finish the homework last night?) — verb: terminar
✅ TRY IT · choose the nosotros/as form
“Mis amigos y yo ___ toda la noche en la fiesta.” (My friends and I danced all night at the party.) — verb: bailar

Your turn: say one sentence about something you did yesterday or last week — a regular -ar verb + a time expression. Say it out loud, then reveal a model.

Ayer hablé con mi mejor amiga por una hora y luego estudié español. Yours will use your own verb and time marker — the frame is [marcador de tiempo] + yo [verbo-é].
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Cada lunes, mi amiga Sofía, que vive en Toledo, me manda un mensaje de voz para contarme su fin de semana. Este es el mensaje de ayer:

—¡Hola! Te cuento mi sábado. Por la mañana desayuné con mi familia y después ayudé a mi abuela en el jardín. Al mediodía, mi hermano y yo preparamos una paella — ¡mi primera vez! Por la tarde, escuché música y bailé un poco en mi cuarto (nadie me miró, ¡qué alivio!). Más tarde, mis amigas y yo caminamos por el centro de Toledo y tomamos un café cerca de la catedral. Compré un regalo pequeño para mi mamá. Por la noche, cenamos tarde, como siempre en España, y descansé bien. ¡Un sábado perfecto!

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find every verb ending in -é, -amos, and -ó. Which subject goes with each one? Notice that Sofía's message moves through her whole day using only the endings from Part 3.
🌎 CULTURA · ESPAÑA
Toledo, la ciudad de las tres culturas

Sofía's Toledo is where the message in today's reading takes place — and it's one of the most layered cities in Spain. For centuries, Christian, Jewish, and Muslim communities lived and built side by side there, leaving behind synagogues, mosques-turned-churches, and cathedrals within a few blocks of each other. Locals still call it la ciudad de las tres culturas (the city of the three cultures). Toledo didn't erase any of its histories to make room for the others — it layered them into one skyline. That habit of holding several traditions together, rather than choosing just one, is exactly the kind of synthesis this module's virtue is about. (In the full course this pairs with this semester's VR project, set in a Spanish-speaking market.)

★ LA VIRTUD · LAS SIETE, JUNTAS

Las siete, juntas — today you learn one small, repeatable pattern: drop -ar, add the pretérito ending. That kind of steady, rule-following practice is perseverancia in miniature, and using the new form to say only what truly happened is honestidad. Neither virtue does much alone; Módulo 8's capstone will ask you to combine all seven, the same way you're about to combine a stem and an ending into one true verb.
