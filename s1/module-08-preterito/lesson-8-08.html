📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You've built the forms — now put them to work telling your own story.
All week you've assembled the pieces of the pretérito: the regular endings and the -car/-gar/-zar spelling changes. Today is a synthesis day — you stop conjugating verbs in isolation and start narrating. You'll review the forms one more time, draft a short spoken account of what you did recently, and rehearse it out loud. That draft becomes the script for your Tarea oral recording.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to narrate recent past activities using regular pretérito verbs (-ar/-er/-ir) in the yo and tú forms.
2
I will be able to correctly apply the -car/-gar/-zar spelling changes in the yo form (busqué, llegué, empecé).
3
I will be able to draft and rehearse a short spoken narration of what I did, as the script for my Tarea oral recording.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

De la gramática a la historia

All week the pretérito has lived in tables and drills. Today it moves into a real sentence about your weekend. Before you look at anything else, think of one thing you did yesterday or over the weekend — just one verb, in your head, in Spanish.

📚 PART 2 · REPASO DE VOCABULARIO · VERBOS DE LA SEMANA 8 min

Escucha y repite — en pretérito

These are verbs you've already met this module, now in their yo-form pretérito. Click the 🔊 and say each one aloud — you'll want several of them ready for your own narration.

  • habléI talked (hablar)
  • estudiéI studied (estudiar)
  • trabajéI worked (trabajar)
  • compréI bought (comprar)
  • comíI ate (comer)
  • escribíI wrote (escribir)
  • salíI went out (salir)
  • busquéI looked for (buscar — c→qu)
  • lleguéI arrived (llegar — g→gu)
  • juguéI played (jugar — g→gu)
  • empecéI started (empezar — z→c)
  • saquéI took (out) (sacar — c→qu)
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Say all four spelling-change verbs in a row — busqué, llegué, jugué, empecé — and notice the pattern: only the yo form changes its spelling, and only so the sound stays the same as the infinitive.
🧠 PART 3 · REPASO RÁPIDO · EL PRETÉRITO REGULAR 8 min

One table, all the forms you need today

You built this table piece by piece earlier in the module. Here it is complete, as a reference while you draft — keep it open in another tab if that helps.

Subject-AR: hablar-ER: comer-IR: escribir

And the spelling-change family — only the yo form changes, and only in spelling (never in sound):

InfinitiveChangeyo form
buscarc → qubusqué
llegarg → gullegué
empezarz → cempecé

Every other form of these three verbs (tú, él/ella, nosotros, ellos) is completely regular — only yo needs the spelling fix, to protect the sound.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO · PREPARA TU TAREA ORAL 13 min
✅ TRY IT · regular -ar preterite
“Ayer yo ___ (estudiar) para el examen de historia.”
✅ TRY IT · regular -ir preterite
“Anoche yo ___ (escribir) un mensaje a mi amiga en España.”
✅ TRY IT · spelling-change verb (-car)
“El viernes yo ___ (buscar) información para mi proyecto sobre España.”

Now draft your script. Write (or say aloud) three to four sentences recounting what you did recently — use at least one regular verb and at least one -car/-gar/-zar verb. This is the script you'll rehearse and record for your Tarea oral. When you've drafted it, reveal a model to compare.

El sábado pasado busqué un restaurante en Madrid con mi familia. Comimos tapas y hablamos toda la noche. Después caminamos por el Parque del Retiro y saqué muchas fotos. ¡Fue un día perfecto! Notice the mix — busqué and saqué (spelling-change), comimos and hablamos and caminamos (regular). Yours will use your own weekend and your own verbs — the frame is simply one sentence per activity, in order.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

—¡Hola, Marta! ¿Qué tal el fin de semana?
—¡Genial! El sábado trabajé por la mañana y después quedé con mis amigas en el centro de Madrid.
—¿Y qué hiciste?
—Llegué tarde, como siempre, pero busqué un bar de tapas cerca de la Plaza Mayor. Comimos patatas bravas y hablamos durante horas — la sobremesa aquí nunca termina.
—¡Qué envidia! Yo empecé a estudiar el sábado y no salí para nada.
—Pues el próximo fin de semana, ¡sales con nosotras!

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find all four spelling-change verbs in Marta's lines. Then notice how differently the two friends spent their Saturday — that contrast is exactly the culture note below.
🌎 CULTURA · ESPAÑA
La sobremesa: cuando la conversación es la actividad

In Marta's story, “hablamos durante horas” isn't an exaggeration — it's a real Spanish institution called la sobremesa: the time spent talking at the table after a meal is finished, sometimes for an hour or more. Meal times themselves run later than in the U.S. — lunch (la comida) around 2:00–3:00 p.m. and dinner (la cena) as late as 9:00–10:00 p.m. — which is why Spaniards often quedan (meet up) with friends in the evening rather than after school. When you narrate your own weekend for your Tarea oral, notice which of your activities are solitary and which ones, like Marta's, are really about time spent with other people.

★ LA VIRTUD · LAS SIETE, JUNTAS

Las siete, juntas — speaking your own true story out loud, for Tarea oral, takes real valor (courage): the willingness to be heard even before every verb form is perfect. It also takes honestidad, telling what you actually did, and responsabilidad, showing up prepared to record it. None of Optima's seven virtues stands alone here — courage to speak, honesty to speak truly, and responsibility to follow through all have to work together, exactly the way Módulo 8 asks every grammar piece you've learned to work together in one spoken story.
