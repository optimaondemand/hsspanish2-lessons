📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Every language has a way of saying “that already happened.” Today you learn Spanish's flags for it.
Welcome to Módulo 8 — Lo que hice (what I did). All semester you've lived in the present tense. Starting now, you'll learn to step one day — or one week — backward and describe what already happened. That new tense is called the pretérito, and you'll meet its verb forms soon. But every past-tense story starts with a signal word that tells your listener when: ayer (yesterday), anoche (last night), la semana pasada (last week). Today's job is simply to learn those signal words — the flags — and to hear the difference between a flag that points backward and one that points at right now.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to explain, in English, what the pretérito is and why Spanish (like English) needs a separate way of talking about finished past actions.
2
I will be able to recognize and say common past-time markers: ayer, anoche, la semana pasada, el fin de semana pasado, hace dos días.
3
I will be able to sort a time marker correctly as pointing to the pasado (ayer…) or the presente (hoy, ahora, siempre).
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Same idea, new direction

In English, you already do this without thinking: “I watch a movie” (right now, in general) versus “I watched a movie” (already finished). Spanish makes that same jump — and the fastest way in is a signal word up front, telling your listener exactly when to point their mental clock.

📚 PART 2 · MARCADORES DE TIEMPO 11 min

The words that point backward

Click the 🔊 and repeat aloud. These twelve words are the flags you'll use all module — ten point to the past, and two (hoy, ahora) stay in the present as a reminder of the contrast.

  • ayeryesterday
  • anochelast night
  • esta mañanathis morning (earlier today)
  • ayer por la tardeyesterday afternoon
  • la semana pasadalast week
  • el fin de semana pasadolast weekend
  • el mes pasadolast month
  • hace un ratoa while ago
  • hace dos díastwo days ago
  • entoncesthen, at that time
  • hoytoday (presente)
  • ahoraright now (presente)

🌎 CULTURA · ESPAÑA
Los mercados de España

Across Spain, weekly open-air markets — mercadillos — still fill town plazas the way they have for centuries: fresh produce, cheeses, spices, and handmade goods laid out under awnings, with vendors calling out prices and regulars greeting each other by name. Madrid's El Rastro has run every Sunday since the 1700s; Barcelona's La Boqueria traces back to a medieval meat market outside the old city walls. This module you'll spend real time in that world — the semester's VR project sends you de compras en el mercado (shopping in the market) to practice exactly the past-tense sentences you're building toward now: what you bought, what you saw, what happened.

★ LA VIRTUD · LAS SIETE, JUNTAS

Las siete, juntas — Optima's seven virtues never work alone, and neither do today's time markers. Telling ayer from hoy takes the quiet discipline of autogobierno (self-government): noticing a small signal and responding correctly instead of guessing. It's the same discipline Módulo 8's capstone will ask of all seven virtues at once — each one a marker, together telling one true story.
