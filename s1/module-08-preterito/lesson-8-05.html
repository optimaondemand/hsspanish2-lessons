Lesson 8.05
★ Busqué, llegué, empecé: los cambios ortográficos
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Busqué, no buscé. Llegué, no llegé. Empecé, no empezé. Same verb, same yo — one small spelling shift.
You already know the regular pretérito endings from earlier in this módulo — -é, -aste, -ó, -amos, -aron for -ar verbs. Today you meet a pattern that isn't really an exception: verbs ending in -car, -gar, and -zar change one letter, but only in the yo form, so the word still sounds the way a Spanish reader expects. Every verb you'll use today — buscar, llegar, jugar, pagar, empezar — you already know from earlier in the course. The only thing that's new is what happens to its spelling when you say “I did it.”
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to explain why -car, -gar, and -zar verbs change spelling in the yo form of the pretérito — and only in that form.
2
I will be able to correctly write and say the yo pretérito form of common -car, -gar, and -zar verbs (busqué, llegué, empecé, and more).
3
I will be able to use these forms to describe something I really did recently — continuing this módulo's lo que hice thread.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

Listen for the sound, not just the letter

Say this out loud: llegar. Notice the hard “g” sound, like in gato. Now imagine writing “I arrived” by just swapping the ending: llegé. In Spanish spelling, a lone g before e sounds like the “h” in gente — completely different. Spanish keeps the sound of the verb stable by adjusting the spelling instead. That's the whole idea behind today's lesson.

📚 PART 2 · VERBOS QUE YA CONOCES 9 min

Same verbs, new yo form — listen and repeat

Every infinitive below is one you already use in the present tense. Click 🔊 to hear the new part — the yo pretérito form — and repeat it aloud.

  • buscar → busquéto look for → I looked for
  • llegar → lleguéto arrive → I arrived
  • jugar → juguéto play → I played
  • pagar → paguéto pay → I paid

  • comenzar → comencéto begin → I began

🧠 PART 3 · EL CAMBIO ORTOGRÁFICO 10 min

One letter changes, only for yo

The pretérito endings you already learned this módulo don't change at all — tú, él/ella, nosotros, ellos stay perfectly regular. Only the yo form needs a spelling adjustment, and only because plain -cé, -gé, and -zé would sound wrong in Spanish:

EndingChange (yo only)Infinitive → yoRest of pretérito (regular)
-carc → qubuscar → busquébuscaste, buscó, buscamos, buscaron
-garg → gullegar → lleguéllegaste, llegó, llegamos, llegaron
-zarz → cempezar → empecéempezaste, empezó, empezamos, empezaron

Why these three letters? In Spanish spelling, c and g sound soft before e/i (cerca, gente) but hard before a/o/u (casa, gato) — so -car and -gar verbs insert a silent u to keep the hard sound. And z almost never appears before e in Spanish, so -zar verbs simply switch to c. Same sound, adjusted spelling — every time, only in yo.

🌎 CULTURA · ESPAÑA
El Retiro y el arte del paseo

The Parque del Retiro, once a royal garden and now Madrid's best-loved public park, is where the reading's narrator plays fútbol and hears street musicians. Spaniards have long practiced el paseo — an unhurried evening walk through a park or plaza, meant for conversation rather than exercise. Sunday afternoons fill the Retiro's lake, rose garden, and wide avenues with families, friends, and performers — a rhythm of public life that has continued largely unchanged for over a century. When you describe what you did in Spain — where you walked, who you met, what you saw — you're describing exactly the kind of day this tradition is built for.

★ LA VIRTUD · LAS SIETE, JUNTAS

Las siete, juntas — sometimes one small piece changes shape so the whole system still works smoothly, exactly like busqué, llegué, and empecé, where a spelling shift keeps the sound consistent. Noticing that kind of small, careful adjustment takes autogobierno, the discipline to slow down instead of rushing past an exception. That's the same discipline the seven Optima virtues need when they adjust to fit a new situation without losing what makes each of them true.
