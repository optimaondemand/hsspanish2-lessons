📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Before you can say what you did, you need the words for what you do — and the words for when.
Module 8 is the gate module: by Day 10 you'll be able to narrate what you did using the pretérito. But grammar is only useful once you have something to say. Today, entirely in the present tense you already own, we build two toolkits: a set of everyday activity verbs (several of which will need a small spelling tweak once we conjugate them — more on that tomorrow), and a set of time expressions that point backward — ayer, anoche, la semana pasada. Tomorrow's lesson hands you the verb forms. Today is about stocking the shelf.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to recognize and use vocabulary for common recent activities — talking on the phone, playing a sport, studying, arriving home, and more.
2
I will be able to use time expressions like ayer, anoche, and la semana pasada to place an activity in the recent past and order events by how recent they are.
3
I will be able to spot which verbs belong to the -car / -gar / -zar family, so I'm ready for the spelling change tomorrow's grammar introduces.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Think in English first — then notice the gap

In English, “yesterday,” “last night,” and “last week” are just words — you don't need a different verb ending to use them. In Spanish, these time words are exactly what will let you use the pretérito correctly starting tomorrow. Today, just get comfortable spotting them.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Your friend texts you: “¡Anoche fue increíble!” (Last night was incredible!) Which part of that sentence tells you when?

⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +4 min
Say the four starred verbs in a row — jugar, tocar, practicar, llegar — and notice what they have in common: jugar ends in -gar; tocar and practicar end in -car; llegar ends in -gar too. That family is exactly what tomorrow's grammar lesson is built around.

🌎 CULTURA · ESPAÑA
El horario español: un día distinto

In much of Spain the clock runs differently than in the U.S.: lunch (la comida) is the biggest meal of the day, often eaten around 2:00 p.m., sometimes followed by a short rest (la siesta) before the afternoon continues; dinner (la cena) is light and eaten late, frequently after 9:00 p.m. That means a Spaniard describing “ayer” or “anoche” is often describing a day shaped around a very different rhythm than yours. This module you'll go deep on Spain — its regions, its rhythms, and eventually a VR project set there — so keep noticing details like this one.

★ LA VIRTUD · LAS SIETE, JUNTAS

Las siete, juntas means no single virtue carries a whole life story alone — just like no single expression here covers everything you did last week. Naming your own recent activities honestly takes honestidad, and building the habit of practicing new vocabulary every day takes perseverancia. By Módulo 8's capstone, all seven virtues will need to show up together, the same way today's whole list of expressions works together to describe one real week.
