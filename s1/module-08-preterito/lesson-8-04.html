📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You already know how to say “I talked.” Today you learn how to say “I ate,” “I wrote,” and “I lived.”
Yesterday you built the -ar preterite — hablé, estudié, escuché — to talk about completed actions. Today you extend that same idea to -er and -ir verbs, which share one identical set of endings. By the end of this lesson you'll be able to narrate a string of things you did — ate, wrote, left, received — entirely in the pretérito, and read a short narrative that mixes all three verb families.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to conjugate regular -er and -ir verbs in the pretérito across all subject pronouns.
2
I will be able to narrate completed past actions — what I ate, wrote, left, or received — using -er/-ir pretérito forms.
3
I will be able to read and understand a short narrative that mixes -ar, -er, and -ir pretérito verbs.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Bring back yesterday's -ar forms

Before you add a new piece, warm up the piece you already have. Say this aloud: Ayer hablé con mi amigo y escuché música. Notice both verbs end in -é for yo — that's the -ar pretérito signature you built yesterday.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Piensa en ayer. ¿Cuál hiciste? (Think about yesterday. Which did you do?)
📚 PART 2 · VERBOS -ER / -IR QUE YA CONOCES 8 min

Review, not new — you've used these infinitives before

These are all -er/-ir verbs from your earlier Spanish. Click 🔊 and say each infinitive aloud — today you'll put every one of them into the pretérito.

  • comerto eat
  • beberto drink
  • aprenderto learn
  • correrto run
  • escribirto write
  • vivirto live
  • salirto leave / go out
  • recibirto receive
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +4 min
Say comí (I ate) and escribí (I wrote) out loud. Both end in -í even though one is -er and one is -ir — that's the pattern Part 3 explains.
🧠 PART 3 · EL PRETÉRITO REGULAR: -ER / -IR 10 min

One set of endings, two verb families

Here's the good news: -er and -ir verbs take exactly the same pretérito endings. Learn one set, and you've learned both families at once.

Subjectcomerescribir
yocomíescribí
túcomisteescribiste
usted / él / ellacomióescribió
nosotros/ascomimosescribimos
ustedes / ellos / ellascomieronescribieron

Compare to -ar: -é, -aste, -ó, -amos, -aron. For -er/-ir it's -í, -iste, -ió, -imos, -ieron — note the í instead of é. One quirk: for -ir verbs, the nosotros form looks identical in the present and the pretérito (vivimos = we live / we lived). For -er verbs it does not (comemos we eat vs. comimos we ate) — context tells you which one is meant.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 10 min

✅ TRY IT · choose the right form
“Mis amigos ___ (escribir) una carta para la clase de inglés.”
✅ TRY IT · ask the question
“¿Tú ___ (salir) temprano de la escuela ayer?”

Your turn: write two sentences about yesterday — one with an -er verb, one with an -ir verb, both in the pretérito. Say them out loud, then reveal a model.

Anoche comí pizza con mis amigos y escribí un mensaje a mi abuela. Yours will use your own verbs and details — the frame is just [yo-form]… y [yo-form]…
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 6 min

El domingo pasado, mi familia y yo visitamos el Rastro, el mercado callejero más famoso de Madrid. Caminamos por las calles llenas de puestos coloridos. Yo compré una bufanda (scarf) de lana, y mi hermana compró unos aretes (earrings) de plata. Comimos churros con chocolate en un puesto pequeño y bebimos limonada fría. Mi padre habló con un vendedor (vendor) sobre la historia de un reloj antiguo, y todos aprendimos mucho sobre el barrio de La Latina. Al final del día, yo escribí los nombres de tres puestos en mi cuaderno para recordarlos. ¡Qué mañana tan perfecta para practicar español y descubrir tesoros!

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find every -er/-ir pretérito verb in the reading (there are five: comimos, bebimos, aprendimos, escribí — plus one more). What did the writer buy, eat, and learn?
🌎 CULTURA · EL RASTRO DE MADRID
Un mercado con siglos de historia

Every Sunday morning, the streets of Madrid's La Latina neighborhood fill with hundreds of stalls selling antiques, art, clothing, and records — this is El Rastro, one of Europe's oldest and largest flea markets, tracing its roots back to the Middle Ages. Its name (“the trail”) comes from the blood trail once left by animals dragged to a nearby slaughterhouse. Today it's a beloved weekend ritual for madrileños and visitors alike — and the kind of authentic, everyday Spain you'll step into later this semester in your VR project, De compras en el mercado, where you'll narrate your own market visit using the very pretérito forms you're building this module.

★ LA VIRTUD · LAS SIETE, JUNTAS

Las siete, juntas reminds us a system only works when its parts stay connected — exactly like -er and -ir verbs, which share endings even though they look different at first. Building that connection takes the same patient perseverancia you used for -ar verbs yesterday, and the responsabilidad to keep practicing even once a pattern feels familiar. Módulo 8's capstone will ask all seven virtues to connect this same way.
