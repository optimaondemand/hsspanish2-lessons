★ Habla de tu gente y tus cosas
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You already have every piece — today you assemble them into something you can say out loud.
Nothing new today. No new words and no new grammar — this is a production day. You'll take the module's two “knowing” verbs (saber and conocer) and its object pronouns — direct (lo, la, los, las) and indirect (me, te, le, nos, les) — and use them to describe the people and the things in your own life. We rehearse with sentence frames and a full model, and then you draft and record your Tarea oral: a short spoken presentation worth 20 points on the presentational rubric.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to build spoken sentences about the people I know using saber and conocer correctly.
2
I will be able to use direct (lo/la/los/las) and indirect (me/te/le/nos/les) object pronouns to talk about the things I give, lend, and send.
3
I will be able to plan, rehearse, and record a short Tarea oral presentation describing the people and things in my life.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Warm up your speaking voice

Today is about saying, not just recognizing. Before you plan anything, warm up: think of one real person in your life — a vecino, a compañera de clase, a family member — and say one true sentence about them out loud right now, mixing a “knowing” verb with something you give or send them. Something like Conozco a mi vecina y le presto libros. Don't aim for perfect — aim for out loud. That first sentence is the hardest; after it, the rest flow.

When you picture recording yourself in Spanish, what's your honest reaction right now?

⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +3 min
Jot a tiny word list on paper: two people you'll mention and two things you give or send them. That four-word list is the whole skeleton of your Tarea oral — everything else is just frames wrapped around it.
🧠 PART 3 · TUS ANDAMIOS · SENTENCE FRAMES 8 min

Frames you fill in — no grammar to invent

These are andamios (scaffolds): ready-made sentence shapes from this module's grammar. Drop your own people and things into the blanks and you have a presentation. Nothing here is new — it's the same saber/conocer and object pronouns you've used all week.

To say…Frame (fill in the blank)Ejemplo
who you're familiar withConozco a ___.Conozco a mi vecina Carmen.
a fact / know-howSé que ___. / Sé ___ (+ infinitive).Sé que ella vive cerca. / Sé cocinar.
replace a thing (direct)¿El/La ___? Lo/La + verb.¿El regalo? Lo tengo aquí.
who you give/send it to (indirect)A ___, le + verb.A mi abuela le mando una carta.

Two reminders you already know: the pronoun sits right before the conjugated verb (le escribo) or attaches to an infinitive (voy a mandarle una foto). And use them one at a time — stacking a direct and an indirect pronoun together is a next-semester move, so keep it simple today.

⭐ ARMA TU GUIÓN · build your script
Say each frame out loud once with your own words filled in. String four of them together and you've spoken your whole Tarea oral before you ever hit record. Aim for 5–6 sentences total.
🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 11 min

Quick pressure-test before you record: pick the version that would be correct on your recording.

✅ TRY IT · replace the thing (direct object)
You want to say “The messages? I send them every day.” (los mensajes → ?)
✅ TRY IT · saber o conocer?
In your presentation you introduce a person: “___ a mi compañera de clase, Sofía.”
✅ TRY IT · who you give it to (indirect object)
“To my grandmother I send a letter” — which is the clean, correct sentence?

Now the real thing: say your whole draft out loud — 5 or 6 sentences describing your people and your things, using at least one saber/conocer verb, one direct object pronoun, and one indirect object pronoun. Say it once start-to-finish, then reveal a full model to compare.

Hola. Me llamo Daniel. Conozco a mi vecina Carmen; es muy simpática y sé que le gusta la música. A veces le presto discos, y ella siempre me da las gracias. También tengo una amiga en Chile. No la veo en persona, pero le mando mensajes cada semana. ¿Las fotos de mi ciudad? Las comparto con ella. Yours will use your own people and things. Keep the check: conocer for people/places, saber for facts & know-how; lo/la/los/las for the thing, me/te/le/nos/les for the person you give it to — and use just one pronoun at a time.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER · UN MODELO 7 min

Read a finished model, then record yours

Here is a complete Tarea oral, written out. Read it aloud twice — the second time at recording pace. Notice how short and natural it is: six sentences, each one built from a frame you just practiced.

Hola, me llamo Marisol. Voy a hablar de la gente y las cosas de mi vida.
Conozco muy bien a mi vecino, el señor Ramírez. Sé que es de Chile y que le encanta el fútbol.
Los fines de semana le presto mis revistas de deportes, y él siempre me da las gracias.
También tengo una amiga, Camila. No la veo mucho porque vive lejos, pero le mando mensajes cada semana.
¿Las fotos de mi familia? Las comparto con ella también.
Esa es mi gente, y esas son las cosas que compartimos. Gracias.

🎤 TAREA ORAL · graded · 20 pts · presentational rubric
Open Tarea oral — Module 5 in Canvas and record a 45–60 second presentation describing the people and things in your life. Include, at minimum: one saber or conocer sentence, one direct object pronoun (lo/la/los/las), and one indirect object pronoun (me/te/le/nos/les). You'll be scored on how much you communicate, accuracy of these forms, and clear pronunciation — not on being flawless. Rehearse two or three times, then record in one take. It opened alongside your Vocabulary Check and Lengua en acción on Day 7.
🌎 CULTURA · CHILE
Hablar de la gente con cariño

When Chileans talk about the people in their lives, they reach constantly for diminutives — those warm -ito / -ita endings. A grandmother isn't just abuela, she's abuelita; a coffee break isn't café, it's the beloved afternoon onces shared with friends and family. Chilean Spanish is famous across Latin America for this softness and for fast, affectionate speech. You don't need to master diminutives today — just notice that describing tu gente is, in every Spanish-speaking culture, an act of warmth. As you record your Tarea oral, let a little of that care come through in your voice: you're not listing people, you're introducing the ones who matter to you. (In the full course this pairs with a short audio clip of a Chilean once conversation.)

★ LA VIRTUD · RESPONSIBILITY

Gabriela Mistral (Chile, 1889–1957), the first Latin American to win the Nobel Prize in Literature, took her responsibility as a teacher and a public voice seriously — she prepared her words with care, as a poet, a teacher, and a diplomat, because she knew people would be listening. Today you practiced a small version of that same responsibility: drafting and rehearsing your presentation instead of improvising blindly, then recording only once you were ready to stand behind what you said. Responsibility means owning your work before anyone else hears it.
