★ Las cosas — lo que llevas, prestas y compartes
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you named the people around you. Today you name the things that pass between them.
Every day you carry, lose, lend, and borrow a small pile of objects — las llaves, el cargador, los audífonos. This is the second half of Módulo 5's theme: las cosas — everyday objects, possessions, and gadgets. There is no new grammar today. We're stocking your shelves with fresh words and putting them to work with verbs you already own from Spanish 1 (tener, llevar, prestar, devolver, compartir). Tomorrow, in 5.03, we'll add the first real grammar of the module — saber vs. conocer.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to recognize and pronounce everyday-object words like el cargador, las llaves, el aparato, and el recuerdo.
2
I will be able to talk about carrying, lending, returning, and sharing my things using llevar, prestar, devolver, and compartir (verbs I already own).
3
I will be able to combine today's objects with yesterday's people words to say who shares what with whom.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Empty your pockets

Take a second and picture what's in your backpack or your pockets right now. Phone, keys, a charger, headphones, maybe a wallet or a little keepsake you carry around. These are las cosas — the things of daily life — and every one of them has a Spanish name you're about to learn. Some you'll even recognize before I say them.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
If you had to leave the house with just one object besides your phone, which would it be? Pick the closest — you'll learn its exact Spanish word in Part 2.

⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +4 min
Two of today's words are a matched pair: prestar (to lend — you hand it over) and devolver (to give it back). If your colega lends you a charger, the polite thing is to devolver el cargador the next day. Keep that pair in mind — you'll use it in the reading and in tomorrow's grammar.
🧠 PART 3 · PON LAS PALABRAS A TRABAJAR 8 min

A quick refresher, not a new rule

To put these objects to work you just need present-tense verbs you already own. Here they are lined up with today's things — notice how the object keeps its own article, and how devolver stem-changes (o→ue) in the singular, exactly like poder and volver from Spanish 1:

Subjectllevar / prestar / compartirdevolver (o→ue)
yollevo / presto / compartodevuelvo
túllevas / prestas / compartesdevuelves
usted / él / ellalleva / presta / compartedevuelve
nosotros/asllevamos / prestamos / compartimosdevolvemos
ustedes / ellos / ellasllevan / prestan / compartendevuelven

Build a sentence the simple way — subject + verb + the thing — and add a person with a or con: Llevo el paraguas en la mochila. Presto el cargador a mi compañero. Comparto los audífonos con mi hermana. This is all review. Tomorrow you'll learn the shortcut words (lo, la, le) that let you avoid repeating the thing's name — but not today.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 9 min
✅ TRY IT · name the object

✅ TRY IT · pick the right verb

✅ TRY IT · the right form of devolver
“Mi hermano y yo ___ los audífonos a nuestro vecino mañana.” Choose the nosotros form.

Your turn: say one true sentence out loud about a thing you share or lend — use one object from today + one verb + a person from yesterday (vecino, compañero, colega, hermano/a). Then reveal a model.

Comparto mi cargador con mi compañera de clase, y ella comparte sus audífonos conmigo. Yours will use your own thing and person — the frame is [Yo] + [verbo] + [la cosa] + con / a + [persona]. Keep the object's article: el cargador, las llaves, los audífonos.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Me llamo Tomás y estudio en Santiago de Chile. En mi mochila siempre llevo lo mismo: las llaves de casa, el cargador del teléfono y los audífonos. Mi compañero Beno siempre olvida sus cosas, así que yo presto el cargador a Beno casi todos los días — y Beno siempre devuelve el cargador al final de la clase. Mi vecina doña Rosa tiene un pequeño recuerdo de lapislázuli, una piedra azul típica de Chile; yo guardo un recuerdo igual en la cartera como amuleto. Cuando llueve, mi hermana y yo compartimos el paraguas hasta el metro. Cosas pequeñas, pero dicen mucho de la gente que uno tiene cerca.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Which three things does Tomás always carry in his backpack? What does he lend to Beno — and what does Beno do at the end of class? Notice how the verbs from Part 3 (prestar, devolver, compartir, guardar) each show up with a real object and a real person from yesterday's list.
🌎 CULTURA · CHILE
El lapislázuli — la piedra azul de Chile

Of all the recuerdos a visitor brings home from Chile, few are as Chilean as lapislázuli — a deep-blue semiprecious stone mined high in the Andes. Chile is one of only two countries in the world (with Afghanistan) that produce it in quantity, and Chileans consider it a national stone. In the artisan markets of Santiago, whole stalls sell rings, pendants, and small carved objects in that unmistakable blue. When a Chilean gives you a piece of lapislázuli, they aren't just handing you an aparato or a trinket — they're giving you a recuerdo, a keepsake meant to carry a memory. Objects, in every culture, are quietly about the people attached to them. (In the full course this pairs with a short clip — a walk through a lapislázuli stall in Santiago.)

★ LA VIRTUD · RESPONSIBILITY

Gabriela Mistral (Chile, 1889–1957), the Nobel-winning poet and teacher, lived responsibly with what she had — she famously directed the proceeds of her writing toward children in need rather than keeping them for herself. Responsibility with las cosas means the same for you: when you pides prestado (borrow) something, you own returning it; when you compartes (share) what's yours, you're using it well. Today's small vocabulary carries a real habit — being someone others can trust with what belongs to them.
