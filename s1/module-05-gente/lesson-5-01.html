📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Every story is really about people — so let's get precise about the people in ours.
Welcome to Módulo 5: La gente y las cosas — the people and things in your world. Later this module you'll learn how to say who and what you know (saber vs. conocer) and how to replace people and things with pronouns. But you can't talk about people until you can describe them, so today is pure vocabulary: words for personality and for the different people around us. No new grammar — just fresh words you'll put to work all module.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to recognize and pronounce personality words like amable, exigente, and terco/a.
2
I will be able to name the people around me — el/la vecino/a, el/la desconocido/a, el/la colega, and more.
3
I will be able to describe a real person using ser + an adjective that agrees with them (a spiral of grammar you already own).
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

People and things

Picture your everyday world. You move through it surrounded by people — family, friends, classmates, the neighbor you wave to, the stranger on the bus — and things — your phone, your backpack, the objects you can't leave home without. That's this whole module: la gente y las cosas. We start with the people, and with the words that tell us what someone is like.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Think of one person you spend time with most days. In one word, what are they like? Pick the closest starting point — you'll learn the exact Spanish word in Part 2.

⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +4 min
Notice the des- pair: un conocido is someone you know a little; add des- and you get un desconocido — someone you don't know at all. That prefix flips meaning to the opposite, just like English un-. File it away: it points straight at this module's big grammar — conocer, "to know a person."
🧠 PART 3 · PON LAS PALABRAS A TRABAJAR 9 min

A quick refresher, not a new rule

To say what someone is like, you reach for ser + an adjective — grammar you already own from Spanish 1. One reminder that matters with people-words: the adjective must agree with the person you're describing. Here's the pattern lined up with today's vocabulary:

Describing…ser + adjectiveExample
a man / boyes generosoMi vecino es generoso.
a woman / girles generosaMi vecina es generosa.
several people (m/mixed)son generososMis compañeros son generosos.
several people (all f)son generosasMis colegas son generosas.
an -e or -ista wordes amable / egoístaElla es amable. Él es egoísta.

Words ending in -e (amable, exigente) or -ista (egoísta) look the same for men and women — only the plural adds -s. Words ending in -o/-a (generoso/a, terco/a) switch endings to match. This is review — nothing new to memorize today.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 8 min
✅ TRY IT · match the word to the person
Tu vecina siempre te saluda con una sonrisa y te ayuda con las bolsas. ¿Cómo es?
✅ TRY IT · name the relationship
Someone you have never met and do not know at all is…
✅ TRY IT · make the adjective agree
“Marta y su hermana son muy ___.” (hardworking) Choose the form that agrees.

Your turn: pick one real person in your life and describe them out loud with ser + one word from today — make the adjective agree. Then reveal a model.

Mi compañera de clase se llama Sofía. Ella es muy graciosa y trabajadora. Yours will use your own person — the frame is [Persona] es / son + [adjetivo que concuerda]. A boy would be gracioso; two people would be graciosos.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Me llamo Camila y vivo en Valparaíso, en Chile. En mi edificio hay gente muy diferente. Mi vecina doña Rosa es mayor y muy amable: siempre me invita a tomar once. Mi compañero Nicolás es gracioso y un poco terco — nunca cambia de opinión. En la esquina siempre veo a un desconocido que lee el periódico; no sé cómo se llama. Y mi mamá, que es trabajadora y sincera, dice que en Chile la gente es cariñosa con los vecinos. Creo que tiene razón.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Who in the passage does Camila not know? Which two words describe her classmate Nicolás? Notice every description uses ser + an adjective that agrees — exactly the pattern from Part 3.
🌎 CULTURA · CHILE
La once — when Chileans gather

In Chile, one of the warmest ways to be with your gente is la once — an afternoon gathering, usually around 5–7 p.m., built around tea or coffee with bread, palta (avocado), cheese, and conversation. It is less about the food than about sitting down together: families, neighbors like doña Rosa, and friends slow down and talk. (Chileans even turn it into a verb — oncear, "to have once.") It's a small daily ritual that says a lot about how a culture values relationships — the very people-words you learned today. (In the full course this pairs with a short authentic clip — a Chilean family inviting a guest a tomar once.)

★ LA VIRTUD · RESPONSIBILITY

Gabriela Mistral (Chile, 1889–1957) — poet, rural schoolteacher, and the first Latin American to win the Nobel Prize in Literature (1945) — took responsibility for every child she taught, choosing her words about each student with real care because she knew a description can lift someone up or wear them down. Describing people, as you did today, carries that same weight: calling someone amable or trabajador/a is a small act of responsibility — you're accountable for describing them honestly and kindly. Responsibility means owning the effect your words have on the people around you.
