⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

One sentence, all the tools

Everything you learned this module can live in a single sentence. Read this one aloud and watch the pieces work together: —Conozco a mi vecina, sé que le gusta la música, y a veces le presto mis audífonos porque los tengo siempre en la mochila. That's conocer (a person) + saber (a fact) + an indirect pronoun (le, to her) + a direct pronoun (los, the headphones) — all in scope, all yours.

🧠 PART 3 · TODO EN UNA HOJA 8 min

The whole module on one page

Nothing here is new — this is your reference sheet for today's graded work. First, the two verbs for “to know”:

VerbUse it for…Example
conocer (yo conozco)people, places — being familiar withConozco a mi vecina. / Conozco Santiago.
saber (yo sé)facts, information, how to do somethingSé dónde vive. / Sé tocar la guitarra.

Remember the personal a before a person: Conozco a mi vecina, but Conozco Santiago (a place — no a).

Second, the two families of object pronouns. Both go right before the conjugated verb. The trick is asking which question the pronoun answers:

TypePronounsAnswersExample
Direct object (the thing)lo, la, los, las¿qué? / whom?¿La mochila? La llevo a clase.
Indirect object (to whom)me, te, le, nos, les¿a quién? / to whom?A mi amigo le presto los audífonos.

Match a direct pronoun to the thing's gender and number (el celular → lo; las llaves → las). Use le / les when the person receives the action, and add a + the person if le would be unclear: Le escribo a mi abuela.

📖 PART 5 · A LEER · TODO JUNTO 7 min

One short reading that uses every tool from the module at once — present tense throughout. The saber/conocer verbs and the object pronouns are in bold so you can track them.

Matías vive en Santiago y conoce a mucha gente en su barrio. Su mejor amiga se llama Ignacia. La conoce desde la escuela primaria, y sabe que a ella le encanta la fotografía.

Ignacia tiene una cámara vieja pero muy buena. Cuando Matías quiere sacar fotos, ella le presta la cámara, y él la cuida con mucho respeto. Los fines de semana suben a un cerro de Valparaíso y sacan fotos de las casas de colores.

Matías no sabe revelar fotos, pero Ignacia sí. Ella le enseña, paso a paso, y él aprende rápido. «Cuando conoces bien a una persona —dice Matías—, también sabes qué compartir con ella.»

📖 COMPRENSIÓN · interpretive reading
According to the reading, what does Matías not know how to do?
⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find each bold word and label it: is it conocer (a person/place), saber (a fact/how-to), a direct pronoun (the thing — the camera), or an indirect pronoun (to whom — Matías/Ignacia)? Notice how le (to him/her) and la (the camera) sit in almost the same spot but answer different questions.

✅ LENGUA EN ACCIÓN · what a strong response includes
Aim for 5–7 connected sentences that:
• name one person with conocer and the personal a (Conozco a…);
• state one fact you know about them with saber (Sé que…);
• describe two things and replace each with a direct-object pronoun (lo, la, los, las) instead of repeating it;
• use one indirect-object pronoun (le / les) with a person (Le presto… a…);
• stay in the present tense — no need for any tense you haven't learned yet.
🌎 CULTURA · CHILE
Valparaíso: una ciudad que se conoce a pie

The port city of Valparaíso — where Matías and Ignacia take their photos — climbs more than forty steep hills (cerros) above the Pacific. To move between them, porteños (the city's residents) ride century-old ascensores, funicular railways that have carried people up the slopes since the 1880s. UNESCO named the historic quarter a World Heritage Site in 2003, partly for these ascensores and partly for the open-air murals that turn whole streets into a gallery. It's a city you don't just visit — you conoces it the way you know a person: by walking it, hill by hill, until its shortcuts and colors feel familiar.

★ LA VIRTUD · RESPONSIBILITY

Gabriela Mistral (Chile, 1889–1957), the Elqui Valley schoolteacher who became the first Latin American to win the Nobel Prize in Literature, took full responsibility for her students' progress — showing up prepared, day after day, in difficult rural classrooms. Today asks the same of you: is it saber or conocer? Lo or le? Responsibility means owning your own preparation — slowing down enough to choose well, then following through as you take the Vocabulary Check and draft your Lengua en acción now that the graded work is open.
