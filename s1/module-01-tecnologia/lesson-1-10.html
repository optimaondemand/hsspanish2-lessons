🧠 PART 3 · LA GRAMÁTICA EN UNA PÁGINA 8 min

The whole module's grammar, on one screen

Two things to have locked in for the quiz. First: the present tense is mostly regular. Whatever the subject, use the normal endings — and remember that every subject except yo stays regular even for the irregular verbs. This is the trap the quiz loves: tú tienes, ella hace, nosotros salimos are all normal.

Subject-ar (mandar)-er (hacer*)-ir (compartir)
yomandohago*comparto
túmandashacescompartes

ustedes / ellos / ellasmandanhacencomparten

*Only the yo form of hacer is irregular (hago) — everything else follows the regular -er pattern.

Second: the six yo-irregulars. Regular for everyone else, but the yo form takes a surprise -go (or -igo for traer). Say each one aloud one more time:

Verbyo formHear itEveryone else (regular)
tener (to have)tengotienes, tiene, tenemos, tienen
hacer (to do / make)hagohaces, hace, hacemos, hacen
poner (to put / post)pongopones, pone, ponemos, ponen
salir (to leave / go out)salgosales, sale, salimos, salen
traer (to bring)traigotraes, trae, traemos, traen
decir (to say / tell)digodices, dice, decimos, dicen

Quiz survival rule: whenever you see yo (or a sentence that means “I…”), pause and ask — is this one of the six? If yes, end it in -go. If not, or if the subject is anyone else, use the normal ending.

🌎 CULTURA · GUATEMALA
El tejido: el primer “código” de Guatemala

Long before smartphones, the Maya were already storing and sharing information in huipiles — the hand-woven blouses worn across the Guatemalan highlands. Each town has its own patterns, colors, and symbols, so a woven huipil can tell you where its wearer is from, her community, even her status — a kind of visual language read at a glance. Today Guatemalan artisans keep this “code” alive by selling their weavings online through las redes sociales and marketplace aplicaciones, reaching buyers on other continents without leaving their villages. The oldest technology of communication and the newest end up working side by side — a fitting way to close a module about how people connect. (In the full course this pairs with a short gallery of regional huipil patterns.)

★ LA VIRTUD · SERVICE

Rigoberta Menchú Tum (Guatemala, b. 1959), the k'iche' Maya activist and 1992 Nobel Peace Prize laureate, closes this module the way she opened it: with a life spent in service to people whose needs went unheard. She could not offer power, so she offered her voice — her testimony traveled the world and changed how it saw Guatemala's indigenous peoples. This module gave you your own small version of that tool: the vocabulary and verbs to say clearly what you do, what you have, and what you can offer someone else. As you take today's quiz, remember that learning to communicate accurately is never just grammar — it's a way of preparing yourself to be of service when it matters.
