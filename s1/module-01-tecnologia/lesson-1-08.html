★ Así uso la tecnología — en mi propia voz
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Everything you built this module now comes together — out loud, in your voice.
Today is a production day: no new grammar and no new words. You already own the technology vocabulary and the six yo-irregular verbs (tengo, hago, pongo, salgo, traigo, digo). Now you'll put them together into a short, first-person description of your own technology and communication life, rehearse it, and record it — that recording is your graded Tarea oral. We scaffold it step by step so that when you hit “record,” you already know what you want to say.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to describe, in the first person, how I use technology and communicate, using tengo, hago, pongo, salgo, traigo, and digo.
2
I will be able to connect my sentences into a smooth spoken paragraph using sentence frames and linking words.
3
I will be able to draft, rehearse, and record my Tarea oral to the presentational rubric.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

Warm up your voice before you record

A recording sounds a hundred times more confident when you've said the words out loud first. Read this line aloud twice, a little smoother each time: —Tengo un celular y una computadora, y todos los días hago la tarea en línea. That single sentence already uses two of today's verbs. Now think about your own life for a moment before we build.

🎤 FONÉTICA · play before you record · g vs. j
Your yo-verbs are full of the letter g. Before a / o / u it's a hard g (as in English “go”): tengo, hago, digo. But before e / i, the same letter sounds like the Spanish j (a breathy “h”): gente, tecnología. Say them back and feel the difference.
🧠 PART 3 · TUS SEIS VERBOS 7 min

The six yo-forms your description leans on

These are the yo-irregular verbs from this module — regular everywhere except the yo form, which takes a surprise -go. Since your Tarea oral is all about you, the yo form is exactly what you need. Click each one and say it aloud.

Verbyo formHear itUse it to say…
tener (to have)tengoTengo un celular nuevo.
hacer (to do / make)hagoHago la tarea en línea.
poner (to put / turn on)pongoPongo el teléfono en silencio.
salir (to go out / leave)salgoSalgo de casa con mis audífonos.
traer (to bring)traigoTraigo el cargador a la escuela.
decir (to say / tell)digoLes digo “hola” a mis amigos por mensaje.

Notice traer adds an -i-: traigo (the “-igo” cousin). All six end in -go in the yo form — that's the whole pattern.

🧠 PART 4 · CONSTRUYE TU DESCRIPCIÓN 12 min

Pick the right yo-form, then build your own lines

First, three quick checks so the yo forms feel automatic. Then you'll draft your own description with the frames below.

✅ TRY IT · choose the yo-form
“Yo siempre ___ el celular en modo silencio en clase.” (I always put my phone on silent…)
✅ TRY IT · choose the yo-form
“Cada mañana les ___ ‘buenos días’ a mis amigos por mensaje.” (Each morning I tell my friends…)
✅ TRY IT · choose the yo-form
“Para hablar con mi familia, ___ una videollamada los domingos.” (To talk with my family, I make a video call…)
✎ SENTENCE FRAMES · fill each one for yourself, then say it aloud
1. Tengo _______ y _______. (what devices you have)
2. Todos los días hago _______ y pongo _______. (a daily habit)
3. Cuando salgo de casa, traigo _______, y les digo _______ a _______. (what you carry + who you message)

Now say your whole description out loud — all three frames in a row, as one smooth paragraph. When you're happy with it, reveal a full model to compare.

Me llamo Daniel y así uso la tecnología. Tengo un celular y una computadora portátil. Todos los días hago la tarea en línea y pongo el teléfono en silencio en clase. Cuando salgo de casa, siempre traigo mis audífonos y el cargador. Les digo “hola” a mis amigos por mensaje y public… publico fotos en mis redes sociales. — Yours will use your own devices and habits; the frame is what matters.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 6 min

A student's model — read it, then borrow its shape

Sofía tiene dieciséis años y vive en Quetzaltenango, Guatemala. Ella describe su vida digital así:

«Me llamo Sofía. Tengo un celular, pero no tengo computadora en casa. Todos los días hago la tarea en el celular y pongo música cuando estudio. Cuando salgo para la escuela, traigo mis audífonos. Me gustan las videollamadas: los domingos hago una videollamada con mi abuela, y siempre le digo “te quiero” antes de colgar.»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find all six yo-irregular verbs in Sofía's paragraph (one appears twice). Notice how short her sentences are — you do not need long, complicated Spanish to score well. Simple, correct, and clear beats long and tangled.
🎤 GRADED · TAREA ORAL (20 pts) · presentational speaking
Record: “Así uso la tecnología”

Record a 45–75 second spoken description of how you use technology and communicate. Speak in the first person and use at least five of the six yo-irregular verbs (tengo, hago, pongo, salgo, traigo, digo). Submit the recording in the Tarea oral — Módulo 1 assignment in Canvas.

Before you record:
• Fill in the three frames from Part 4 with your real life.
• Say the whole thing aloud twice — once slowly, once at a natural pace.
• Record in a quiet spot. It's fine to glance at your notes, but don't read word-for-word — speak.

🌎 CULTURA · GUATEMALA
La radio comunitaria — tecnología en veintidós idiomas

Communication technology looks different in the Guatemalan highlands. Alongside 22 living Mayan languages, dozens of radios comunitarias (community radio stations) broadcast the news, music, and health information in K'iche', Kaqchikel, Mam, and more — reaching mountain villages that a cell signal often can't. For many families the radio, not the smartphone, is the everyday communication tool, and younger Guatemalans are now blending the two: recording songs and messages in Mayan languages and sharing them on las redes sociales. Technology, in other words, is helping keep ancient languages alive online. (In the full course this pairs with a short clip of a K'iche'-language community broadcast.)

★ LA VIRTUD · SERVICE

Rigoberta Menchú Tum (Guatemala, b. 1959), a K'iche' Maya activist, won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1992 for a lifetime spent serving people whose voices had been silenced. When she could not reach the powerful any other way, she used the one tool she had — her voice — and her testimony Me llamo Rigoberta Menchú carried the needs of her people to the whole world. Service often begins exactly there: with someone deciding to speak clearly on behalf of others. Every time you record your own voice in this course, you're practicing that same willingness to put your voice to use.
