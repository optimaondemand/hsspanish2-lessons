📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Nothing new today — today the pieces snap together, and your first graded work opens.
You've spent six days building this module's toolkit: vocab set A (devices & screens), vocab set B (messaging & social media), the full present-tense spiral, and the six yo-irregulars (tengo, hago, pongo, salgo, traigo, digo). Today is integration — no new words, no new rules — we mix everything in one place so it works together, not in separate drawers. This is also the day the module's graded layer opens: the Vocabulary Check (listening) and Lengua en acción (written) are now available to you in Canvas. Today's practice is your on-ramp to both.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to combine set A and set B vocabulary with the present tense in one connected sentence.
2
I will be able to choose the correct yo-irregular form (tengo, hago, pongo, salgo, traigo, digo) inside a full sentence.
3
I will be able to write a short, true paragraph about my own technology use — the exact skill the Lengua en acción asks for.

🧠 PART 3 · REPASO DE GRAMÁTICA · TODO EL PRESENTE 8 min

One table, the whole module's grammar

Here's the key idea that makes the yo-irregulars easy: they're irregular only in the yo form. Every other person is the plain present tense you've used since Spanish 1. Read down the yo column — that's the only column you had to memorize this module.

And the regular present is still doing most of the work: Yo uso la computadora, descargo una aplicación, publico una foto y comparto el enlace. Mix a regular verb, a yo-irregular, and a vocab word and you have a real sentence — which is exactly what Part 4 asks for.

🧠 PART 4 · PRÁCTICA MIXTA 12 min

Every item below mixes vocabulary and grammar on purpose — that's what integration means. Read the whole sentence before you choose.

✅ TRY IT · present spiral + set B

Your turn — and this is a warm-up for the Lengua en acción: write (or say) two connected sentences about your own day with technology. Use at least one word from each vocab set and one yo-irregular. Then reveal a model.

Todas las mañanas salgo de casa y traigo mi celular y mi computadora portátil. En el autobús me conecto en línea, publico una foto en las redes sociales y le mando un mensaje a mi mejor amiga. Yours will use your own routine — the frame is Salgo / traigo / hago… (a yo-irregular) + a device from set A + an action from set B.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

«Me llamo Diego y estudio en línea desde Antigua, Guatemala. Por la mañana salgo temprano al café de mi tía porque allí hay buena señal. Traigo mi computadora portátil y mi celular. Primero pongo la contraseña y me conecto; luego hago la tarea y descargo las lecturas para leerlas sin internet. Cuando termino, publico mis apuntes y comparto el enlace con mis compañeros. Si alguien no entiende, le mando un mensaje o hacemos una videollamada. No tengo una conexión rápida, pero siempre les digo: «la señal es lenta, pero aprendemos igual.»»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find all six yo-irregulars in Diego's paragraph (salgo, traigo, pongo, hago, tengo, digo). Then count how many words come from set A and how many from set B. Notice: everything here is present tense — nothing you haven't already built.

🌎 CULTURA · GUATEMALA
El Popol Vuh: un libro antiguo en una pantalla nueva

The Popol Vuh — the sacred creation story of the K'iche' Maya of Guatemala — is one of the great books of the Americas, telling how the world and the first people were made. For centuries it survived only in fragile handwritten copies. Today the oldest surviving manuscript has been fully digitized by a university library and is free to read en línea, while Guatemalan teachers comparten illustrated versions and read-alouds on las redes sociales. It's a perfect picture of this module's theme — and of today's lesson: something very old and something very new, integrated. A story once spoken aloud around a fire now lives on the same kind of pantalla you hold in your hand. (In the full course this pairs with a look at a page of the digitized manuscript.)

★ LA VIRTUD · SERVICE

Rigoberta Menchú Tum (Guatemala, b. 1959), the K'iche' Maya activist who won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1992, spent her life making sure her people's story was written down and shared so it could serve others long after she told it. Service means offering what you know so someone else is better off, and that's exactly what happens when the Popol Vuh is digitized for anyone to read, or when a student like Diego shares his notes so a classmate can keep up. As you write your own paragraph today, remember that putting your ideas into clear words that help someone else understand is itself a small act of service.
