★ Traigo y digo — el club «-igo» completa el cuadro
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Two more verbs join the irregular-yo family — and one of them has a second surprise hiding inside.
On Day 3 you cracked the -go club (tengo, hago, pongo, salgo). Today we finish the picture with two verbs whose yo form does something slightly fancier. Traer (to bring) doesn't just add -go; it slips an i in first — traer → traigo. That's the -igo pattern. And decir (to say / to tell) does two things at once: it stem-changes e→i (like verbs you already met) and takes the -go — decir → digo. Same big rule as Tuesday: only the yo form is irregular. No new vocabulary today — these are verbs and words you already own.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to produce the -igo yo-form of traer (traigo) and explain why it isn't just -go.
2
I will be able to produce digo and recognize the two things happening in it — the e→i stem change and the -go ending.
3
I will be able to sort a verb into the right group — plain -go vs. -igo — and keep the other persons regular.

🧠 PART 3 · EL PATRÓN «-IGO» Y DECIR 12 min

First, the two new yo forms

Click to hear each infinitive and its yo form. Say the pair aloud — traer → traigo, decir → digo.

  • traerto bring  →  yo traigo («-igo»)
  • decirto say / to tell  →  yo digo (e→i + «-go»)

The -igo pattern: traer → traigo

Where the Day-3 verbs just tacked -go onto the stem (pon→pongo), traer adds an i first: tra→traigo. Everything past yo is a completely regular -er verb:

SubjecttraerExample (recycled vocab)
yotraigo  ← «-igo»Yo traigo el portátil a clase.
tútraes¿Traes el móvil a la escuela?
usted / él / ellatraeElla trae sus apuntes en la tablet.
nosotros/astraemosNosotros traemos los cargadores.
ustedes / ellos / ellastraenEllos traen el móvil a todas partes.

Two other everyday verbs join this same -igo club — oír → oigo (to hear) and caer → caigo (to fall). You only need to recognize those two for now; traigo is the one to master today.

decir → digo: two changes in one form

Decir is the show-off of the group. Its yo form does both tricks at once: the stem vowel changes e→i (dec→dic) and it takes the -go → digo. Notice the e→i stem change also shows up in tú, él, and ellos — but nosotros keeps the original e:

SubjectdecirExample (recycled vocab)
yodigo  ← e→i + «-go»Yo digo la verdad en mis mensajes.
túdices¿Qué dices en la videollamada?
usted / él / elladiceElla dice «hola» por el chat.
nosotros/asdecimosNosotros decimos la contraseña en voz baja.
ustedes / ellos / ellasdicenEllos dicen muchas cosas en la red social.

The whole irregular-yo picture, at last

Put today's two beside Tuesday's four and the family is complete. Every one is irregular only in yo:

GroupInfinitive → yoWhat happens
plain «-go»tener→tengo, hacer→hago, poner→pongo, salir→salgostem + -go
«-igo»traer→traigostem + i + -go
e→i + «-go»decir→digostem change and -go

Spiral reminder: all of this is still the present tense — the same present you consolidated in the repaso. Nothing here is a new tense; it's just the yo corner of the present, filled in.

📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Lo que traigo y lo que digo

«Me llamo Diego y estudio en Antigua, Guatemala. A clase siempre traigo el portátil y el móvil en la mochila. Cuando el profesor hace una pregunta, yo digo la respuesta en voz alta — y si no sé algo, también lo digo: «No estoy seguro.» Mi amigo Marcos trae una tablet y dice que es más ligera. Por la tarde hacemos una videollamada para estudiar; nosotros decimos las respuestas en voz baja para no molestar a la familia. Al final del día, siempre digo la verdad en mis mensajes: es más fácil que recordar una mentira.»

🌎 CULTURA · GUATEMALA
El Popol Vuh: lo que se dice se guarda

Long before phones and video calls, the K'iche' Maya of the Guatemalan highlands passed their history by voice — storyteller to listener, generation to generation. Around 1550 that oral tradition was written down as the Popol Vuh («Book of the Community»), the great K'iche' account of creation and the hero twins. It survives today because someone chose to record what elders said — lo que decían — so it would not be lost. That's the deep version of this module's theme: every technology, from a written book to a videollamada, is really a way to carry what we say across distance and time. (In the full course this pairs with a short read-aloud of an opening line of the Popol Vuh.)

★ LA VIRTUD · SERVICE

Rigoberta Menchú Tum (Guatemala, b. 1959) turned the simple act of saying into a lifetime of service to her people. Her 1983 testimony began with her own name — «Me llamo Rigoberta Menchú» — and then told the world what was happening to indigenous Guatemalans, work honored with the Nobel Peace Prize in 1992. Today's verb decir carries that same spirit: to tell the truth on someone else's behalf is a form of service, and when you practice digo, traigo, you're rehearsing the words that let you speak up for others, not just yourself.
