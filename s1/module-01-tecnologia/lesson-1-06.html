★ Guatemala conectada — los irregulares en yo, todos juntos
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
No new rules today — just the six yo-irregulars, working together in a real story.
Over the last few days you built the two halves of this module's grammar: the -go group (tengo, hago, pongo, salgo) and the -igo group (traigo, digo). Today we don't add anything — we use all six at once. You'll read about a young Maya artisan in Guatemala who brings her family's woven huipiles to the world through a phone, and you'll watch every one of these yo forms appear in context. Same verbs, real life.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to produce all six yo-irregular forms (tengo, hago, pongo, salgo, traigo, digo) correctly inside full sentences.
2
I will be able to read a culture-rich passage about Guatemala and spot every yo-irregular the writer uses.
3
I will be able to describe my own connected day — what I do, put, bring, say, and where I go out — using these verbs.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

Warm the six verbs back up

Quick memory jog before we read. All six of this module's tricky verbs behave normally with everyone except yo, where a hidden g shows up: tengo, hago, pongo, salgo, traigo, digo. Read them aloud once. Now think about your own morning with a phone in your hand.

🧠 PART 3 · LOS SEIS, EN UNA TABLA 8 min

One picture of everything you already know

This is a repaso table, not a new rule. Every verb here is irregular only in the yo form; the other people (tú, él/ella, nosotros, ellos) follow the normal present-tense pattern you already use. Read across each row:

Infinitiveyo formIn a sentence (present)
tenertengoYo tengo un celular con buena conexión.

Two families, one habit: -go (tengo, hago, pongo, salgo) and -igo (traigo, digo). Decir also stem-changes (e→i) in the other forms — digo, dices, dice — but that's review, not new here.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 12 min

The yo form, in context

Each sentence is about a connected day. Choose the correct yo form, then check.

✅ TRY IT · salir
“Cada mañana yo ___ de casa temprano para tener buena señal en el bus.” (Every morning I leave home early…)
✅ TRY IT · tener
“Yo ___ una aplicación para vender los tejidos de mi familia.” (I have an app to sell my family's weavings.)
✅ TRY IT · poner
“Cuando termino un huipil, yo ___ una foto en línea.” (When I finish a huipil, I post a photo online.)

Your turn: how would you say “I bring my phone to class every day”? Say it out loud first, then reveal a model.

Traigo mi celular a clase todos los días. From traer → the yo form is traigo (the -igo family). Yours can name any object you carry — the frame is Traigo… a clase.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Ixchel, tejedora y emprendedora

«Me llamo Ixchel y vivo en Chichicastenango, Guatemala. En casa hablamos k'iche' y español. Cada mañana salgo temprano al mercado con mi abuela. Yo tengo un teléfono con conexión a internet, y hago algo nuevo para mi familia: pongo fotos de nuestros huipiles — los tejidos mayas que teje mi mamá — en una aplicación. Cuando llega un mensaje de un cliente, siempre digo «muchas gracias» y después traigo el tejido al centro para enviarlo. Así la tecnología lleva el arte maya al mundo entero.»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find all six yo-irregulars Ixchel uses (there's one of each). Then notice teje mi mamá — why is it teje, not tejo? (The subject is her mother, not yo.) The g only appears when Ixchel talks about herself.
🌎 CULTURA · GUATEMALA
El huipil: un texto que se lleva puesto

The huipil — a hand-woven blouse Maya women have made on the backstrap loom for centuries — is Guatemala's most famous authentic artifact. Its colors and patterns aren't decoration only: they say which town and community the wearer belongs to, almost like a woven ID card. Guatemala is home to more than 20 Mayan languages, among them k'iche', spoken by over a million people. Today many young artisans like Ixchel use phones and apps to sell their weaving directly — though internet access is still uneven, and rural highland villages often have far weaker conexión than the capital. Technology is opening a door; it hasn't opened it equally for everyone yet. (In the full course this pairs with a short gallery of real huipiles by region.)

★ LA VIRTUD · SERVICE

Rigoberta Menchú Tum (Guatemala, b. 1959), a K'iche' Maya woman, won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1992 for a life devoted to serving indigenous communities during and after Guatemala's long civil war. Her testimony Me llamo Rigoberta Menchú carried the voice of people the world had ignored, placing her own story at the service of theirs. When Ixchel puts her community's art online under its real name, she practices a small piece of that same virtue — using a screen to serve and honor people who might otherwise go unseen.
