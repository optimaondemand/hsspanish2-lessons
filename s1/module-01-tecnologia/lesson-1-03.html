Lesson 1.03
★ La «g» escondida: tengo, hago, pongo, salgo
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Two days of vocabulary are loaded. Today the yo form fights back — and it hides an extra g.
For two days you've dropped new technology words into the present tense you already own — the regular -ar / -er / -ir engine from Spanish 1. That's the spiral: old grammar, new words. Today we finally add new grammar — but no new vocabulary. Four verbs you already know (tener, hacer, poner, salir) do something surprising in the yo form only: they add a g → tengo, hago, pongo, salgo. Every other person stays regular. Learn the trick once and it unlocks a whole family of verbs.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to produce the yo-irregular forms tengo, hago, pongo, and salgo.
2
I will be able to explain that only the yo form is irregular — the other persons keep their regular endings.
3
I will be able to describe my own technology habits using these verbs in true present-tense sentences.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

You already say one of these every day

Say this out loud: Yo tengo un celular. You've been saying tengo since Spanish 1 — tengo 16 años, tengo hambre, tengo una pregunta. Notice it isn't «teno.» There's a hidden g in there. That little g is today's whole lesson: three more everyday verbs pull the exact same trick.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Gut check: when you hear “yo tengo un celular,” how does that yo form feel to you?
📚 PART 2 · ESCUCHA LAS CUATRO FORMAS «YO» 8 min

Hear the hidden g, then say it back

These aren't new words — you already know all four verbs. What's new is the sound of the yo form. Click the 🔊 and repeat aloud, listening for the -go ending each time.

  • tengoI have  ·  tener
  • hagoI do / I make  ·  hacer
  • pongoI put / I put on  ·  poner
  • salgoI leave / I go out / I exit  ·  salir
⭐ FÍJATE BIEN · say the pair
Say each infinitive and its yo form together, out loud: tener → tengo, hacer → hago, poner → pongo, salir → salgo. Hear how the g only shows up in the yo form? Hold on to that — the table in Part 3 shows what happens to everybody else.
🧠 PART 3 · LA GRAMÁTICA · VERBOS «YO» IRREGULARES (-GO) 10 min

One irregular form — the rest behave

Here's the whole idea in one sentence: for these verbs, the yo form ends in -go, and every other person is regular. Start by pairing each infinitive with its surprise yo form:

InfinitiveEnglishyo form
tenerto havetengo
hacerto do / to makehago
ponerto put / to put onpongo
salirto leave / to go outsalgo

Now watch what happens to the other persons. Cover the yo row and these verbs are completely regular — ordinary -er and -ir endings, no g anywhere:

yohagopongosalgo

One footnote about tener. Its yo form joins the club — tengo — but you already know from Spanish 1 that tener also has an e→ie stem change in the other forms: tú tienes, él/ella tiene, ellos tienen (but regular nosotros tenemos). So tener is doing two things at once — the -go yo form and the stem change you reviewed in Módulo 0.

All present tense, all recycled vocabulary: Yo hago la tarea en la computadora. Yo pongo mi contraseña. Yo salgo de la aplicación. Yo tengo una videollamada a las seis.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 10 min
✅ TRY IT · the yo form of hacer
“Todas las noches yo ___ la tarea en mi computadora portátil.” (Every night I do my homework on my laptop.)
✅ TRY IT · the yo form of poner
“Antes de dormir, yo ___ el celular a cargar.” (Before sleeping, I put my phone on to charge.)
✅ TRY IT · only the yo is irregular
Which sentence is correct? Remember: only yo takes the -go form; the other persons stay regular.

Your turn (commit, then reveal): say one true sentence about your own morning or night using at least one -go verb (tengo, hago, pongo, salgo) plus a technology word you already know. Say it out loud first — then reveal a model.

Todas las mañanas pongo mi contraseña, hago la tarea en mi computadora y salgo de casa con el celular cargado. Yours will use your own routine — the frame is Yo pongo… / hago… / salgo… / tengo… + a word from Lessons 1.01–1.02.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

«Me llamo Diego y vivo en Antigua, Guatemala. Todas las mañanas hago lo mismo: pongo mi contraseña, me conecto y reviso mis mensajes. Tengo una aplicación para hablar con mi prima en la capital; cada domingo hacemos una videollamada. Cuando termino, salgo de la aplicación y cargo el celular. Por la tarde salgo con mis amigos, pero siempre pongo el teléfono en silencio para escuchar la marimba en el parque.»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find all four -go yo forms in Diego's paragraph (hago, pongo, tengo, salgo). Then look at hacemos: it comes from hacer, but it has no g — because it's nosotros, not yo. That's the whole rule in action.
🌎 CULTURA · GUATEMALA
La marimba: el sonido de Guatemala

The marimba — a large wooden xylophone played by several musicians at once — is the national instrument of Guatemala, named a national symbol by law and central to festivals, weddings, and town-square gatherings across the country. Its deep, rippling sound is so tied to Guatemalan identity that people call it el alma de Guatemala (“the soul of Guatemala”). And it lives in your vocab, too: today's young marimbistas record their performances and publican them on las redes sociales, so a teenager in Antigua like Diego can salir to the park to hear it live — and a listener anywhere can hear it through a pantalla. (In the full course this pairs with a short marimba listening clip.)

★ LA VIRTUD · SERVICE

Rigoberta Menchú Tum (Guatemala, born 1959), a K’iche’ Maya activist, won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1992 for a life of service to Indigenous peoples whose rights had been ignored. There's a quiet echo in today's grammar. The most personal form of any verb is yo — I have, I do, I make. Menchú spent her yo — everything she personally had, did, and gave — in the service of others; as you practice tengo, hago, pongo, salgo, notice that each sentence is also a chance to say what you can offer someone else.
