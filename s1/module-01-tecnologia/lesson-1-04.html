★ Mi rutina digital — los verbos «-go» en acción
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you learned the rule. Today you live in it — in the first person.
No new grammar and no new words today — this is a use-it day. You already met the four -go verbs (tengo, hago, pongo, salgo) in Lesson 1.03, and you already have a bank of technology vocabulary from Lessons 1.01–1.02. Now we put them together to do one real thing: describe your own digital routine — talking about yourself, so everything lands in the yo form. We'll practice with quick checks and then read how a teenager in Guatemala describes her connected day.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to describe my own daily tech routine in the first person using tengo, hago, pongo, and salgo.
2
I will be able to choose the right form of a -go verb by first checking who the subject is (yo vs. everyone else).
3
I will be able to read a short first-person text about someone's digital routine and understand it without translating word by word.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

Think about your own morning

Before you touch any grammar, picture the first fifteen minutes of your day. What technology do you have in your hand? What do you do with it? Where do you put your phone before you leave the house? Every one of those four verbs — tener, hacer, poner, salir — is about to describe you, so every one will take that little -go tail. Warm up your ear first.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Which of these is closest to your real first move in the morning? (No right answer — just notice the verb in each option.)
📚 PART 2 · FRASES PARA TU RUTINA DIGITAL 7 min

Ready-made pieces — all words you already know

Nothing here is new: these are your -go yo-forms from Lesson 1.03 attached to the technology vocabulary from Lessons 1.01–1.02. Click the 🔊, repeat each chunk aloud, and picture yourself saying it. Think of them as building blocks you'll snap together in Part 4.

  • tengo…I have — Tengo un celular y una computadora portátil.
  • hago…I do / make — Hago la tarea en línea.
  • pongo…I put / place — Pongo la contraseña en la aplicación.
  • salgo…I go out / leave — Salgo de casa con el celular cargado.

And the tech words those sentences lean on — hear them again:

  • el celularcell phone
  • en líneaonline
  • la aplicaciónapp
  • mandar un mensajeto send a message / text
⭐ FÍJATE · the silent yo
Notice each verb chunk really begins with a hidden word: (yo). In Spanish you usually drop the yo because the -go ending already announces “this is about me.” That's the whole reason today matters — the ending, not a separate word, carries the person.
🧠 PART 3 · CÓMO SE USA 8 min

From rule to routine

You proved the pattern yesterday: only yo takes the -go; everyone else is regular. Today we just aim it at a purpose — a whole day, from waking up to logging off. Here is the same set of verbs, each shown the way you'll actually use it to talk about your own tech habits:

To say…yo formA full sentence (recycled vocab)
I have a devicetengoYo tengo un celular y una computadora portátil.
I do / make somethinghagoHago la tarea en línea y hago videollamadas con mi familia.
I put / place somethingpongoPongo la contraseña y luego pongo el teléfono a cargar.
I go out / leavesalgoSalgo de casa a las siete con el celular en la mochila.

The one move that never fails: before you pick a form, ask ¿quién? — who is doing it? If the answer is I / yo, reach for the -go form. If it's a friend, a class, or “we,” the verb goes back to its regular ending (hace, pone, sale… hacemos, ponemos, salimos). You'll practice exactly that decision next.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 11 min

Each item is about a real digital routine. First ask who is doing the action, then commit to a form and read the feedback.

✅ TRY IT · describe your own routine
You're telling a classmate about your morning: “Por la mañana yo ___ la tarea en línea antes de salir.” (In the morning I do my homework online before leaving.)
✅ TRY IT · two blanks, one subject
“Antes de dormir, yo ___ el celular a cargar y ___ la alarma.” Both blanks are about you and both use poner. Which form fills them?
✅ TRY IT · careful — who is the subject?
“Yo salgo temprano, pero mi hermano ___ tarde porque hace videollamadas.” What goes in the blank for mi hermano (salir)?

Your turn to build a real sentence: describe three true things about your own tech morning, in order — what you have, what you do online, and when you leave. Use tengo…, hago…, salgo… Say it out loud first, then reveal a model to compare.

Tengo un celular y una computadora portátil. Por la mañana hago la tarea en línea y mando un mensaje a mis amigos. Salgo de casa a las siete y media. All three verbs describe me, so all three take -go: tengo, hago, salgo. Yours will use your own devices and times — keep the frame, change the details.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER · «Mi rutina digital» 7 min

Sofía, 16 años, vive en Antigua, Guatemala. Read her post once for the gist, then again hunting for every -go verb. Don't translate word by word — you already know these words.

«¡Hola! Me llamo Sofía y les cuento mi rutina digital. Cada mañana tengo el celular al lado de la cama, así que primero pongo la contraseña y reviso las redes sociales. Antes del desayuno hago la tarea en línea; mi clase de matemáticas usa una aplicación y allí pongo mis respuestas.

Cuando salgo de casa, siempre llevo el celular cargado porque en el bus mando mensajes a mis amigas. En la escuela nosotros tenemos un grupo de chat: yo pongo fotos de mis proyectos y mis compañeros ponen preguntas. Por la tarde hago una videollamada con mi abuela, que vive lejos, en Cobán. Ella no sale mucho, pero con la videollamada estamos juntas igual.

Por la noche pongo el teléfono a cargar y no lo tengo en la cama — así duermo mejor. ¡Esa es mi rutina!»

⭐ FÍJATE · same verb, two endings
Sofía says pongo four times but her classmates ponen. Why the different endings? Same verb, different subject: yo → pongo, ellos → ponen. The question below checks whether you caught who does what.
✅ ¿COMPRENDISTE? · reading check
According to the text, what does Sofía do at night with her phone?
🌎 CULTURA · GUATEMALA
Antigua, Cobán y la señal que sube la montaña

Sofía's video calls to her grandmother in Cobán cross real geography. Guatemala is a country of highlands and volcanoes, and a cell signal that reaches the colonial city of Antigua easily may struggle to climb into the coffee-growing mountains of Alta Verapaz. Over the last decade, mobile phones have leapfrogged older landline networks: for many rural Guatemalan families the first phone in the house was a celular, not a home phone, and a weekly videollamada is how grandparents in the mountains stay part of a grandchild's daily life in the city. Technology here is measured less by the newest gadget than by a simple, human question — ¿llega la señal? (does the signal reach?). (In the full course this pairs with a short map of Guatemala's departments.)

★ LA VIRTUD · SERVICE

Rigoberta Menchú Tum (Guatemala, b. 1959), the K'iche' Maya activist and 1992 Nobel Peace Prize laureate, spent her life making sure isolated communities were not forgotten. Service means reaching the people technology too often skips, and that's exactly what happens when Sofía's grandmother in the mountains joins a videollamada: a small act of service, using a device to make sure someone isn't left out. The everyday routine you're reading about today — calling, connecting, checking in — is one of the simplest ways this virtue shows up in ordinary life.
