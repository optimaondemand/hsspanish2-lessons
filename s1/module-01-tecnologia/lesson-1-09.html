Before you meet her, take thirty seconds with this: when something is unfair and you feel small against it, what can one ordinary person actually do? There's no wrong answer — just notice what you believe.

📖 PART 3 · A LEER 13 min

La voz de Rigoberta Menchú

Read it once all the way through for the gist, then a second time more slowly. A quick note: in Spanish, storytellers often tell true history in the present tense to make it feel alive and close — so you'll see present-tense verbs describing events from decades ago. That's on purpose; just read them as “and then this happens.”

Rigoberta Menchú Tum es una mujer maya k'iche' de Guatemala. Es hija de una familia campesina de las montañas, una familia que trabaja mucho pero vive en la pobreza. Rigoberta habla español y también k'iche', la lengua de su pueblo. Para millones de personas, ella es hoy un símbolo de la justicia.

En Guatemala, los pueblos indígenas no siempre tienen los mismos derechos que otras personas. Muchas familias trabajan la tierra, pero no reciben justicia. Rigoberta ve este problema de cerca y decide actuar. No usa armas — usa su voz. Habla, escribe y viaja por el mundo para defender los derechos de su gente.

En su famoso libro Me llamo Rigoberta Menchú, ella cuenta la historia de su vida y de su comunidad, y el mundo por fin la escucha. En 1992 recibe el Premio Nobel de la Paz: es la primera mujer indígena que gana este premio. Con el dinero del premio, crea una fundación para ayudar a los pueblos indígenas de las Américas.

Cuando Rigoberta habla con los jóvenes, su mensaje es sencillo. En un español simple, dice algo así:

«Yo digo la verdad. Hago lo que puedo por mi pueblo. Pongo mi voz al servicio de la justicia, y traigo la historia de mi gente a todo el mundo.»

(A simplified paraphrase in the spirit of her message — not a word-for-word quotation.)

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Two things to notice before you move on. (1) Meaning: Rigoberta's weapon is her voz — she communicates, exactly the theme of this whole module. (2) Grammar: her four-verb quote is a gift for tomorrow's quiz — digo, hago, pongo, traigo are four of your six yo-irregulars. Circle them and say the sentence aloud.
🧠 PART 4 · REPASO EXPRÉS PARA EL GRAMMAR QUIZ 6 min

Your six yo-irregulars, one last time

Nothing new — this is the pattern the Grammar Quiz will test. Each of these verbs is regular for every subject except yo, where it takes a surprise -go (and traer adds an extra -i-: traigo). Rigoberta used four of them in her quote. Click each and say it aloud once more.

Verbyo formHear itFrom today's reading
tener (to have)tengoTengo una voz, como Rigoberta.
hacer (to do / make)hagoHago lo que puedo por mi pueblo.
poner (to put / place)pongoPongo mi voz al servicio de la justicia.
salir (to go out / leave)salgoSalgo a defender los derechos.
traer (to bring)traigoTraigo la historia de mi gente al mundo.
decir (to say / tell)digoDigo la verdad.

Quiz-day habit: whenever the subject is yo, pause and ask — is this one of the six? If yes, it ends in -go. Every other subject (tú, ella, nosotros…) stays regular: tienes, hace, ponemos.

🧠 PART 5 · PRACTÍCALO 8 min

Check the reading — and warm up for the quiz

One comprehension check, then two yo-form checks in exactly the style of tomorrow's Grammar Quiz.

✅ TRY IT · check your reading

✅ TRY IT · choose the yo form

✅ TRY IT · choose the yo form

Now make it yours. Think of one true thing you stand for, then say one Spanish sentence out loud using a yo-irregular verb (digo, hago, or pongo). Say it first, then reveal a model.

Yo digo la verdad a mis amigos y hago mi trabajo con honestidad. Cuando veo algo injusto, pongo mi voz al servicio de los demás. — Yours will name your own value; what matters is the correct yo-form ending in -go. That is exactly what the quiz rewards tomorrow.
🌎 CULTURA · GUATEMALA
El quetzal — el ave que no vive sin libertad

Guatemala's national bird is the quetzal, a brilliant green-and-crimson bird sacred to the ancient Maya. A famous legend says the quetzal cannot live in a cage — held captive, it dies — so Guatemalans made it their symbol of freedom. It flies on the national flag, and the country even named its money after it: prices are counted in quetzales. The image fits today's reading perfectly: just as the quetzal must be free to live, Rigoberta Menchú insists that her people must be free to speak, to keep their languages, and to receive justice. A bird, a coin, and an activist — all carrying the same idea of libertad. (In the full course this pairs with an image of the quetzal on the Guatemalan flag and a one-quetzal coin.)

★ LA VIRTUD · SERVICE

Rigoberta Menchú Tum (Guatemala, b. 1959) is this module's model of service — giving her time, her testimony, and her voice to people who had none. She could not vote or be heard in her own country, so she made her voice travel: through testimony, books, and speeches carried across the world, work recognized with the Nobel Peace Prize in 1992. Notice how service, for her, begins with communication — the very theme of this module. Ella dice la verdad, hace lo que puede, y pone su voz al servicio de los demás. The lesson for you is small but real: every time you use your Spanish to help someone, include them, or carry a message on their behalf, you are practicing the same virtue on a human scale.
