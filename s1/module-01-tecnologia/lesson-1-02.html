📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you named your devices. Today you learn how you actually talk through them.
In Lesson 1.01 you built vocab set A — the machines and tools (el celular, la computadora portátil, la pantalla, la aplicación, descargar). Today we add set B: the words for messaging and social media — how you send, post, share, and connect. This is a vocabulary day: no new grammar. We simply drop the new words into present-tense sentences you already know how to build.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to recognize and pronounce eight new words for messaging and social media.
2
I will be able to use the new words in simple present-tense sentences I already know how to build (mando, publico, comparto, me conecto).
3
I will be able to read a short conversation about staying in touch online and understand the gist.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

Warm up your set A words

Before we add anything, let's switch on what you built yesterday. Picture your phone in your hand. Which set A words come back right away? El celular, la computadora portátil, la pantalla, la aplicación, descargar… Say each one aloud. Today's eight new words all describe things you do with those tools.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer

⭐ FÍJATE BIEN · two friendly warnings
1. La videollamada is one word — video + llamada (call). 2. Las redes sociales is plural (literally "the social nets") and en línea is two words (literally "in line"). And el enlace is your everyday word for a clickable link — think en- + lace, "to lace together."
🧠 PART 3 · VOCABULARIO EN USO 8 min

Slot the new words into present tense you already own

No new grammar today — just the regular present tense you already use, now carrying today's words. Four of the new terms are verbs: mandar and publicar are regular -ar verbs, compartir is a regular -ir verb, and conectarse is a regular -ar verb that takes a reflexive pronoun (you connect yourself). Here they are in the three forms you use most:

Model sentences: Yo publico fotos en las redes sociales. Tú compartes el enlace. Ella se conecta en línea por la noche. Nosotros mandamos un mensaje. Same endings you already know — brand-new vocabulary riding on top.

📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

—Oye, ¿usas mucho las redes sociales?
—Sí, todos los días. Publico fotos y comparto videos con mis amigos.
—¿Y hablas con tu familia en Guatemala?
—Claro. Hacemos una videollamada cada domingo. Cuando no contesto el teléfono, mando un mensaje.
—¿Me pasas el enlace de tu canal?
—Sí, te lo mando ahora. Nos conectamos en línea esta noche, ¿va?
—¡Perfecto!

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
How does this person stay close to family that lives far away? Find three different set B words in the conversation and say what each one means. Notice: every sentence uses present tense you already know — the only new part is the vocabulary.
🌎 CULTURA · GUATEMALA
El k'iche' en línea

Guatemala is home to more than 20 Indigenous Maya languages; k'iche' alone has over a million speakers. For decades those languages had almost no presence in national media — but that is changing online. Young Guatemalan creators now use las redes sociales to publicar short lessons, compartir music, and post word-of-the-day videos in Maya languages on YouTube and TikTok. Technology that could have erased small languages is being used to keep them alive and to conectar a new generation, en línea, with languages far older than Spanish itself. Every module this year you'll visit a different corner of the Spanish-speaking world; this one is Guatemala.

★ LA VIRTUD · SERVICE

Rigoberta Menchú Tum (Guatemala, b. 1959), a K'iche' Maya activist, won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1992 for a life spent in service — using every tool she had to carry the testimony of her people to a world that would not otherwise listen. Long before social media, her chosen tool was the same one at the heart of today's lesson: compartir, sharing. Service means putting your voice or your platform to work for someone else, and every time you compartes or publicas something that truly helps another person, you practice a small version of what she did.
