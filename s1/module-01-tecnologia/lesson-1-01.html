📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You already narrate your day in the present tense — today we point it at the screen in your hand.
Welcome to Module 1. This unit is about la tecnología y la comunicación — how we connect, and the words for the devices we do it with. Today has one job: bring back your Spanish 1 present tense habits and load in a first set of technology words — vocab set A: devices & screens. No new grammar today; we're re-lighting the engine you already built and giving it something new to talk about. Later this week you'll add the yo-irregular verbs (tengo, hago, pongo…) — but not until the vocabulary feels comfortable.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name eight core technology words — devices, screens, and two everyday actions (descargar, cargar).
2
I will be able to reactivate the present tense to say what I do with technology (uso, descargo, cargo).
3
I will be able to read a short paragraph about technology and pick out the devices and actions I recognize.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

Switch your Spanish back on — and look at your screen

You use technology every hour you're awake. That makes it the perfect place to restart your Spanish: you already know the ideas, so you only need the words. Read this aloud and notice how much you follow without translating: Todos los días uso mi celular. Descargo música y una aplicación nueva. Por la noche cargo el teléfono. The verbs are plain present tense — the same engine you ran all last year.

📚 PART 2 · VOCABULARIO A · DISPOSITIVOS Y PANTALLAS 12 min

Listen, then say each one back

Click the 🔊 and repeat aloud in a clear voice. Nouns come with el or la — learn the article with the word, every time. The last two are verbs (infinitives): you'll conjugate them in Part 3.

⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Notice the family resemblance: cargar is "to charge/load," and des—cargar literally "un-load" → to download. Say them as a pair. And la aplicación is often shortened in speech to la app — but the full word keeps its accent on the last syllable: aplicación.
🧠 PART 3 · REPASO · EL PRESENTE CON -AR 8 min

The engine you already have

This isn't new — it's the regular -ar present tense from Spanish 1, pointed at technology. Two of today's words (descargar, cargar) are regular -ar verbs, and so is usar (to use). Drop the -ar, add the ending:

Subjectendingusar → to usedescargar → to download
yo-ousodescargo
tú-asusasdescargas
usted / él / ella-ausadescarga
nosotros/as-amosusamosdescargamos
ustedes / ellos / ellas-anusandescargan

So: Yo uso la computadora portátil. Tú descargas una aplicación. Ella carga el celular. Same endings you already know — new nouns to fill the sentence.

📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

«Me llamo Ixchel y vivo en Quetzaltenango, Guatemala. Todos los días uso mi celular para hablar con mi familia. En la escuela trabajo con una computadora portátil: escribo en el teclado y leo en la pantalla. Para entrar necesito mi contraseña. Me gusta descargar música y una aplicación para aprender el idioma maya k’iche’. Por la noche siempre cargo el celular.»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find all eight of today's words in Ixchel's paragraph. Which two are verbs (actions) and which six are nouns (things)? Notice every verb is just the plain present tense you reviewed in Part 3.
🌎 CULTURA · GUATEMALA
Un idioma antiguo en una pantalla nueva

Guatemala is the heart of the Maya world: alongside Spanish, its people speak more than 20 living Maya languages, including k’iche’, kaqchikel, and q’eqchi’. Today young Guatemalans are carrying those languages onto the very devices in your vocab list — there are Maya-language aplicaciones, keyboards, and social-media accounts teaching words on a pantalla. When Ixchel downloads an app to study k’iche’, she's doing something people have done for 4,000 years — passing on her language — with a brand-new tool. (In the full course this pairs with a short clip of a Maya-language creator.)

★ LA VIRTUD · SERVICE

Rigoberta Menchú Tum (Guatemala, born 1959), a K’iche’ Maya activist, won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1992 for a lifetime spent in service to her people — carrying their needs and their voice to a world that had ignored them. Service means using what you have to help someone besides yourself, and today young Guatemalans do exactly that with the devices in your vocab list: a celular and an aplicación can serve a family member far away or a whole community trying to keep its language alive. When Ixchel downloads an app to study k’iche’, or calls her family on her celular, she's using technology the way Menchú used her voice — in service of others.
