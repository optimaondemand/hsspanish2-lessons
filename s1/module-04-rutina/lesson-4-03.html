★ Los verbos reflexivos (a fondo) — el pronombre y dónde va
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You already know the words. Today we open the engine that makes them move.
For two days you've been collecting routine verbs and eyeing that little -se tag on the end. Today it finally does something. A reflexive verb describes an action the subject does to or for themselves — and that idea rides on a reflexive pronoun: me, te, se, nos, os, se. Three things to nail down today: (1) match the pronoun to the subject, (2) tell a reflexive verb from its non-reflexive twin (lavar vs. lavarse, acostar vs. acostarse), and (3) know where the pronoun goes — right before a conjugated verb, or hooked onto the end of an infinitive. No new vocabulary today; just the machinery.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to match each reflexive pronoun (me / te / se / nos / os / se) to its subject and place it right before the conjugated verb.
2
I will be able to tell a reflexive verb from its non-reflexive twin (lavar vs. lavarse, acostar vs. acostarse) by asking who receives the action.
3
I will be able to attach the pronoun to an infinitive after voy a, necesito, or tengo que (voy a levantarme = me voy a levantar).
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

Two sentences, one tiny word of difference

Read both aloud and feel the difference: Ana despierta a su hermano. (Ana wakes up her brother.) vs. Ana se despierta. (Ana wakes up — herself.) The only change is se. In the first, the action lands on someone else; in the second, it bends back on Ana. That "bending back" is the whole idea of a reflexive verb — and today you'll learn exactly how to build and place it.

🧠 PART 3 · EL PRONOMBRE Y SU LUGAR 12 min

1 · The full pronoun set — it must match the subject

Every reflexive verb travels with a partner pronoun, and the pronoun changes with the subject. The verb conjugates exactly as it normally would; the pronoun is what makes it reflexive. In the present tense it sits right before the conjugated verb.

SubjectPronounlevantarse (regular -ar)
yomeme levanto
tútete levantas
usted / él / ellasese levanta
nosotros/asnosnos levantamos
vosotros/as (España)osos levantáis
ustedes / ellos / ellassese levantan

Notice se does double duty — it covers the third person singular and plural. And the stem changes you learned in Module 2 still apply: despertarse → me despierto (e→ie), acostarse → me acuesto (o→ue), vestirse → me visto (e→i). The pronoun never stops the boot.

2 · Reflexive vs. non-reflexive — the same verb, two jobs

Many verbs come in a pair: one form for doing the action to yourself (reflexive, with the pronoun) and one for doing it to someone or something else (no pronoun). Ask one question: who receives the action?

Reflexive — action bends back on youNon-reflexive — action lands on someone/something else
Yo me lavo. (I wash up / wash myself.)Yo lavo el coche. (I wash the car.)
Ella se despierta. (She wakes up.)Ella despierta a su hijo. (She wakes up her son.)
Nosotros nos acostamos. (We go to bed.)Nosotros acostamos al bebé. (We put the baby to bed.)

The shortcut: if the doer and the receiver are the same person → reflexive pronoun. If the receiver is someone else → drop the pronoun. That's the whole difference between lavarse and lavar, or acostarse and acostar.

3 · Where the pronoun goes

There are two legal spots for the pronoun. When there's one conjugated verb, the pronoun goes right in front of it. When an infinitive is in the sentence (after voy a, necesito, quiero, tengo que…), you get a choice: put the pronoun before the conjugated verb, or attach it to the end of the infinitive — both are correct.

SituationWhere the pronoun goesExample
One conjugated verbbefore the verb (only option)Me ducho por la mañana.
voy a + infinitiveattached to the infinitive…Voy a ducharme ahora.
voy a + infinitive…or before the conjugated verbMe voy a duchar ahora.
necesito / tengo que + infinitiveattached to the infinitiveNecesito levantarme temprano.

Two rules to keep straight: the pronoun still has to match the subject (you say voy a levantarme, never voy a levantarse for "I"), and you can't leave it floating between the two verbs (✗ voy me a duchar). Front of the conjugated verb, or hooked to the infinitive — nowhere in between.

📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Diana lives in Lima, Perú. Read her morning and hunt for two things: the reflexive verbs (she does the action to herself) and the one verb that lands on someone else.

«Hola, me llamo Diana. Entre semana me despierto a las seis, pero no me levanto en seguida — me quedo cinco minutos más en la cama. Luego me ducho, me visto con el uniforme y me lavo los dientes. Mi hermana pequeña no se despierta sola, así que yo la despierto a ella. Antes de salir, tengo que peinarme muy rápido, porque el autobús no espera. Por la noche voy a acostarme temprano… casi siempre.»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Answer in your head: Why is it me despierto (Diana) but la despierto a ella (her sister)? — because the receiver changes: herself vs. someone else. And find the infinitives with a pronoun hooked on: peinarme after tengo que, and acostarme after voy a. Both could also be written me tengo que peinar / me voy a acostar.
🌎 CULTURA · PERÚ
Despertarse bajo la garúa de Lima

Diana se despierta in Lima, one of the largest desert cities on earth — it almost never rains there. Yet for much of the year (roughly May to November) the capital wakes up under a thick, gray coastal fog called la garúa. The sky stays so low and colorless that Limeños affectionately call it «cielo panza de burro» — "donkey-belly sky." Waking up, levantarse, and getting going under that soft gray light is simply what a Lima morning feels like. A small reminder that the same verb, me despierto, describes mornings that look and feel completely different across the Spanish-speaking world. (In the full course this pairs with a short photo set of Lima's coast at dawn.)

★ LA VIRTUD · SELF-GOVERNMENT

Santa Rosa de Lima (Perú, 1586–1617), the first person born in the Americas to be canonized a saint and the patroness of Lima and of Perú, is remembered for self-government — the quiet work of ruling oneself. She ordered her whole day by hand: fixed hours for prayer, for work in her small garden that helped support her family, and for caring for the sick and the poor. Today's grammar is a fitting mirror of that virtue. A reflexive verb, after all, is the grammar of things you do to yourself — me levanto, me acuesto — and building a good routine is exactly the daily, self-directed work self-government asks of us. The pronoun says it out loud: the person shaping your day is you.
