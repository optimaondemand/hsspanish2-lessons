🧠 PART 3 · LO QUE EL QUIZ VA A REVISAR 10 min

Three things, one page

The Grammar Quiz checks exactly three moves you've practiced all module. Here they are side by side — read each example aloud.

1. Pronoun placement. With one conjugated verb, the pronoun goes before it. With a verb + infinitive (like voy a… or after antes de), it can go before the first verb or attach to the infinitive.

SituationWhere the pronoun goesExample
One conjugated verbBefore the verbMe despierto a las seis.
Verb + infinitive (voy a…)Before the first verb or attached to the infinitiveMe voy a duchar. = Voy a ducharme.
After antes de / después deAttached to the infinitiveAntes de acostarme, leo un poco.

2. Stem-changes. Several routine verbs change their stem vowel in every form except nosotros/as. The pronoun still matches the subject.

Subjectdespertarse (e→ie)acostarse (o→ue)vestirse (e→i)
yome despiertome acuestome visto
túte despiertaste acuestaste vistes
usted / él / ellase despiertase acuestase viste
nosotros/asnos despertamosnos acostamosnos vestimos
ustedes / ellos / ellasse despiertanse acuestanse visten

Trap to avoid: nosotros keeps the plain stem (nos acostamos, not nos acuestamos).

3. Frequency-adverb placement. Most sit right before the verb or at the start/end of the sentence. Nunca goes before the verb, or use no + verb + nunca.

AdverbTypical spotExample
siempre / a veces / a menudoBefore the verbSiempre me peino por la mañana.
todos los díasStart or endMe levanto temprano todos los días.
nuncaBefore the verb, or no… nuncaNunca me duermo en clase. = No me duermo nunca en clase.

✅ TARGET 2 · stem-change

✅ TARGET 3 · frequency-adverb placement
Which sentence places the frequency adverb naturally? (meaning: I always wake up early)

Your turn, out loud: describe three parts of your day, each with one reflexive verb + one frequency adverb. Say all three first — then reveal a model to check yourself.

Todos los días me despierto a las seis y media. A veces me visto antes de desayunar, pero normalmente desayuno primero. Nunca me acuesto después de las once entre semana.
Notice: the pronoun (me) stays glued to each verb, the stem-changers change (me despierto, me visto), and each frequency adverb sits before the verb. Yours uses your own times — that's all the quiz asks.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 6 min

Un día con Diego, en Lima

Read it once for the story, then a second time hunting for every reflexive verb and frequency adverb. Everything here is from this module — think of it as the reading half of tomorrow's quiz.

Me llamo Diego y vivo en Lima, la capital del Perú, junto al océano Pacífico. Todos los días me despierto a las seis, pero casi siempre me levanto unos minutos después — la neblina gris de Lima invita a quedarse en la cama. Primero me ducho rápido; luego me visto con el uniforme del colegio y me peino frente al espejo. Casi nunca desayuno mucho: normalmente pan con palta y un vaso de jugo. Antes de salir, siempre me despido de mi abuela, que ya está despierta. Por la tarde, después de las clases, a veces me reúno con mis amigos cerca de la Plaza Mayor. Por la noche me acuesto a las diez y media y me duermo escuchando el mar a lo lejos. Mañana, otra vez, la misma rutina — y me gusta que sea así.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Answer out loud in Spanish: ¿A qué hora se despierta Diego? ¿Qué hace primero, y qué hace después? ¿Con qué frecuencia se reúne con sus amigos? Then count them — nine reflexive verbs and six frequency adverbs hide in Diego's day. Finding them is quiz practice.
🌎 CULTURA · PERÚ
Un país, tres mundos: costa, sierra y selva

Diego's foggy morning by the Pacific is only one Peruvian day. Perú is really three worlds in one country: the costa (the desert coast, where Lima sits under its famous winter garúa, a gray sea-mist); the sierra (the high Andes, home to Cusco and Machu Picchu, the heart of the old Inca empire); and the selva (the vast Amazon rainforest to the east). A student's rutina looks different in each: coastal Limeños wake to cool gray light, Andean families rise in thin, bright mountain air, and Amazonian towns start the day with river heat by dawn. Same country, same language — and, in the highlands, often quechua too — but three very different mornings. When you read a Peruvian routine, you're reading a geography. (In the full course this pairs with a short authentic map-and-photo gallery of the three regions.)

★ LA VIRTUD · SELF-GOVERNMENT

Santa Rosa de Lima (Lima, Perú, 1586–1617) — the first person born in the Americas to be canonized a saint — is the patron of the very city where Diego wakes up. What people remembered about her was not one grand act but a whole life ordered by her own hand: she rose early, kept fixed hours for work, prayer, and caring for the sick and poor, and chose the steady, disciplined path over comfort. That is self-government: ruling yourself through small, repeated daily choices rather than dramatic ones. It's exactly the virtue hiding inside a good rutina, and inside good study habits. Reviewing calmly today, a day early, instead of cramming tomorrow, is self-government you can practice this very afternoon.
