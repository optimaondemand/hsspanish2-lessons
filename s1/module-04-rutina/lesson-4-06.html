★ Un día en el Perú — de Lima a Cusco
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You have all the parts. Today you read them working together in a real country.
No new grammar and no new vocabulary today — this is a day to see it work. You'll combine the reflexive routine verbs (me despierto, me levanto, me acuesto) with the frequency words from yesterday (siempre, generalmente, a veces, nunca) inside two longer readings about daily life in Perú: one in coastal Lima, one high in the Andes in Cusco. Your real work today is interpretive reading — understanding who does what, when, and how often, and noticing how geography shapes a routine.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to read a paragraph that combines reflexive verbs and frequency words, and correctly answer how often a person does each thing.
2
I will be able to compare two Peruvian routines and explain how climate and place (coast vs. mountains) change a daily rhythm.
3
I will be able to describe a Peruvian mealtime custom and connect it to what I read.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Where you live shapes your day

Before you meet two Peruvian teenagers, think about your own morning. Does the weather where you live change your routine — when you get up, what you wear, how you get to school? In Perú the answer is dramatic: the coast and the mountains feel like two different worlds. Read this line aloud: —En Cusco las noches siempre son frías, así que me acuesto temprano. You already understand every word.

🧠 PART 3 · LAS DOS PIEZAS JUNTAS 8 min

Reflexive verb + frequency word, in one clean sentence

You are not learning anything new here — you're clicking two familiar pieces together. The frequency word usually sits right before the reflexive pronoun + verb, or at the front of the sentence. The reflexive pronoun (me / te / se) still stays glued to its verb. Watch the pattern:

Frequency wordReflexive verbFull sentence
Siempreme levantoSiempre me levanto a las seis.
Generalmenteme duchoGeneralmente me ducho por la mañana.
A vecesme acuestoA veces me acuesto tarde.
Nuncame despiertoNunca me despierto antes de las cinco.

Reminder from yesterday's nunca rule: Nunca me acuesto tarde is correct because nunca comes before the verb. If you flip it after the verb, you'd need no: No me acuesto nunca tarde. Same meaning. When you read today, notice each writer keeps the frequency word up front — that's the most natural spot.

📖 PART 5 · A LEER · DOS RUTINAS PERUANAS 8 min

Mateo, en Lima · Nayra, en Cusco

«Me llamo Mateo y vivo en Lima, la capital, justo al lado del mar. En invierno Lima casi nunca tiene sol: hay una neblina gris que llamamos la garúa. Todos los días me despierto a las seis y media y me levanto enseguida, porque el tráfico siempre es terrible. Generalmente desayuno rápido y tomo el micro (el autobús) al colegio. A veces almuerzo un cebiche cerca del mar, pero normalmente almuerzo en casa con mi familia. Nunca me acuesto antes de las once.»

«Yo soy Nayra y vivo en Cusco, en los Andes, a más de tres mil metros de altura. Aquí el sol es fuerte de día, pero las noches siempre son frías. Cada día me levanto temprano y casi siempre tomo un mate de coca caliente en el desayuno — ayuda con la altura. Generalmente almuerzo a la una: el almuerzo es la comida principal del día. Dos veces por semana ayudo a mi abuela en el mercado de San Pedro. Por la noche me acuesto temprano, porque a veces hace mucho frío.»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Underline every frequency word in both paragraphs (there are ten). Then ask: which routine sounds more like yours — Mateo's fast, foggy Lima morning, or Nayra's cold, high-altitude Andean day? Notice that both writers put the frequency word at the front of the sentence.

🌎 CULTURA · PERÚ
Un país, tres mundos: costa, sierra y selva

Perú is really three geographies stacked side by side, and each one gives its people a different daily rhythm. On the costa, Lima sits by the Pacific, where the winter garúa — a fine coastal fog — can hide the sun for weeks. Up in the sierra, Cusco (the old Inca capital) sits above 3,300 meters, so locals drink mate de coca to ease the thin mountain air and bundle up against cold nights. To the east lies the Amazon selva, hot and humid. One custom, though, crosses all three: el almuerzo, the midday meal, is the largest of the day, and many families still stop to eat it together. So when Mateo and Nayra both mention lunch at home, they're sharing the same Peruvian value from two very different climates. (In the full course this pairs with a short photo set — the garúa over Lima and the terraces above Cusco.)

★ LA VIRTUD · SELF-GOVERNMENT

Santa Rosa de Lima (Perú, 1586–1617), the first canonized saint of the Americas, lived her whole life in the same coastal city as Mateo. Her virtue was self-government — ruling one's own day so the good is done reliably, not just when it's easy. Notice that the frequency words you practiced today are the vocabulary of self-government itself: to say honestly “siempre” about a healthy habit, and “nunca” about one that harms you, is to have taken charge of the kind of steady rhythm Rosa modeled. Mateo and Nayra both order their days around family and the midday meal — small, self-chosen patterns are self-government in ordinary life.
