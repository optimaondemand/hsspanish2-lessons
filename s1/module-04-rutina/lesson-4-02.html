Lesson 4.02
★ Por la noche — How the Day Winds Down
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday your day started; today it ends — and you get the words that tell us when.
In Lesson 4.01 you collected the words for how your morning begins — despertarse, levantarse, ducharse. Today, on Day 2, we do the other half: the evening-and-night words for winding down — taking off your clothes, drying off, relaxing, resting, going to bed, falling asleep. Then we add a small but powerful set of time-of-day phrases (por la mañana / por la tarde / por la noche) and sequence words (antes de, después de, temprano, tarde) that let you line those actions up in order. Still no conjugating — just recognizing and matching the vocabulary. The grammar that makes these verbs move (me acuesto, te duermes…) is coming in Lesson 4.03; today we stock the shelves.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to recognize and pronounce the core evening-and-night words (quitarse la ropa, secarse, relajarse, acostarse, dormirse…) as vocabulary.
2
I will be able to use the time-of-day phrases por la mañana / por la tarde / por la noche and the sequence words antes de, después de, temprano, tarde to say roughly when something happens.
3
I will be able to match each evening action to the right Spanish word and place it correctly before or after another step.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

Rewind your evening

Think about last night. Somewhere between finishing homework and falling asleep, your day quietly shut itself down — you changed out of your clothes, maybe showered and dried off, relaxed for a while, and eventually went to bed. That whole stretch is por la noche, and it has its own small script, just like the morning did. Read this aloud and see how much you can already picture: Por la noche me relajo, me acuesto y me duermo.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
What is the last thing you usually do before you fall asleep at night?
📚 PART 2 · VOCABULARIO B — POR LA NOCHE 13 min

Listen, then say each one back

Here are the evening-and-night actions, in roughly the order they happen as the day ends. Click the 🔊, listen, and repeat each word aloud in a clear voice. Learn them as vocabulary for now. Notice that most end in -se (just like yesterday's morning words) — but keep an eye on the odd one out at the bottom.

  • quitarse la ropato take off one's clothes
  • ponerse la ropato put on one's clothes
  • secarseto dry off / dry oneself
  • relajarseto relax
  • acostarseto go to bed / lie down
  • dormirseto fall asleep
  • descansarto rest (no -se!)
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Two of these are exact opposites: quitarse la ropa (take clothes off) and ponerse la ropa (put clothes on) — you do one at night and the other in the morning. And look at the last word: descansar is the only one with no -se ending. That's a hint that it behaves a little differently from the others — we'll see exactly why in Lesson 4.03. For now, just file it away.
🔍 PART 3 · FÍJATE — LAS PALABRAS DEL TIEMPO 8 min

The words that say when

A routine isn't just a pile of actions — it's actions in order. These little phrases let you say when each step happens, with no grammar to conjugate. Click the 🔊 to hear each one, then read the table below to see how they group.

  • por la mañanain the morning
  • por la tardein the afternoon
  • por la nocheat night / in the evening
  • antes debefore
  • después deafter
  • tempranoearly
  • tardelate

Here's how they sort — the top three name a stretch of the day; the rest fix the order and the timing:

TypePhrasesWhat it tells you
Stretch of the daypor la mañana, por la tarde, por la nochewhich part of the day: morning, afternoon, or night
Orderantes de, después dewhether one step comes before or after another
Timingtemprano, tardewhether it happens early or late

A quick warning about one word: tarde shows up in two roles this year. On its own, tarde means late (Me acuesto tarde = I go to bed late). Inside the phrase por la tarde, it means afternoon. Same spelling, two jobs — the surrounding words tell you which one you're looking at.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 8 min

Match the word, place it in time

These check meaning and order only — no conjugating. Pick the answer that fits, and read the feedback.

✅ TRY IT · which word?
You step out of the shower and dry yourself with a towel. Which verb names that?
✅ TRY IT · which time phrase?
Which phrase would you use for something that happens at night?
✅ TRY IT · before or after?
First you go to bed, and then you fall asleep. So dormirse happens ___ acostarse.

Your turn: name the last three things you do at night, in order, using today's words — and try to slip in one time phrase (por la noche, antes de, tarde…). Just the vocabulary; no conjugating needed. Say it out loud, then reveal one possible answer.

Por la noche: relajarse → acostarse → dormirse. Another version: quitarse la ropa, descansar un poco, y acostarse — no muy tarde. Yours will use your own steps. In Lesson 4.03 you'll learn to turn acostarse into me acuesto (“I go to bed”) and say a whole routine in real sentences.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 4 min

Mateo, un estudiante de Cusco, Perú, tiene su rutina de la noche en una nota. Como la lista de Valentina en la lección 4.01, son solo los pasos — pero ahora con las palabras del tiempo:

La rutina de la noche de Mateo
Por la tarde, después del colegio: la tarea
8:00 — la cena con la familia
Después de la cena — relajarse un poco (música, un libro)
9:30 — quitarse la ropa y ponerse el pijama
9:40 — acostarse (no muy tarde, porque se levanta temprano)
9:50 — dormirse

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Cover the English and read Mateo's note top to bottom. Which step happens después de dinner? Does he go to bed temprano or tarde? And which two words from today's Part 2 list — secarse and descansar — don't appear on his note at all?
🌎 CULTURA · PERÚ
La noche peruana — la cena y los anticuchos

Mateo eats la cena at 8:00 — and that's normal in Perú, where dinner tends to be later and lighter than the big midday meal (el almuerzo is the main event of the day). On a warm night in Lima, families and friends step out por la noche to buy anticuchos — skewers of marinated beef heart grilled over charcoal at street carts — a beloved dish with roots reaching back to Andean and African cooking in colonial Perú. Up in the highlands around Cusco, nights turn cold fast once the sun drops, so an evening there often ends with a hot drink and an early bedtime. Learning the night words means learning the tables and streets they belong to. (Later this module you'll read more about daily life in the Peruvian sierra.)

★ LA VIRTUD · SELF-GOVERNMENT

Yesterday we met Santa Rosa de Lima (Perú, 1586–1617) through the discipline of beginning a day well. Self-government also governs how a day ends. Rosa ordered her evenings toward rest and quiet after work and prayer, and there is real wisdom in that ordinary rhythm: choosing to relajarse, to descansar, and to acostarse at a reasonable hour rather than tarde is a small daily act of ruling yourself well. Notice this week how the words you're learning — por la noche, antes de dormirse — describe not just a schedule but a choice you make for yourself, every night.
