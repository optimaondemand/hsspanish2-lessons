★ Di tu rutina en voz alta — graba la Tarea oral
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you wrote your routine. Today you say it out loud — and record it for a grade.
Nothing new today — no new verbs, no new grammar. This is a presentational speaking day: you take the same daily routine you built all week and move it from the page to your voice. You'll warm up your pronunciation, rehearse a smooth, ordered description (primero… luego… después…), and then draft and record your Tarea oral — a short spoken description of your day, graded on a presentational rubric (20 pts). Speaking a language you can already write is mostly about one thing: doing it out loud, on purpose, until it flows.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to pronounce the routine verbs clearly, with pure Spanish vowels and the stress on the right syllable (me despierto, me acuesto).
2
I will be able to speak a connected description of my daily routine in order, using sequencing words and frequency adverbs to link my sentences.
3
I will be able to draft and record my Tarea oral, matching what the presentational rubric rewards.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

Warm up your mouth, not just your memory

Writing is silent; speaking is physical. Before you record anything, read this line aloud three times, a little slower each time, and feel where your voice pushes hardest: —Primero me despierto, luego me ducho y después me visto. Spanish vowels stay short and clean — no gliding the way English does. Say me ducho, not “may DOO-chOH.” That crispness is half of sounding fluent.

📚 PART 2 · PRONUNCIACIÓN — ESCUCHA Y REPITE 9 min

Shadow the audio, word by word

Everything here is review from Days 1–7 — nothing new. But this time the goal isn't meaning, it's sound. Click the 🔊, then repeat immediately, copying the rhythm exactly. Notice the bold syllable: that's where the stress lands. Aim to match the voice, not beat it.

La rutina (say each one, then say the me form after it)
  • despertarseto wake up → me despierto
  • levantarseto get up → me levanto
  • ducharseto shower → me ducho
  • cepillarse los dientesto brush teeth → me cepillo los dientes
  • peinarseto comb hair → me peino
  • vestirseto get dressed → me visto
  • acostarseto go to bed → me acuesto

🧠 PART 3 · SONAR CON FLUIDEZ 8 min

Three habits that make you sound fluent

No new grammar — just three sound habits to hold onto while you record. Each one is small; together they are the difference between “reading words” and “speaking Spanish.”

HabitWhat to doHear it in…
Pure vowelsKeep a-e-i-o-u short and clean; don't let them glide.me ducho · normalmente
Right stressSay the bold syllable a touch louder — it often lands on the stem-change.me despierto · me acuesto · me visto
Link, don't stopLet the sequencing word glide you into the next verb — no long pause.Primero me ducho… luego me visto… después desayuno.

Here is a full model — the shape your recording should take. Click 🔊 to hear it spoken at a natural, unhurried pace, then read along:

Hola. Me llamo Ana. Normalmente me despierto a las seis y media. Primero me ducho, luego me visto y después me cepillo los dientes. Todos los días desayuno con mi familia. Por la noche nunca me acuesto muy tarde. ¡Y esa es mi rutina!

Count them: a greeting, a name, five ordered actions with sequencing words, a frequency adverb in almost every sentence, and a closing line. That is exactly what earns full marks — and it fits comfortably in 45–60 seconds.

🧠 PART 4 · ENSAYA Y GRABA 11 min

Three quick checks, then you draft your own

Each of these targets something the rubric listens for — stress, sequencing, and a natural opening. Choose, then say the whole correct version out loud before moving on.

✅ TRY IT · where does the stress go?
You want to say me despierto (I wake up) so it sounds right. Which syllable do you say a little louder?
✅ TRY IT · open the sequence
You're about to describe the very first thing you do each morning. Which word should start that sentence?
✅ TRY IT · start the recording well
Which is the most natural way to open a spoken presentation of your routine?

Now draft your routine out loud

Say your real day from start to finish — a greeting and your name, then five actions in order (sequencing words linking them), a frequency adverb on most sentences, and a closing line. Say it twice before you reveal the frame below to check your shape.

Hola, me llamo ______. Normalmente me despierto a las ______. Primero me ______, luego me ______ y después me ______. Todos los días ______. Por la noche nunca me acuesto ______. ¡Y esa es mi rutina!

Fill each blank with your own habit and time. Keep the pronoun (me) before every verb, keep the stress on the bold syllables from Part 3, and let the sequencing words glide you forward. That filled-in version is your Tarea oral script — rehearse it twice more, then record.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER · UN MODELO HABLADO 5 min

«Hola, me llamo Mateo y vivo en Arequipa, Perú. Así es mi rutina. Normalmente me despierto a las seis; el sol de la sierra entra temprano por la ventana. Primero me ducho con agua caliente porque hace frío, luego me visto y después me peino rápido. Todos los días me cepillo los dientes antes de salir. Desayuno pan con queso y un mate. Por la noche casi nunca me acuesto tarde, porque mañana me despierto otra vez con el sol. ¡Y esa es mi rutina diaria!»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Read Mateo's routine aloud once, at his pace, as if it were your recording. Notice the shape: he opens with a greeting and his name, links every action with a sequencing word, drops a frequency adverb into most sentences, and closes with a clean final line. Then swap in your own habits and say it again — that is your Tarea oral.
🎤 SE ABRE HOY · TAREA ORAL NOW OPEN IN CANVAS

20 pts
Tarea oral · Mi rutina diaria
Record a 45–60 second spoken description of your daily routine. Presentational (speak it — don't read a full script word-for-word). Graded on a presentational rubric.
What the rubric rewards
• At least five reflexive verbs, each with the pronoun before it
• Sequencing words linking your actions in order
• A frequency adverb in most sentences
• Clear pronunciation and a smooth, unhurried pace
Recording tip: use the audio/video recorder built into the Canvas submission. Do a quick test clip first, then your real take. It's fine to glance at your frame from Part 4 — just don't read it straight through; let it come out as speech.
🌎 CULTURA · PERÚ
La sobremesa — cuando hablar es la costumbre

In Perú — as across the Spanish-speaking world — a shared meal rarely ends when the food does. The sobremesa is the time at the table after eating, when family and friends stay to talk, sometimes for an hour or more. It's a daily reminder that speaking is a social habit, not a performance: you narrate your day, you listen to others narrate theirs. When you record your Tarea oral today, you're practicing exactly the everyday skill a Peruvian teenager uses at every sobremesa — telling the ordinary story of a day, out loud, to someone who's listening. (In the full course this pairs with a short clip of a family sobremesa and a listening check.)

★ LA VIRTUD · SELF-GOVERNMENT

Santa Rosa de Lima (Perú, 1586–1617), the first saint of the Americas, practiced self-government not only in how she ordered her days but in how she spoke — with care and restraint. Recording your voice takes that same quieter self-government: the discipline to rehearse, to slow down, to try a line again instead of rushing the take. Ella hablaba con calma y se gobernaba a sí misma cada día. Speaking well, like living well, rewards the person willing to go slowly and rule their own pace on purpose. Give your recording that same unhurried care.
