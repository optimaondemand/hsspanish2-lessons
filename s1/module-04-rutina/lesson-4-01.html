★ La rutina diaria — Por la mañana
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Every day you've talked about — sports, free time, school — starts the exact same way: you wake up and get ready.
In Módulo 3 you described what you do for fun — los deportes and free-time activities. Now we rewind to the very start of the day. This module is La rutina diaria (the daily routine), and today we open the door on the morning. Your only job in this lesson is to collect the words for a typical morning — despertarse, ducharse, peinarse… — and get them into your ear with audio. No new grammar today: we learn these as vocabulary first, then put them to work in Lesson 4.03.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to recognize and say the ten morning-routine verbs of vocab set A (despertarse, levantarse, ducharse, bañarse…).
2
I will be able to match each verb to the everyday morning action it names and place a few of them in a sensible order.
3
I will be able to recognize the -se ending as the “self” marker I first met in Spanish 1, and reflect on my own morning.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

Before the game, before the fun…

Think back to Módulo 3 — you talked about jugar al fútbol, correr, nadar, everything you do for fun. But none of that happens until you've done the quiet, ordinary things first: you woke up, you got out of bed, you got ready. That's la rutina diaria, and this is where every day actually begins. Today we just gather the words.

📚 PART 2 · VOCAB SET A — POR LA MAÑANA 12 min

Listen, then say each one back

Here is a whole morning, in words. Click the 🔊 and repeat each one aloud. Notice they all end in -se — that's the “self” marker you met in Spanish 1, telling you the action is something you do to yourself. Today just learn them as words; you'll conjugate them in Lesson 4.03.

  • por la mañanain the morning
  • despertarseto wake up
  • levantarseto get up / get out of bed
  • ducharseto shower
  • bañarseto bathe / take a bath
  • lavarse los dientesto brush your teeth
  • lavarse la carato wash your face
  • afeitarseto shave
  • peinarseto comb / do your hair
  • vestirseto get dressed
  • maquillarseto put on makeup
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Go back through the list and play it in the order you actually do things: maybe despertarse → levantarse → ducharse → lavarse los dientes → vestirse. Notice which words share the same ending (-arse) — most of today's are -ar verbs, and only vestirse ends in -irse.
🧠 PART 3 · REPASO — LA SEÑAL -SE 6 min

A quick reminder, not a new rule

You already met this idea in Spanish 1, so this is repaso (review), not new grammar. Many verbs come in two flavors: a plain one, and one with -se on the end. That little -se means the action loops back on the person doing it — you don't just wash something, you wash yourself. Every word in today's list carries it. For now, all you need to do is recognize the ending.

Plain verb (you know it)With -se (today's words)What the -se signals
lavar — to wash (something)lavarse la carayou wash your own face
peinar — to comb (someone)peinarseyou comb your own hair
despertar — to wake (someone)despertarseyou wake yourself up

That's as far as we go today. In Lesson 4.03 you'll turn these into full sentences — me despierto, te duchas, se viste — with the reflexive pronouns. Right now: just the words.

📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

«¡Hola! Me llamo Valeria y vivo en Arequipa, Perú. Por la mañana mi casa es un poco caótica. Primero suena la alarma — es la hora de despertarse. Luego hay que levantarse (¡lo más difícil!), ducharse rápido y lavarse los dientes. Mi hermano necesita más tiempo para peinarse, y mi mamá siempre tiene que maquillarse antes del trabajo. Al final, todos tenemos que vestirse y salir corriendo.»

🌎 CULTURA · PERÚ
El desayuno peruano

In Perú, the morning routine ends where the day's energy begins — at the breakfast table. A classic desayuno might be pan con palta (bread with avocado), fresh cheese, and a hot cup of quinua or mate de coca to take the edge off the thin Andean air. In cities like Lima and Arequipa, street bakeries fill with the smell of warm pan francés before dawn. Learning to despertarse y ducharse in Spanish is step one; soon you'll be describing what you eat right after. (In the full course this pairs with a short photo gallery of Peruvian breakfasts.)

★ LA VIRTUD · SELF-GOVERNMENT

Santa Rosa de Lima (Perú, 1586–1617) was the first person born in the Americas to be named a saint, and she is remembered for a life of remarkable self-government — ordering each day around prayer, work, and caring for the sick and the poor of Lima. No one imposed that order on her; she chose it and kept it, hour by hour. As we begin this module, notice that the vocabulary of a rutina diaria is really the vocabulary of governing your own day.
