🧠 PART 3 · REPASO DE GRAMÁTICA — UN VISTAZO FINAL 8 min

Everything the quiz tests, on one page

You have seen all of this across Days 3–5. Read each table once, slowly, and notice which row you'd want to look at twice.

1. Reflexive pronoun — match the subject, place it right.

SubjectPronounExample (levantarse)
yomeme levanto
tútete levantas
usted / él / ellasese levanta
nosotros/asnosnos levantamos
ustedes / ellos / ellassese levantan

Placement: right before the conjugated verb (me ducho), or attached to an infinitive/gerund (voy a ducharme / estoy duchándome).

2. Stem-changes still happen inside the reflexive.

InfinitiveChangeyo form
despertarsee → ieme despierto
acostarseo → ueme acuesto
dormirseo → ueme duermo
vestirsee → ime visto

3. Frequency adverbs — the safe spots.

AdverbSafe placementExample
siempre / nuncabefore the verbSiempre me despierto temprano.
todos los díasstart or endMe ducho todos los días.
a veces / a menudousually at the startA veces me acuesto tarde.

Remember the nunca rule: nunca before the verb needs no no (Nunca me duermo en clase); after the verb it does (No me duermo nunca en clase). Before-the-verb is the clean choice.

Your turn, one last rehearsal: say three true sentences about your routine — one with a stem-changing reflexive, one with siempre or nunca, and one with todos los días. Say them out loud, then reveal a model to check your placement and stem-changes.

Todos los días me despierto a las seis y media. Siempre me visto antes de desayunar. Nunca me acuesto después de las once. Check yourself: pronoun right before each verb (me), stem-changes intact (me despierto, me visto, me acuesto), and each frequency word before the verb or at the front.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER · LA RUTINA DE UN GUÍA EN MACHU PICCHU 7 min

One last passage — every verb and frequency word in it is review. Read for the whole picture, then do the self-check.

Me llamo Daniel y soy guía turístico en Machu Picchu, en el sur del Perú. Mi rutina no es como la de otras personas. Todos los días me despierto a las cuatro y media de la mañana, porque los primeros turistas llegan muy temprano. Siempre me levanto en la oscuridad, me ducho rápido y me visto con ropa cómoda y una chaqueta, porque en la montaña hace frío al amanecer. Casi nunca desayuno mucho — solo un mate y pan — porque camino todo el día.

A veces trabajo diez horas seguidas subiendo y bajando la ciudadela inca. Por eso, por la noche siempre me acuesto temprano y me duermo en seguida — ¡estoy agotado! Pero amo mi trabajo: todos los días veo salir el sol sobre una de las maravillas del mundo.

🌎 CULTURA · PERÚ
Machu Picchu y el reloj del sol

Daniel's pre-dawn routine isn't unusual for a Machu Picchu guide. Built by the Inca in the 15th century high in the Andes and brought to the wider world's attention in 1911, the stone citadel was engineered around the sun: certain windows and the carved Intihuatana stone (“the hitching post of the sun”) align with the solstices. The Inca ordered daily life by the sun's rhythm, and today's visitors still climb before dawn to watch it rise over the peaks. It's a fitting close to a module about la rutina: for centuries, in this corner of Perú, the deepest daily routine of all has been to rise with the sun. (In the full course this pairs with a short virtual tour of the citadel.)

★ LA VIRTUD · SELF-GOVERNMENT

Santa Rosa de Lima (Isabel Flores de Oliva, 1586–1617), the first person born in the Americas to be canonized a saint, spent her short life mastering not grand things but ordinary ones — her hours, her appetites, her daily order — through self-government. This whole module has quietly been about the same virtue. Learning to say me despierto, me acuesto, siempre, nunca is also learning to see your own day clearly and choose how to rule it. As Module 4 closes, notice this: the students who did a little every day — who treated “siempre” as a promise to themselves — are the ones walking into today's quiz calm. That is self-government, and it will serve you long after the Spanish.
