Lesson 4.04
★ Reflexives in Use — Narrating a Real Day
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you built the tool. Today you pick it up and actually describe a day.
No new rules today — just using them. You already conjugate the stem-changing routine verbs (me despierto, me acuesto, me visto). Now we put them to work in real sentences and in a short reading. The one move to notice: when a reflexive verb follows voy a… or tengo que…, it stays as an infinitive and the pronoun rides along — voy a ducharme, tengo que levantarme. That's not a new verb; it's the same reflexive you know, in a new position.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use the stem-changing routine verbs (despertarse e→ie, acostarse/dormirse o→ue, vestirse e→i) correctly in full present-tense sentences.
2
I will be able to place the reflexive pronoun correctly after ir a and tener que (voy a ducharme / tengo que levantarme).
3
I will be able to read a short paragraph about someone's daily routine and understand who does what, when, and how often.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Say your morning, in order

Before we read someone else's routine, wake up your own. In your head, run the first ten minutes of a school morning as Spanish sentences: Me despierto… me levanto… me ducho… me visto… Notice how the little me shows up on every verb — because every one of these actions happens to you.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
On a normal school day, which describes you best?
📚 PART 2 · REPASO RÁPIDO 8 min

Nothing new — just tune your ear

These are the routine verbs and connectors you already own from earlier this week. Click the 🔊 and say each one back once more, so the sounds are ready when you read and speak today. The color note is just a reminder of each verb's stem change.

  • despertarseto wake up (e→ie)
  • levantarseto get up
  • ducharseto shower
  • vestirseto get dressed (e→i)
  • acostarseto go to bed (o→ue)
  • dormirseto fall asleep (o→ue)

And the ordering words you already know — they'll hold your routine together like glue:

  • primerofirst
  • luegothen / next

  • finalmentefinally
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Chain four verbs with the connectors out loud: Primero me despierto, luego me levanto, después me ducho y finalmente me visto. Say it three times, a little faster each time — you're rehearsing the exact shape of today's reading.
🧠 PART 3 · EL PRONOMBRE EN LOS INFINITIVOS 9 min

Where the pronoun goes with voy a and tengo que

You already use ir a + infinitive ("I'm going to…") and tener que + infinitive ("I have to…"). When the second verb is reflexive, you have two correct choices, and they mean exactly the same thing: put the pronoun before the whole phrase, or attach it to the end of the infinitive. Either way, the pronoun still matches the subject.

PhrasePronoun beforePronoun attached (one word)
ir a + ducharse (yo)Me voy a duchar.Voy a ducharme.
tener que + levantarse (yo)Me tengo que levantar.Tengo que levantarme.
querer + acostarse (él/ella)Se quiere acostar.Quiere acostarse.

The pronoun never changes to fit the infinitive — it fits the subject. Yo → me, tú → te, él/ella → se. So it's voy a ducharme (yo) but va a ducharse (él).

Watch the stem change stay put too: the conjugated verb (voy, tengo, quiere) is what carries the sentence, so the reflexive verb simply stays in its clean infinitive form — no stem change needed there. Compare Me acuesto tarde (conjugated, o→ue) with Voy a acostarme tarde (infinitive, no change).

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 12 min

Put the pieces together

✅ TRY IT · after tener que
"Es tarde. Yo tengo que ___ ahora mismo." (It's late. I have to get up right now.)
✅ TRY IT · same meaning?
Which sentence means exactly the same as "Me voy a duchar"?
✅ TRY IT · conjugated, with the stem change
"Normalmente mi hermano ___ a las once de la noche." (Normally my brother goes to bed at eleven at night.)

Your turn: say "Tomorrow I'm going to get dressed quickly and go to bed early." Use voy a… twice with the pronoun attached. Say it out loud first, then reveal.

Mañana voy a vestirme rápido y voy a acostarme temprano. Both reflexive verbs stay as infinitives after voy a, and the pronoun me (because it's yo) attaches to each one. You could also say me voy a vestir… y me voy a acostar… — same meaning.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

La rutina de Valentina

«Hola, soy Valentina y vivo en Arequipa, en el sur del Perú. De lunes a viernes tengo que levantarme temprano, a las seis menos cuarto. Primero me despierto —Arequipa casi siempre tiene cielo azul y mucho sol— y luego me levanto enseguida. Me ducho, me visto con el uniforme y desayuno pan con queso. A veces mi mamá me despierta si el despertador no suena, pero normalmente me despierto sola. Por la noche me acuesto antes de las diez y me duermo enseguida, porque estoy cansada. Los fines de semana es diferente: no tengo que levantarme temprano, así que duermo hasta tarde. ¡Es mi día favorito!»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find the two infinitive constructions (tengo que levantarme). Then spot the one verb that is not reflexive: mi mamá me despierta. Why? Because the action lands on Valentina, not on her mom — the receiver is a different person, so it's not reflexive for the mom.
✅ ¿COMPRENDES? · check the reading
According to the paragraph, what does Valentina do on weekends (los fines de semana)?
🌎 CULTURA · PERÚ
Arequipa, la Ciudad Blanca

Valentina's city, Arequipa, is Perú's second-largest and is nicknamed la Ciudad Blanca — the White City — for its historic buildings made of sillar, a pale volcanic stone from the three volcanoes that ring the town, including the towering Misti. That high, dry, sun-bright climate shapes daily routine: mornings start early and clear, and lunch — not dinner — is the big meal of the day. When Valentina says Arequipa casi siempre tiene cielo azul, she's describing a real fact of life at 2,300 meters. (In the full course this pairs with a short photo tour of the sillar architecture and a listening clip.)

★ LA VIRTUD · SELF-GOVERNMENT

Santa Rosa de Lima (Perú, 1586–1617), the first canonized saint of the Americas, ordered every day around prayer, work, and care for the sick and poor — not because anyone made her, but because she chose to govern herself. Notice that today's grammar is the grammar of self-government: tengo que levantarme, voy a acostarme temprano. Every reflexive verb is literally an action you direct at yourself — and choosing a steady routine, a little each day, is self-government in practice.
