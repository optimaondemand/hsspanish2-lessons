🧠 PART 3 · EL SISTEMA COMPLETO DE VERBOS DE BOTA 8 min

All four stem changes on one page

Here is the whole boot family you built across Lessons 2.03–2.06, gathered in one table. The idea is always the same: in the present tense the stressed vowel of the stem changes in every form except nosotros/as — the four changed forms trace the shape of a boot. Only the kind of change differs. Read the yo column aloud.

CambioInfinitivosyoEjemplo
e→ieempezar, querer, preferir, pensarempiezo, quiero, prefiero, piensoLa clase empieza a las ocho.
o→uepoder, almorzar, dormir, volverpuedo, almuerzo, duermo, vuelvoYo almuerzo al mediodía.
e→ipedir, servir, repetirpido, sirvo, repitoEn el recreo yo pido una baleada.
u→uejugarjuegoPor la tarde yo juego al fútbol.

Three things to hold onto. (1) The endings are the plain present endings (-o, -es, -e, -emos, -en…) — only the stem vowel changes. (2) Nosotros keeps the plain stem: empezamos, almorzamos, pedimos, jugamos — no change (that's the "sole" of the boot). (3) Some schedule verbs are not boot verbs — terminar is regular: la clase termina a la una. On the quiz, always ask first: is this even a boot verb?

🌎 CULTURA · HONDURAS Y EL SALVADOR
El tiempo antes del reloj: Copán

Long before anyone in the region said son las dos de la tarde, the Maya of Copán — a great city in western Honduras, today a UNESCO World Heritage site — were among the most precise timekeepers in the ancient world. Their carved stone estelas and the famous Escalinata de los Jeroglíficos (Hieroglyphic Stairway, the longest Maya text ever found) record dates using the Long Count calendar, tracking days across thousands of years with astonishing accuracy. Honduras and El Salvador share this Maya inheritance: the same civilization that reached into El Salvador's highlands. So when you organize your day into horas and a horario, you're joining a very old Central American habit — measuring time carefully, and building life around it. (In the full course this pairs with a short look at a photo of the Copán stairway and a Long Count date.)

★ LA VIRTUD · COURAGE

All module long you have followed Óscar Romero (El Salvador, 1917–1980), the archbishop of San Salvador who showed up at the same hour every Sunday to speak for the poor and oppressed, even though it was dangerous, and was assassinated for it in 1980. His courage was not one heroic instant but steadiness kept on schedule, again and again. Take your self-check honestly and your quiz calmly — that same steady courage is what finishes a module well.
