Lesson 2.03
★ Los verbos «de bota» — cuando la raíz cambia de sonido
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The middle of the verb changes its sound — but not in every person, and there's a reason why.
We're still in the present tense you consolidated in the repaso, and still using verbs you already know from Spanish 1 — querer, poder, dormir, empezar… Today we name a pattern you've been half-hearing all along: in many common verbs, the vowel in the stem (the middle of the verb) shifts — e→ie (quiero) or o→ue (puedo). Teachers call these «boot» verbs (verbos de bota) because of where the change happens. The one idea to hold onto: the stem changes in every form except nosotros and vosotros — and today you'll see exactly why. No new vocabulary; just a pattern getting a name.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to explain the «boot» pattern — that the stem vowel changes in every form except nosotros/vosotros, because those are the two forms where the stress moves off the stem.
2
I will be able to conjugate e→ie verbs (querer, empezar, pensar, preferir, entender, cerrar) and o→ue verbs (poder, dormir, volver, almorzar, contar) across all persons.
3
I will be able to use these verbs to talk about my daily schedule (Empiezo las clases a las ocho, prefiero estudiar por la tarde, no puedo dormir…).
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

Trust your ear first

You've said ¿Quieres ir al cine? and No puedo since Spanish 1 — those little sound shifts in the middle (quer→quier, pod→pued) are already in your mouth. You just haven't had a name for them yet. Before we explain the rule, let your ear vote.

📚 PART 2 · VERBOS QUE YA CONOCES 8 min

The two families — hear the shift

All of these infinitives are review from Spanish 1. Click the 🔊 to hear the infinitive, then the yo form beside it, where the change shows up loudest. Say each pair aloud and listen for the vowel that grows a diphthong.

Familia 1 · e → ie
  • empezarto begin / start  →  yo empiezo
  • quererto want  →  yo quiero
  • pensarto think  →  yo pienso
  • preferirto prefer  →  yo prefiero
  • entenderto understand  →  yo entiendo
  • cerrarto close  →  yo cierro
Familia 2 · o → ue
  • poderto be able / can  →  yo puedo
  • dormirto sleep  →  yo duermo
  • volverto return / come back  →  yo vuelvo
  • almorzarto have lunch  →  yo almuerzo
  • contarto count / to tell  →  yo cuento
⭐ FÍJATE · the shared clue
The ending is completely normal in all of these — empiezo, quiero, puedo take the same -o as any regular yo. The only thing that moved is the vowel in the middle: e→ie or o→ue. That's the whole pattern — a change to the stem, never to the endings you already know.
🧠 PART 3 · EL PATRÓN DE LA BOTA 12 min

Why the shape is a boot

Write the six present-tense forms in two columns — singular on the left, plural on the right. Now shade every form whose stem changes. The shaded cells trace the outline of a boot (una bota): straight down the left side, then a kick out to the bottom-right. The two forms that stay outside the boot — up in the top-right corner — are nosotros and vosotros. They never change.

Boot #1 — querer (e→ie)

Person (singular)quererPerson (plural)querer
yoquiero nosotros/asqueremos
túquieres vosotros/asqueréis
usted / él / ellaquiere ustedes / ellos / ellasquieren

Shaded = stem changes (e→ie). Unshaded = nosotros queremos, vosotros queréis keep the original e. Four forms change; two don't — that's the boot.

Boot #2 — poder (o→ue)

Person (singular)poderPerson (plural)poder
yopuedo nosotros/aspodemos
túpuedes vosotros/aspodéis
usted / él / ellapuede ustedes / ellos / ellaspueden

Same shape, different vowel: o→ue. And notice again — nosotros podemos, vosotros podéis keep the plain o.

🎯 THE WHY · it's about stress, not luck
Say the forms out loud and feel where your voice pushes hardest. In QUIEro, QUIEres, QUIEre, QUIEren, the stress lands on the stem — and a stressed e/o in Spanish naturally breaks into ie/ue. But in queREmos and queRÉIS, the stress jumps to the ending, leaving the stem vowel quiet — so it stays a plain e. The boot isn't a random list to memorize; it's simply “the stem changes wherever the stem is stressed.”

The rest of today's verbs, at a glance

Every verb below follows one of the two boots exactly. Learn the yo form and the whole boot falls into place:

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 9 min

First decide the subject: is it inside the boot (change the stem) or is it nosotros/vosotros (leave it alone)? Commit to an answer, then read the feedback.

✅ TRY IT · querer, yo
“Yo ___ empezar la tarea temprano hoy.” (I want to start the homework early today.)
✅ TRY IT · poder, tú
“¿___ (tú) volver a casa a las cuatro?” (Can you come home at four?)
✅ TRY IT · dormir, ellos
“Mis hermanos ___ ocho horas cada noche.” (My brothers sleep eight hours every night.)
✅ TRY IT · careful — who's the subject?
“Nosotros ___ el almuerzo a mediodía.” (We eat lunch at noon.) Which form fits nosotros?

Your turn: say “I want to sleep, but I can't” in Spanish. Two boot verbs are hiding in there — and one infinitive that stays untouched. Say it out loud first, then reveal.

Quiero dormir, pero no puedo. The two yo verbs are inside the boot, so both change: querer → quiero (e→ie) and poder → puedo (o→ue). But dormir here is an infinitive after quiero — it isn't conjugated, so it keeps its plain o. (If we said “we can't,” it would be no podemos — outside the boot, no change.)
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 4 min

«Hola, me llamo Marisol y vivo en Tegucigalpa, Honduras. Mi horario es intenso. Las clases empiezan a las siete y media, así que prefiero levantarme temprano. A mediodía almuerzo con mis amigas y contamos los planes del fin de semana. Por la tarde vuelvo a casa, pero no puedo descansar todavía: pienso estudiar dos horas más. Mi hermano y yo queremos ver una serie por la noche, pero primero cerramos los libros. Yo casi siempre duermo ocho horas — sin dormir bien, no entiendo nada en clase.»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find the boot verbs that changed (like empiezan, prefiero, almuerzo, vuelvo, puedo, pienso, duermo, entiendo) and the two that didn't: contamos and cerramos — both nosotros. Why do almuerzo and contamos behave differently? Same kind of verb, different subject: only forms inside the boot change.
🌎 CULTURA · HONDURAS
Copán: los mayas que medían el tiempo

This module is about el horario — schedules and telling time — and few peoples in history kept time more precisely than the Maya of Copán, in western Honduras. More than a thousand years ago, Copán's astronomers tracked the sun, moon, and Venus so accurately that their calculation of the solar year was off by only minutes. They carved their observations into towering stone estelas and into the famous Escalinata de los Jeroglíficos — a staircase of more than 2,000 glyphs, the longest Maya text ever found. Their calendar didn't just count days; it organized planting, ceremony, and daily life — a horario written in stone. Today the ruins of Copán are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. (In the full course this pairs with a short clip touring the Copán stelae.)

★ LA VIRTUD · COURAGE

Óscar Romero (El Salvador, 1917–1980), archbishop of San Salvador, quería — wanted — only safety and dignity for the poor and oppressed, and every Sunday he podía have stayed silent but chose not to. He kept speaking from the pulpit even after threats on his life, and was assassinated in 1980 while saying Mass; the Church declared him a saint in 2018. His courage shows that quiero and puedo are not just verbs to conjugate — they are choices we make when something matters.
