📖 PART 3 · A LEER 9 min

Óscar Romero: una voz a la misma hora

Written in the present tense (the “historical present”) so you can read it with the grammar you already have. Watch for the boot verbs — they’re in bold.

«Óscar Romero es el arzobispo de San Salvador, en El Salvador. Su horario tiene un momento muy importante: cada domingo, a la misma hora, habla por la radio. Su homilía empieza por la mañana y toda la comunidad escucha.

Romero quiere la paz y prefiere la verdad al silencio. En la radio defiende a los pobres y a las familias que sufren. La situación es peligrosa, pero él no puede callar; para él, hablar es un deber. Muchas personas vuelven a la radio cada semana porque en su voz encuentran esperanza.

Romero no pide fama ni dinero. Pide justicia. «Cuando doy comida a los pobres», dice, «me llaman santo; cuando pregunto por qué los pobres no tienen comida, me llaman otra cosa.» Domingo tras domingo, a la hora de siempre, vuelve al micrófono. Esa constancia — hacer lo correcto, siempre a la misma hora — es su verdadera valentía.»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find every boot verb in bold and name its change (empieza, quiere, prefiere, defiende = e→ie; puede, vuelven, encuentran, vuelve = o→ue; pide = e→i). Then find the time expression that repeats three times — how does “a la misma hora” carry the whole point of the reading?

All four stem-changes, on one page

Two things the quiz loves to test. (1) The nosotros/as form has no change — empezamos, almorzamos, pedimos, jugamos. (2) Not every schedule verb is a boot verb: terminar, estudiar, tomar, escuchar, hablar are regular — no stem change at all. When in doubt, ask: is this verb on the boot list? If not, keep the stem.

🧠 PART 5 · AUTOEVALUACIÓN · A PUNTO PARA EL QUIZ 14 min

One self-test per stem-change type

Answer each one before you check — that’s how you find out what to review tonight. There are four items, one for each change, plus the nosotros trap. Use Reset to try any of them again.

✅ SELF-TEST 1 · e→ie (empezar)
“La homilía de Romero ___ por la mañana.” (Romero’s homily starts in the morning.)
✅ SELF-TEST 2 · o→ue (dormir) — the nosotros trap
“Mi familia y yo ___ ocho horas cada noche.” (My family and I sleep eight hours each night.) — watch the subject!
✅ SELF-TEST 3 · e→i (pedir)
“En el mercado yo ___ pupusas para el almuerzo.” (At the market I order pupusas for lunch.)
✅ SELF-TEST 4 · u→ue (jugar)
“Después de clase, a las cuatro, mis amigos ___ al fútbol.” (After class, at 4:00, my friends play soccer.)

🌎 CULTURA · EL SALVADOR Y HONDURAS
La comunidad se reúne a la misma hora

In both El Salvador and Honduras, community life still runs on shared, repeated hours. Sunday morning fills the plaza and the church; the mid-day almuerzo pulls families home; and in the evening neighbors gather again — often at a pupusería in El Salvador or a baleada stand in Honduras — to eat and talk over the day. Older traditions of mutual aid live on too: in rural areas a mano vuelta (“a turned hand”) means neighbors take turns helping one another with harvests and house-building, no money exchanged. When Óscar Romero spoke on the radio every Sunday a la misma hora, he was using something these cultures already understood deeply — that a community is held together by faithful, shared moments. Courage, there, is rarely solitary; it is practiced together, on schedule. (In the full course this pairs with a short audio clip of a Salvadoran Sunday market.)

★ LA VIRTUD · COURAGE

Óscar Romero (El Salvador, 1917–1980), archbishop of San Salvador, spent years speaking for the poor and oppressed of his community from the radio pulpit, until his assassination in 1980; the Church declared him a saint in 2018. His courage was rooted in community — he spoke because his people needed a voice. As you prepare for this module's quiz, borrow a little of that same courage: face the grammar that is hardest for you, on purpose, before the test.
