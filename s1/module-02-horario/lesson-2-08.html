★ Describe tu horario, en voz alta — graba la Tarea oral
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Your whole week of clocks and “boot” verbs comes out of your mouth today — in order, out loud, and recorded.
No new grammar and no new words today — that's the whole point of a production day. You already have the time expressions (¿a qué hora?, a las ocho, a mediodía, de la mañana…) and the stem-changing “boot” verbs (empiezo, almuerzo, vuelvo, prefiero, juego, duermo). Now you'll assemble them into a smooth, spoken description of your own daily schedule, rehearse the diphthong sounds, and record your Tarea oral (20 points, presentational rubric). Think of today as the dress rehearsal and the performance.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to narrate my daily schedule in order, pairing each activity with a clock time (a las ocho, a mediodía, a las tres de la tarde).
2
I will be able to use stem-changing “boot” verbs correctly in the yo form (empiezo, almuerzo, prefiero, vuelvo, juego) and pronounce their diphthongs cleanly.
3
I will be able to draft, rehearse, and record my Tarea oral so it meets all three rubric criteria: task completion, accuracy, and pronunciation/fluency.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

Warm up your voice before you record

Athletes stretch; speakers warm up their mouth. Say this chain aloud three times, a little faster each time, and lean into the double-vowel sounds: Por la mañana empiezo, a mediodía almuerzo, por la tarde vuelvo. Notice the ie and ue each glide as one sound — that's the “boot” change you've practiced all week, and it's today's pronunciation focus.

📚 PART 2 · REPASO EXPRÉS 8 min

Your toolkit, one more time (nothing new)

These are the pieces you'll snap together for your recording — all from this module. Click the 🔊 and say each one back so it's fresh in your mouth. First, the stem-changing verbs in the yo form (the shape you'll use most):

  • empiezoI begin (empezar, e→ie)
  • prefieroI prefer (preferir, e→ie)
  • quieroI want (querer, e→ie)
  • almuerzoI have lunch (almorzar, o→ue)
  • vuelvoI return / go back (volver, o→ue)
  • duermoI sleep (dormir, o→ue)
  • pidoI ask for / order (pedir, e→i)
  • juegoI play (jugar, u→ue)

And the time expressions that pin each activity to the clock:

  • ¿a qué hora?at what time?
  • a las ochoat eight o'clock
  • y mediahalf past (:30)
  • de la mañanain the morning (a.m.)
  • a mediodíaat noon
  • por la tardein the afternoon
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +4 min
Say your real morning aloud, pinning three verbs to three times: Empiezo las clases a las…, almuerzo a…, vuelvo a casa a… Locking the order and the clock now means your recording won't stall later.
🧠 PART 3 · FONÉTICA: EL DIPTONGO + LA ESTRUCTURA 7 min

Say the boot vowels as one gliding sound

When a boot verb changes its stem, the two vowels (ie, ue) form a diphthong — a single, smooth glide, not two chopped syllables. The stress lands right on that glide. Click each 🔊 and copy the sound exactly:

  • empiezae→ie · say em⁠-PIE⁠-za, glide the ie
  • almuerzao→ue · say al⁠-MUER⁠-za, one sound
  • prefieree→ie · the ie carries the stress

Tip: don't split it into …i…e… or …u…e…. Let the vowels run together. Comprehensible and steady beats fast and choppy on the rubric.

The recipe for your narration

You're not inventing anything new — you're following a frame. Each sentence stacks the same three pieces you already know: a moment of the day, a clock time, and a boot verb.

Momento del díaLa horaVerbo con cambio (yo)
Por la mañana,a las ochoempiezo las clases.
A mediodía,a las docealmuerzo en la cafetería.
Por la tarde,a las tresvuelvo a casa y juego al fútbol.
Por la tarde,—prefiero hacer la tarea temprano.
Por la noche,a las diezduermo unas ocho horas.

Sequencers help too: primero, luego, después, finalmente. And remember the boot only changes in yo/tú/él/ellos — the nosotros form keeps the original vowel (empezamos, almorzamos). For your recording you'll mostly speak in yo, so the boot is always on.

🧠 PART 4 · PREPARA TU GRABACIÓN 11 min

Fix the small things now, not on tape

Three quick checks on the exact moves your recording needs. Then draft your own out loud and compare it to a model.

✅ TRY IT · boot the verb
“Primero, mi clase de matemáticas ___ a las ocho.” (begins — empezar, e→ie)
✅ TRY IT · the yo form
“A mediodía yo ___ en la cafetería con mis amigos.” (have lunch — almorzar, o→ue)
✅ TRY IT · tell the time
It's 2:30 in the afternoon and you say when you get home. Which is correct?

Now the real thing: say four sentences of your own schedule out loud, in order, each with a boot verb and a clock time. Say it first — then reveal a full model to check yourself against.

Por la mañana empiezo las clases a las ocho. A mediodía almuerzo en casa a las doce y media. Por la tarde vuelvo del gimnasio a las cuatro y juego videojuegos un rato. Finalmente, por la noche prefiero leer y duermo a las diez y media.
Notice the frame: momento del día → hora → boot verb. Yours will use your real times and activities — that's exactly what “Task Completion” on the rubric rewards.

Before you record, run your draft past this quick self-check. Say your draft once more, then open the checklist and tick each item in your head.

Does your draft have…
  • □ four or more boot verbs in the yo form (empiezo, almuerzo, vuelvo, prefiero, juego, duermo, pido…)?
  • □ three or more clock times (a las ocho, a mediodía, a las tres de la tarde)?
  • □ at least two sequencers (primero, luego, después, finalmente)?
  • □ clean diphthongs — are you gliding ie and ue as one sound?
Any box you can't tick is a two-minute fix now — much easier than noticing it after you record.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER · EL MODELO 7 min

Read the full model below, then press 🔊 to hear it spoken at a natural pace — this is the sound you're aiming for. Read along twice, matching the diphthongs.

«¡Hola! Me llamo Mateo y vivo en San Salvador. Entre semana mi horario empieza temprano. Por la mañana las clases empiezan a las siete y media; primero tengo matemáticas y luego ciencias. A mediodía almuerzo en la cafetería y casi siempre pido pupusas con mi mejor amigo. Por la tarde vuelvo a casa a las tres y juego al fútbol un rato. Después prefiero hacer la tarea, porque por la noche quiero descansar. Finalmente, duermo unas ocho horas. ¡Así es un día normal en mi horario!»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Count them: how many boot verbs can you find in Mateo's paragraph? (Eight: empieza, empiezan, almuerzo, pido, vuelvo, juego, prefiero, quiero, duermo — even more!) And how many clock times? That's a great target for your own recording — five or six sentences with the clock woven through.
🎤 TAREA ORAL · MÓDULO 2 · 20 PUNTOS · graba en Canvas
«Un día en mi horario»

Record a 45–60 second spoken description of your own daily schedule, using Canvas media recording. This is presentational speaking — you talking to the camera, no partner needed. To earn full marks, include all of these:

  • at least four stem-changing boot verbs in the yo form (empiezo, almuerzo, vuelvo, prefiero, juego, duermo, pido…),
  • at least three clock times (a las ocho, a mediodía, a las tres de la tarde…),
  • the sequencers in order — primero, luego, después, finalmente,
  • at least one momento del día (por la mañana / por la tarde / por la noche).

Speak, don't read word-for-word — glance at your notes, then look up and talk. You may re-record as many times as you like; submit your best take.

🌎 CULTURA · EL SALVADOR / HONDURAS
Dos horarios en un solo día: la doble jornada

In many public schools across El Salvador and Honduras, the same building runs two school days. One group of students attends the turno matutino (morning shift, roughly 7:00 a.m. to noon) and a different group attends the turno vespertino (afternoon shift, about 1:00 to 6:00 p.m.). This doble jornada lets one campus serve far more children than seats would otherwise allow. So when a Salvadoran teen says «mis clases empiezan a la una», that early-afternoon start isn't a typo — it's their shift. As you record tu horario today, notice how your own a qué hora depends on where in the world you study. (In the full course this pairs with a side-by-side of a morning-shift and afternoon-shift schedule.)

★ LA VIRTUD · COURAGE

Óscar Romero (El Salvador, 1917–1980), archbishop of San Salvador and now a canonized saint, used his voice publicly every week to defend the poor and oppressed, even after threats made the danger clear — he was assassinated in 1980 while saying Mass. That same courage, on a much smaller scale, is what it takes to press record and speak Spanish out loud. Do it anyway, imperfectly and clearly, the way Romero kept speaking anyway.
