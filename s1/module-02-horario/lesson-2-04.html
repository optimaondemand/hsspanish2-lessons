★ Mi horario de verdad — los verbos de bota en acción
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The last three days you met the boot verbs. Today you actually use them — to describe your own real school day.
Nothing new today — no new grammar and no new words. Instead we take the stem-changing verbos de bota from Lessons 2.01–2.03 and put them to work on your actual horario: when your classes empiezan and terminan, which subject you prefieres, what you puedes do after school, when you almuerzas and how long you duermes. We stay with the two changes you already know — e→ie and o→ue — and then read how a student in Honduras describes his day.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use e→ie boot verbs (empezar, querer, preferir) to say when things start and which subject I prefer.
2
I will be able to use o→ue boot verbs (poder, almorzar, dormir) to describe my real daily schedule with time expressions.
3
I will be able to read a Honduran student's school-day paragraph and find the boot verbs and time expressions inside it.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Warm the boot back up

Quick memory jog before we use them. Remember the picture: in a boot verb the stem changes in every form except nosotros — those four changed forms make the shape of a boot. Say each one out loud: empezar → empiezo, querer → quiero, poder → puedo, almorzar → almuerzo, dormir → duermo. Two changes only today — e→ie and o→ue.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Think about your own school day. What time does your first class usually start?

For each word, whisper a sentence that joins it to a boot verb: Mi horario empieza a las siete. Prefiero las ciencias. Almuerzo en el recreo. That pairing — schedule word + boot verb + a time — is exactly what today is about.
🧠 PART 3 · LOS VERBOS DE BOTA EN TU HORARIO 8 min

The same boot verbs — now aimed at your day

This isn't a new rule — it's the rule from Lessons 2.01–2.03, doing real work. Here are the six boot verbs you already learned, each turned into a sentence about a school day. Read the last column out loud.

InfinitivoCambioyoEn tu horario
empezare→ieempiezoLa clase de inglés empieza a las ocho.
querere→iequieroYo quiero estudiar ciencias.
preferire→ieprefieroYo prefiero la clase de historia.
podero→uepuedoNo puedo descansar hasta las tres.
almorzaro→uealmuerzoYo almuerzo a la una en el recreo.
dormiro→ueduermoYo duermo ocho horas cada noche.

Two reminders. (1) The stem changes in every form except nosotros — nosotros empezamos, almorzamos, dormimos keep the plain stem (the "sole" of the boot). (2) Not every schedule verb is a boot verb: terminar is regular — la clase termina a la una y media, no change. Watch for that contrast in the reading.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 12 min

Pick the boot form your schedule needs

✅ TRY IT · empezar (e→ie)
“Mi primera clase ___ a las siete de la mañana.” (My first class starts at 7:00 a.m.)
✅ TRY IT · poder (o→ue)
“Después del colegio yo no ___ salir; tengo mucha tarea.” (After school I can't go out; I have a lot of homework.)
✅ TRY IT · preferir (e→ie)
“Yo ___ la clase de ciencias porque hacemos experimentos.” (I prefer science class because we do experiments.)

Your turn: how would you say “I eat lunch at noon and I sleep eight hours”? Say it out loud first, then reveal a model.

Almuerzo a las doce y duermo ocho horas. Two o→ue boot verbs working together: almuerzo (from almorzar) and duermo (from dormir). Your own answer just needs your real lunchtime and hours of sleep.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 6 min

El horario de Diego

«Me llamo Diego y vivo en Tegucigalpa, la capital de Honduras. Mi horario es muy ocupado. Las clases empiezan a las siete de la mañana y terminan a la una y media de la tarde. Mi primera materia son las matemáticas, pero yo prefiero las ciencias. A las diez tenemos el recreo, y a esa hora yo almuerzo una baleada con mis amigos. Después del colegio no puedo descansar mucho, porque quiero mejorar mi inglés y tomo una clase en línea a las cuatro. Por la noche duermo ocho horas, porque al día siguiente todo empieza otra vez muy temprano.»

✅ COMPRENSIÓN · ¿a qué hora?
According to the text, at what time do Diego's classes start?
✅ COMPRENSIÓN · la materia
Which subject does Diego say he prefers?
⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find all six boot verbs in Diego's text (empezar appears twice). One schedule verb, terminar, is not a boot verb — can you spot it and see that its stem doesn't change? Which part of Diego's day is most like yours, and which is different?
🌎 CULTURA · HONDURAS
Dos turnos y una baleada

In Honduras, many public schools run on a doble jornada — a morning shift (jornada matutina) and an afternoon shift (jornada vespertina) — so that one building can serve twice as many students. That's why an horario matters so much: Diego's classes empiezan at 7:00 and terminan at 1:30 precisely because the afternoon group arrives right after. At the mid-morning recreo, the classic snack is a baleada: a thick flour tortilla folded over refried beans, cheese, and cream — sold from stands outside almost every colegio and considered a national dish of Honduras. (In the full course this pairs with a short photo essay of a Tegucigalpa school day.)

★ LA VIRTUD · COURAGE

Óscar Romero (El Salvador, 1917–1980), archbishop of San Salvador, prefería to speak the truth even though he entendía exactly how dangerous it was. He kept speaking anyway, and was assassinated in 1980 while saying Mass; in 2018 the Church declared him a saint. His life shows what those same stem-changing verbs mean in practice — you prefieres what is right, you entiendes the risk, and you act anyway.
