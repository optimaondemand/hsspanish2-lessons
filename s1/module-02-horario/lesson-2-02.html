★ Mi horario escolar — Las materias y el día
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Every school day already has a shape — today you get the Spanish words to describe it.
On Day 1 you refreshed how to tell time — ¿qué hora es? and a la(s)… Today you add the other half of a schedule: the subjects you study (las materias) and the little words that put a day in order (primero, luego, después, finalmente). This is a vocabulary day — no new grammar. You'll lean on present-tense verbs you already own, especially tener (tengo la clase de…), so you can spend your energy on the words and on building a real horario.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name the main school subjects (las materias) in Spanish and say which classes I have with tengo la clase de…
2
I will be able to put the parts of my school day in order using primero, luego, después, finalmente, plus el recreo and el almuerzo.
3
I will be able to read a simple schedule and build my own horario, slotting in the times I learned on Day 1.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Your day, in Spanish already

Picture the last school day you had. You went from one class to the next in a certain order, you had a break, you ate. Spanish has one word for that whole map of your day: el horario — the schedule. Today you'll fill it in.

📚 PART 2 · LAS MATERIAS Y EL DÍA ESCOLAR 12 min

Listen, then say each one back

Click the 🔊 and repeat aloud. First, the school subjects — las materias (also called las asignaturas). Notice each one carries its article (el / la / las); learn the article with the word.

  • las matemáticasmath (always plural)
  • las cienciasscience
  • la historiahistory
  • el arteart
  • la educación físicaphysical education (P.E.)
  • la músicamusic
  • la literaturaliterature
  • el idiomalanguage (e.g. el español, el inglés)

Next, the words that frame the school day — el día escolar:

  • el horarioschedule
  • la materia(school) subject
  • la asignaturasubject (synonym of materia)
  • la clase de…the … class (la clase de arte)
  • el recreorecess / break
  • el almuerzolunch

And the connectors that put your day in order — para poner el día en orden:

  • primerofirst
  • luegothen / next
  • despuésafter / afterward
  • finalmentefinally
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Walk through your own subjects in the order you have them, out loud: Primero, la clase de… luego, la clase de… Don't worry about times yet — just chain the subjects with the four connectors until the sequence feels natural.
🧠 PART 3 · REPASO · TENER + LA CLASE DE… 8 min

Nothing new — just a verb you already have

To say what classes you have, you reach for tener — the same verb you reviewed in Module 0. Here it is again, so you can pair it with today's vocabulary:

SubjecttenerExample with a class
yotengoYo tengo la clase de historia a las nueve.
tútienes¿Qué clase tienes primero?
usted / él / ellatieneElla tiene educación física después del recreo.
nosotros/astenemosNosotros tenemos el almuerzo al mediodía.
ustedes / ellos / ellastienenEllos tienen la clase de música finalmente.

The pattern is simple: tener + la clase de + subject + a la(s) + time (from Day 1). Example: Tengo la clase de ciencias a las diez y media. You'll build a whole schedule this way — and the boot-verb stem changes that give this module its name arrive tomorrow, in 2.03.

📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

«¡Hola! Me llamo Sofía y estudio en San Salvador, El Salvador. Mi horario es muy completo. Primero, a las siete y media, tengo la clase de matemáticas. Luego tengo ciencias e historia. A las diez tenemos el recreo — mi parte favorita. Después del recreo tengo la clase de arte y educación física. Al mediodía es el almuerzo; normalmente como pupusas con mis amigos. Finalmente, por la tarde, tengo la clase de idiomas: estudio inglés. ¡Me gusta mucho mi horario!»

🌎 CULTURA · HONDURAS Y EL SALVADOR
Dos jornadas, un horario

In many public schools across Honduras and El Salvador, the building serves two sets of students a day — a doble jornada. One group attends the jornada matutina (morning, roughly 7:00 a.m. to noon) and another the jornada vespertina (afternoon). Because the space is shared, a student's whole horario — classes, recreo, and almuerzo — is packed into a single half-day. At break you might find friends sharing baleadas in Honduras or pupusas in El Salvador. So when you read a Central American schedule, the times you learned on Day 1 also tell you which jornada a student belongs to. (In the full course this pairs with a real sample schedule to compare with your own.)

★ LA VIRTUD · COURAGE

Óscar Romero (El Salvador, 1917–1980), archbishop of San Salvador, kept his own weekly horario: a Sunday radio homily defending the poor and oppressed, broadcast even after threats made clear the danger. He was assassinated in 1980 while saying Mass and was declared a saint in 2018. As you build your own horario de clases this week, remember that his courage was really just keeping a difficult appointment, again and again.
