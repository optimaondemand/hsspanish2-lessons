📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Nothing new today — just everything you've built this module, working together.
This is an integration day: no new verbs, no new vocabulary. You already have the three pieces — how to tell time (son las ocho, a las doce, y media, en punto), the schedule words (el horario, la clase, la materia, el almuerzo, temprano, tarde), and the stem-changing “boot” verbs that describe what happens and when (empezar, almorzar, volver, dormir, querer, preferir, poder, pedir, jugar). Today you weave all three into one connected description of your day. This also matters because graded work opens today: your first two module grades — the Vocabulary Check and Lengua en acción — go live in Canvas, and this lesson models both before you attempt them.

🧠 PART 3 · LOS VERBOS “BOTA”, EN FOCO 8 min

Two habits to lock in

Nothing new — just the two habits that make these verbs sound right. Habit one: the stem changes in every form except nosotros. If you draw a line around the forms that change, they make the shape of a boot (la bota) — and nosotros sits in the empty gap at the top. Here is empezar (e→ie):

Subjectempezar (e→ie)Example with a time
yoempiezoYo empiezo a las ocho.
túempiezas¿A qué hora empiezas?
usted / él / ellaempiezaLa clase empieza a las nueve.
nosotros/asempezamos (no change!)Nosotros empezamos temprano.
ustedes / ellos / ellasempiezanLas clases empiezan a las ocho.

Habit two: there are four kinds of change — but the boot shape is the same for all of them. Learn the yo form of each and you have the pattern:

ChangeVerbs in this moduleyo form
e→ieempezar, querer, preferirempiezo, quiero, prefiero
o→uealmorzar, poder, volver, dormiralmuerzo, puedo, vuelvo, duermo
e→ipedir, servir, repetirpido, sirvo, repito
u→uejugar (the only one)juego

Rule of thumb for this module: if the subject is yo, tú, él/ella, or ellos/ellas, change the stem; if it's nosotros, leave it alone — almuerzo but almorzamos, vuelvo but volvemos.

Now the whole thing — a model of Lengua en acción

The graded task asks for 4–5 connected sentences about your horario, in order, each pinned to a time and using at least three boot verbs. Draft yours out loud or on paper first — then reveal a model to compare.

Mi día empieza temprano. Mi primera clase empieza a las ocho de la mañana y prefiero la clase de historia porque quiero aprender mucho. A las doce almuerzo con mis amigos en la cafetería. Después de las clases vuelvo a casa a las tres y a veces juego al fútbol. Por la noche siempre duermo ocho horas.

Notice the pattern the graders look for: a time on nearly every action (a las ocho, a las doce, a las tres), stem-changing boot verbs used correctly (empieza, prefiero, quiero, almuerzo, vuelvo, juego, duermo), and connectors holding it in order (después, y, por la noche). Yours will use your own times and subjects.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

«Me llamo Diego y vivo en Tegucigalpa, la capital de Honduras. De lunes a viernes mi día empieza a las seis. Mi primera clase empieza a las siete en punto y termina a la una. Prefiero la clase de matemáticas, pero también quiero estudiar música. A las doce almuerzo — casi siempre pido baleadas, mi comida favorita. Después vuelvo a casa a las dos y descanso un rato. A veces juego al fútbol con mis primos, y por la noche duermo ocho horas porque el día siguiente empieza temprano otra vez.»

🌎 CULTURA · HONDURAS / EL SALVADOR
Dos horarios en un mismo edificio — la doble jornada

In much of Honduras and El Salvador, a single school building serves two full sets of students on the same day. The turno matutino (morning shift) meets from roughly 7:00 a.m. to noon; then the turno vespertino (afternoon shift) uses the same classrooms from about 1:00 to 6:00 p.m. So when a Salvadoran student says mis clases empiezan a la una, that time tells you which shift is theirs. Between shifts comes lunch — in El Salvador that often means pupusas, thick corn tortillas filled with cheese, beans, or pork, the national dish; in Honduras, Diego's baleadas, a folded flour tortilla with beans, cream, and cheese. A schedule, it turns out, is also a menu and a rhythm of a whole community. (In the full course this pairs with an authentic school-schedule reading inside the Vocabulary Check.)

★ LA VIRTUD · COURAGE

Óscar Romero (El Salvador, 1917–1980), archbishop of San Salvador, filled his own horario with one fixed, dangerous appointment: the Sunday radio homily where he spoke for the poor and oppressed, week after week, until his assassination in 1980. He was declared a saint in 2018. As you put together your complete día in Spanish and open your first graded work this week, remember that his courage was built the same way yours will be — one scheduled, honest effort at a time.
