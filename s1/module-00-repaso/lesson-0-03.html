★ El presente: verbos en -ar
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
One ending pattern unlocks hundreds of verbs — and you already know most of the words.
Yesterday you reactivated your classroom and technology words; today we turn on the engine that makes them into sentences. The biggest family of Spanish verbs ends in -ar (hablar, estudiar, trabajar…), and every regular one follows the same recipe: drop the -ar, add an ending that matches who is doing the action. You met this in Spanish 1 — this is a repaso (spiral review), so nothing here is brand new. By the end you'll conjugate any regular -ar verb on sight.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to conjugate a regular -ar verb in all six present-tense forms by dropping -ar and adding -o, -as, -a, -amos, -áis, -an.
2
I will be able to pick the ending that matches a given subject (yo, tú, ella, nosotros, ellos).
3
I will be able to say what I and other people do every day using familiar -ar verbs.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

You're already conjugating

Read this aloud: Yo hablo español. Tú hablas inglés. Ella habla francés. Same verb, three different endings — and you understood every one without translating. Those endings are the whole lesson. They tell you who is doing the action, so Spanish often doesn't even need the word “yo” or “tú” — the ending already says it.

🧠 PART 3 · LA RECETA DE LOS VERBOS -AR 9 min

Drop -ar, add the ending

Take the infinitive (hablar), remove the -ar to get the stem (habl-), and add the ending that matches the subject. Here is the full pattern on hablar (to speak):

SubjectEndinghablar → habl-
yo-ohablo — I speak
tú-ashablas — you speak
usted / él / ella-ahabla — you (formal) / he / she speaks
nosotros/as-amoshablamos — we speak
vosotros/as (España)-áishabláis — you all speak
ustedes / ellos / ellas-anhablan — you all / they speak

Because the ending already names the subject, Spanish usually drops yo, tú, nosotros and just says hablo, hablas, hablamos. The words in parentheses are only for emphasis or clarity.

Same recipe, any regular -ar verb

The endings never change — only the stem does. Watch three more verbs snap into the same slots:

Subjectestudiartrabajarescuchar
yoestudiotrabajoescucho
túestudiastrabajasescuchas
ellaestudiatrabajaescucha
nosotros/asestudiamostrabajamosescuchamos
ellos/ellasestudiantrabajanescuchan

Learn the six endings once and you can conjugate every regular -ar verb — and that's the largest verb family in Spanish.

✅ TRY IT · who is doing it?

Your turn: how would you say “She listens to music and I use the computer”? Say it out loud first, then reveal a model.

Ella escucha música y yo uso la computadora. Two different subjects, two different endings: ella → escucha, and yo → uso. Same recipe both times.

🌎 CULTURA · EL MUNDO HISPANO
Todo el mundo estudia español

You are not studying alone. According to Spain's Instituto Cervantes, more than 24 million people around the world are learning Spanish as a foreign language right now — from Brazil to Japan to the United States, where Spanish is by far the most-studied language in schools. Every one of them conjugates the exact verb you drilled today: estudiar. When you say estudio español, you join a Spanish-speaking world of nearly 600 million people. ★ LA VIRTUD · LAS SIETE VIRTUDES

Las siete virtudes aren't only about big moments — most days they show up as perseverancia: saying hablo, hablas, habla… again and again until the pattern is automatic. Sticking with a regular verb chart when it feels repetitive is a quiet rehearsal of autogobierno, choosing the steady work over the easy shortcut. That is the habit this whole semester is built to strengthen.
