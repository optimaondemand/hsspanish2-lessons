Lesson 0.02
★ La clase y la tecnología — Your tools, back in Spanish
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The room you learn in has a name for everything — and today you get those names back.
Yesterday you switched your Spanish back on with greetings. Today we refresh the words for the things around you when you study — both the classic classroom objects (el cuaderno, la mochila) and the technology you actually reach for (la computadora portátil, los audífonos, la contraseña). No new grammar today: we're stocking your vocabulary shelf and re-using verbs you already know — hay, usar, necesitar — to talk about what's in the room. Naming your world in Spanish is the first step to living part of your day in it.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name common classroom objects and technology tools in Spanish with the correct article (el / la).
2
I will be able to use familiar verbs (hay, usar, necesitar) to say what's in the room and what I use to study.
3
I will be able to describe the tools I reach for most, combining classroom and technology words in short present-tense sentences.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Look up from the screen

Take ten seconds and look around wherever you're studying right now. How many of those objects can you already name in Spanish? Probably more than you'd guess — el libro, la silla, el teléfono… Today we fill the gaps and add the words for the devices Spanish 1 barely touched.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
When you study, which tool do you reach for the most?

⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Sort the sixteen words into two piles out loud: la clase (classroom objects) and la tecnología (devices). Then say each one with its article and point to it if it's near you. Saying la mochila, el teclado — article and all — is how the gender sticks.
🧠 PART 3 · PON LAS PALABRAS A TRABAJAR 7 min

Three verbs you already own

You don't need any new grammar to start using today's words — just three verbs from Spanish 1. Slot your new nouns into these familiar frames:

Frame (review)MeaningExample with today's words
hay + nounthere is / there areEn la clase hay una pizarra y un reloj.
uso (usar)I useYo uso la computadora portátil todos los días.
necesito (necesitar)I needPara la clase necesito un cuaderno y un bolígrafo.

A quick reminder from Spanish 1: hay never changes — it means both "there is" and "there are." And the article shifts with number: un cuaderno → unos cuadernos, la silla → las sillas. That's all the machinery you need for this lesson — the real grammar refresh starts tomorrow.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 10 min
✅ TRY IT · pick the right tool
You have to type a long essay for class. Which tool do you use most?
✅ TRY IT · complete the sentence
“Para entrar en la plataforma del colegio, necesito mi ___.” (To log in to the school platform, I need my ___.)
✅ TRY IT · el o la?
Which article goes with the word for “screen”? “___ pantalla”

Your turn: how would you say “There are two computers in the classroom”? Say it out loud first, then reveal a model.

Hay dos computadoras en la clase. Hay covers "there are"; computadora becomes computadoras for the plural. Swap in your own numbers and objects — the frame stays the same.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

«Esta es mi mesa de estudio. En mi mochila siempre hay un cuaderno, un bolígrafo y un lápiz. Sobre el escritorio tengo mi computadora portátil y una tableta. Para las tareas, uso mucho el teclado y la pantalla grande. Cuando escucho música o un pódcast en español, necesito mis audífonos. Y para entrar en la plataforma del colegio, siempre necesito mi contraseña. Me gusta la tecnología, pero el cuaderno y el bolígrafo nunca fallan.»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Make two lists from the paragraph: the classroom words and the technology words. Then find the three review verbs doing the work — hay, uso, necesito. Which one tells you what she has? Which tells you what she uses?
🌎 CULTURA · EL MUNDO HISPANO
Un idioma muy conectado

Spanish is one of the most-used languages on the internet — roughly the third, after English and Mandarin, by number of users online. But here's the twist for the words you just learned: technology vocabulary shifts from country to country. In much of Latin America a computer is la computadora; in Spain it's el ordenador. Your phone is el celular across the Americas but el móvil in Spain, and the little clicker is el ratón in some places and el mouse in others. Same language, many keyboards — part of what makes 21 countries feel like one big, noisy group chat. (In the full course this pairs with a short clip comparing tech words across regions.)

★ LA VIRTUD · LAS SIETE VIRTUDES

Among las siete virtudes that anchor Optima's character thread this year, today's habit is responsabilidad: taking care of your own mochila, computadora portátil, and cuaderno before class starts. A student who names their tools and keeps them in order is practicing autogobierno too — managing yourself so the real work goes smoothly. Small, ordinary care like this is exactly how the seven virtues get practiced, one class period at a time.
