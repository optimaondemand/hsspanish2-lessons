Lesson 0.06
★ Tener y las expresiones con tener
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Sometimes Spanish has what English simply is.
You already know tener means to have — tengo un perro, tienes dos clases. Today we spiral it back and add its most useful trick. In a whole family of everyday phrases, Spanish uses tener + a noun exactly where English uses “to be” + an adjective: you don't say “I am hungry” — you say “I have hunger” (tengo hambre). We'll also refresh tener que + infinitivo for saying what you have to do. No brand-new grammar — just tener, put to work.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to conjugate tener in the present for every subject (tengo, tienes, tiene, tenemos, tienen).
2
I will be able to use tener expressions (tener hambre, sed, frío, sueño, … años) where English would use “to be.”
3
I will be able to say what I — and others — have to do, using tener que + infinitivo.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

“I have hunger”?

Here's a difference worth noticing. In English you say you are hungry, are cold, are 16 years old. Spanish sees these as things you have: tengo hambre (I have hunger), tengo frío (I have cold), tengo dieciséis años (I have sixteen years). Same idea, different verb. Once you expect tener here, a dozen everyday phrases fall into place at once.

📚 PART 2 · LAS EXPRESIONES CON TENER 11 min

Listen, then say each one back

Every phrase below is tener + a noun — and every one translates into English with “to be.” Click the 🔊 and repeat each aloud. Say them with feeling; these are the phrases you'll reach for daily.

  • tenerto have (the verb itself)
  • tener hambreto be hungry (lit. to have hunger)
  • tener sedto be thirsty
  • tener fríoto be cold
  • tener calorto be hot / warm
  • tener sueñoto be sleepy
  • tener miedoto be afraid
  • tener prisato be in a hurry
  • tener razónto be right
  • tener … añosto be … years old
  • tener ganas deto feel like (doing something)
  • tener queto have to (do something)
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
To say very hungry, thirsty, cold… use mucho/mucha — and it must agree with the noun: tengo mucha hambre (hambre is feminine), but tengo mucho frío (frío is masculine). For age it's muchos: mi abuelo tiene muchos años.
🧠 PART 3 · EL VERBO TENER (REPASO) 9 min

Conjugate it once, use it everywhere

Every phrase from Part 2 rides on the conjugated verb. Tener is a familiar friend from Spanish 1: it has an irregular yo form (tengo) and an e→ie stem change in the “boot” forms. Here it is, warm and ready:

SubjecttenerExample
yotengoYo tengo quince años.
tútienes¿Tienes hambre?
usted / él / ellatieneElla tiene prisa.
nosotros/astenemosNosotros tenemos sed.
ustedes / ellos / ellastienenEllos tienen sueño.

Notice the boot: tengo, tienes, tiene … tienen change the stem to tie-, but tenemos keeps te-. Same pattern you saw with other e→ie verbs.

Tener que + infinitivo — what you have to do

To say something is an obligation, use tener que followed by a verb in the infinitive (the plain -ar / -er / -ir form — you don't conjugate the second verb):

FormulaExampleEnglish
tengo que + infinitivoTengo que estudiar.I have to study.
tienes que + infinitivoTienes que comer algo.You have to eat something.
tenemos que + infinitivoTenemos que salir ya.We have to leave now.

Only tener changes with the subject; the action verb stays in its plain infinitive form. Tener ganas de works the same way: Tengo ganas de viajar (I feel like traveling).

Tengo mucha hambre y tengo que comer ahora. “I'm hungry” → tengo hambre (with mucha for “really,” agreeing with feminine hambre); “I have to eat” → tengo que + the infinitive comer.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 4 min

«Hola, me llamo Valentina y tengo quince años. Vivo en Quito, Ecuador. Los lunes siempre tengo prisa por la mañana porque mi colegio empieza temprano. Cuando llego a casa, tengo mucha hambre y como con mi familia. Mi hermano pequeño solo tiene seis años y, por la noche, siempre tiene sueño muy pronto. Yo tengo que estudiar un poco más, pero este fin de semana tengo ganas de descansar. ¡Creo que tengo razón — lo necesito!»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find all seven tener phrases in the paragraph. How old is Valentina, and how old is her brother? Which sentence tells you what she has to do, and which tells you what she feels like doing?
🌎 CULTURA · EL MUNDO HISPANO
Tener quince años

In much of the Spanish-speaking world, turning fifteen is a milestone. The quinceañera — the celebration for a girl who tiene quince años — marks the passage from childhood toward adulthood, and is honored from Mexico to Ecuador to the Mexican-American communities of the United States. A traditional fiesta de quince can include a special Mass, a formal dress, a waltz with family, and the symbolic última muñeca (last doll). Notice that the whole idea lives inside today's grammar: to say your age in Spanish is literally to say how many years you have. (In the full course this pairs with a short clip and a reflection on coming-of-age traditions across the Hispanic world.)

★ LA VIRTUD · LAS SIETE VIRTUDES

Tener que is the grammar of obligation — and living up to your obligations is exactly responsabilidad, one of las siete virtudes that run through this whole course. Saying tengo que estudiar is easy; actually studying is the virtue. Notice today how naming a responsibility out loud, in Spanish or English, is often the first step toward keeping it.
