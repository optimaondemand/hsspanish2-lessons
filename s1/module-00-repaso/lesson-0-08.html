Lesson 0.08
★ Describe tu mundo — pon todo junto
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You've spent a week gathering the tools — today you use all of them at once to talk about your own life.
Nothing new today — that's the point. This is a production lesson: you'll combine the whole present-tense toolkit from this module — ser, estar, tener, gustar, and your regular -ar / -er / -ir verbs — to describe yourself and your school in several connected sentences. By the end you'll have a spoken draft ready to shape into your Tarea oral: “Describe tu mundo.”
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to combine ser, tener, gustar, and present-tense verbs to describe myself in several connected sentences.
2
I will be able to describe my school and a favorite class using classroom and technology vocabulary from this module.
3
I will be able to organize a 60–90 second spoken draft with connectors (primero, también, por eso) for my Tarea oral.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

You already have everything you need

Think back over this module. You can say who you are (ser), how you feel and where things are (estar), what you have and how old you are (tener), and what you like (gustar) — plus dozens of everyday verbs. Today you don't collect any new tools; you assemble them. Read this aloud and notice how many tools are working at once: Me llamo Sofía, tengo dieciséis años y soy de Texas. Estudio en una escuela en línea y me gusta mucho la clase de arte.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
When you describe “tu mundo,” which part are you most eager to talk about?
📚 PART 2 · PALABRAS QUE CONECTAN 7 min

The glue that turns sentences into a paragraph

A good spoken description isn't a list — it flows. These little connector words stitch your sentences together so your Tarea oral sounds like a person talking, not a vocabulary quiz. Click the 🔊 and repeat each one aloud.

  • primerofirst
  • luegothen / next
  • tambiénalso / too
  • ademásbesides / what's more
  • porquebecause
  • por esothat's why / so
  • por ejemplofor example
  • favorito / favoritafavorite
  • en mi opiniónin my opinion
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +4 min
Take two sentences you already know — Soy de Miami. and Me gusta el fútbol. — and join them with a connector: Soy de Miami y también me gusta el fútbol. Try three more pairs. Connectors are the fastest way to sound more fluent without learning anything new.
🧠 PART 3 · TU CAJA DE HERRAMIENTAS 8 min

The four verbs that carry a self-description

This is a review, not a new rule. Almost any sentence about “your world” runs on one of these four verbs. Pick the one that matches the job you need done:

VerbIts jobYour sentence (yo)
serwho/what you are · origin · lasting traitsSoy estudiante. Soy de Orlando. Soy trabajador.
estarhow you feel · where things areEstoy contento hoy. Mi escuela está en la ciudad.
tenerwhat you have · your ageTengo dieciséis años. Tengo dos clases de ciencias.
gustarwhat you likeMe gusta la música. Me gustan las matemáticas.

Remember the gustar agreement: one thing → me gusta; more than one → me gustan. And your regular verbs still do the everyday work: estudio, aprendo, escribo, uso la computadora.

A description that touches all four — who you are, how you feel, what you have, what you like — already sounds full and complete. That's your target for today.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO Y ARMA TU BORRADOR 12 min

Grab the right tool, then build your draft

✅ TRY IT · choose the verb
“Yo ___ diecisiete años.” (I'm seventeen years old.)
✅ TRY IT · ser or estar?
“Mi escuela ___ muy grande y moderna.” (My school is very big and modern — a lasting trait.)
✅ TRY IT · make gustar agree
“A mí ___ las clases de arte y de música.” (I like the art and music classes.)

Now build the opening of your own description out loud — name + age + where you're from + one thing you like. Say it before you peek, then reveal a model.

Hola, me llamo Daniel. Tengo dieciséis años y soy de Tampa. Estudio en una escuela en línea y me gustan los videojuegos. Yours will use your own facts — the frame is Me llamo… Tengo… años y soy de… Me gusta(n)…
🎤 TU BORRADOR · TAREA ORAL: “DESCRIBE TU MUNDO”
The Tarea oral is a 60–90 second recorded talk where you describe yourself and your world — you speaking to the camera, no partner needed. Draft it now in four moves, using a connector between each:
  1. Quién soy — name, age, where you're from (ser, tener).
  2. Mi escuela — where you study and what it's like (estudiar, ser/estar).
  3. Mi clase favorita — one class you like and por qué (gustar + porque).
  4. Mis gustos — one or two things you like to do (gustar, connector también).
Write it as a short script, then say it aloud two or three times before Day 9 — smoother each pass. You'll record and submit the Tarea oral in Canvas; today is the draft.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER · UN MODELO 7 min

Read one student's “mundo” — then borrow the shape

«¡Hola! Me llamo Valeria y tengo diecisiete años. Soy de Quito, Ecuador, pero ahora estudio en una escuela en línea. Mi escuela es pequeña y mis profesores son muy simpáticos. Primero uso la computadora para mis clases y luego hago la tarea en mi cuarto. Mi clase favorita es la clase de ciencias porque me gustan los experimentos. Además, me gusta mucho la música y también toco la guitarra. En mi opinión, mi mundo es tranquilo y feliz.»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find each of the four toolbox verbs in Valeria's paragraph — where does she use ser, estar, tener, and gustar? Then circle every connector (primero, luego, porque, además, también, en mi opinión). Notice she uses me gustan los experimentos (plural) but me gusta la música (singular) — that's the agreement you practiced. Her paragraph is a ready-made skeleton for your own draft.
🌎 CULTURA · EL MUNDO HISPANO
Un mundo, muchos mundos

When Valeria in Quito describes su mundo, it looks different from a student's in Buenos Aires, Madrid, or Los Ángeles — different schools (el colegio, la prepa, el instituto), different foods, different landscapes — yet all of it is described in the same language. That's the quiet wonder of the Spanish-speaking world: 500 million people across 21 countries, each with a distinct daily life, sharing one set of words to tell about it. As you draft your own mundo today, you're joining that enormous conversation. (In the full course this pairs with a short gallery of students introducing their worlds from across the hispanophone map.)

★ LA VIRTUD · LAS SIETE VIRTUDES

Recording your own voice and sending it in takes a real, quiet valor — one of las siete virtudes this course keeps asking you to practice. Rehearsing your description two or three times before you hit record is responsabilidad toward your own progress, not perfectionism. Courage to be heard and responsibility to prepare well: today's Tarea oral needs both.
