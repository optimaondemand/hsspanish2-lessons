★ Repaso y autoevaluación — Pulling It All Together
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Today you don't learn anything new — you find out how much you already own.
This is the last day of the repaso. Nothing new goes in today; instead you take everything Module 0 reactivated — the present tense of regular -ar / -er / -ir verbs plus your four workhorse verbs ser, estar, tener, and gustar — and check it against yourself. Work through each self-check honestly. Where you hesitate, that's your study list for the Grammar Quiz. Where you're quick, that's proof your Spanish is fully back on.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to conjugate a regular -ar / -er / -ir verb in every present-tense form without stopping to think.
2
I will be able to choose correctly between ser and estar and use tener and gustar in a full sentence.
3
I will be able to name exactly which points I still need to review before I open the Grammar Quiz.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Take your own temperature

A good review starts with an honest look at where you stand. Read this little self-portrait aloud and notice which pieces feel automatic: Me llamo… Soy de… Estoy bien. Tengo dieciséis años y me gusta la música. Four different verbs, one sentence about you. That's the whole module in miniature.

🧠 PART 3 · EL PRESENTE DE UN VISTAZO 10 min

The whole present tense on two tables

First, the regular endings you drop onto any verb after you remove -ar / -er / -ir. Notice the -er and -ir columns are identical except for the nosotros form:

Subject-ar (hablar)-er (comer)-ir (vivir)

Then the four irregular verbs Module 0 leaned on. Keep this as your quick-reference — the third column is when you reach for each one:

Verbyo · tú · él/ella formsUse it for…
ser (to be)soy · eres · esidentity, origin, what something is: Soy de Texas. Ella es alta.
estar (to be)estoy · estás · estáfeelings, location, condition right now: Estoy cansado. El libro está aquí.
tener (to have)tengo · tienes · tienepossession & age: Tengo un perro. Tengo quince años.
gustar (to please)me / te / le gusta(n)likes: gusta + one thing, gustan + more than one: Me gusta el café. Me gustan los libros.

The one-line reminder for ser vs. estar: ser tells us who/what (permanent identity), estar tells us how/where (a state or place that can change).

🧠 PART 4 · AUTOEVALUACIÓN 12 min

Four checks — one for each verb family

Answer each one before you check it. A miss isn't a problem — it's a signpost pointing you to what to review tonight.

✅ CHECK 1 · regular present
“Mis amigos y yo ___ español todos los días.” (My friends and I study Spanish every day.)
✅ CHECK 2 · ser vs. estar
“Hoy yo ___ un poco cansada.” (Today I'm a little tired.)
✅ CHECK 3 · tener
“¿Cuántos años ___ (tú)?” (How old are you?)
✅ CHECK 4 · gustar
“A mí me ___ las clases de arte.” (I like art classes.)

Now put all four verbs to work at once. Describe yourself out loud in one sentence: your name, where you're from, how you feel today, one thing you have, and one thing you like. Say it first, then reveal a model.

Me llamo Sofía. Soy de Chicago, estoy contenta hoy, tengo dos hermanos y me gustan los deportes. Yours uses your own facts — the frame is Soy de… (ser) + estoy… (estar) + tengo… (tener) + me gusta(n)… (gustar). If you produced all four cleanly, you're ready for the quiz.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

«Hola. Me llamo Diego y soy de Quito, la capital de Ecuador. Ahora mismo estoy en la biblioteca porque estudio para un examen. Tengo diecisiete años y vivo con mi familia cerca del centro. En mi tiempo libre me gusta la música y me gustan mucho los idiomas — aprendo español, inglés y un poco de quechua. Los fines de semana mis amigos y yo comemos en el mercado y hablamos de todo. ¡La vida en Quito me gusta mucho!»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Every Module 0 verb is hiding in this paragraph. Find one example of each: a regular -ar, a regular -er, a regular -ir, ser, estar, tener, and gustar. Why is it me gusta la música but me gustan los idiomas? (One thing vs. more than one.)
🌎 CULTURA · EL MUNDO HISPANO
Del saludo al mundo entero

Two weeks ago you started with a single hola. Today you can introduce yourself, describe how you feel, say what you have, and share what you like — and you can do it with speakers from Quito to Sevilla to San Juan. That reach is real: Spanish is official in 21 countries and spoken by roughly 500 million people, and the small differences you'll meet — ¿cómo estás? in one country, ¿cómo andas? in another — are the texture of a living language, not obstacles. Every module this year drops you into a new corner of that world. This module was the doorway; keep walking through it. (In the full course this closes with a short mapa del mundo hispano reflection before the Grammar Quiz.)

★ LA VIRTUD · LAS SIETE VIRTUDES

You've now practiced pieces of las siete virtudes — valor, cortesía, honestidad, perseverancia, autogobierno, servicio, and responsabilidad — across these ten days, usually without noticing it happening. Two weeks of small, steady sessions turned a rusty summer into a working present tense; that is perseverancia in practice. Bring the same habits into today's self-check and into Module 1 — the seven virtudes don't end with this module, they just keep showing up in new grammar.
