★ El presente: verbos en -er / -ir
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you switched the -ar engine back on. Today the other two verb families come along — and they're nearly identical.
Spanish has exactly three regular verb families, sorted by the last two letters of the infinitive: -ar (Lesson 0.03), and today -er and -ir. Here's the good news you may have forgotten over the summer: -er and -ir verbs are conjugated almost exactly the same way — they differ in only two forms. Master one and you've nearly mastered both. Today we reactivate familiar verbs like comer, leer, vivir, and escribir so you can say what you eat, read, and write, and where you live.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to conjugate regular -er verbs in the present tense (comer → como, comes, come…).
2
I will be able to conjugate regular -ir verbs and name the only two forms (nosotros and vosotros) where -er and -ir differ.
3
I will be able to describe everyday activities — what I eat, read, and write, and where I live — using -er and -ir verbs.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

You already know the pattern

Yesterday you conjugated -ar verbs by dropping the ending and adding -o, -as, -a, -amos, -áis, -an. The -er and -ir families work the very same way — drop the ending, add new endings. Read these aloud and feel how natural they still are: Yo como pan. Tú lees un libro. Nosotros vivimos aquí.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Which of these everyday actions do you do most on a normal day?
📚 PART 2 · VERBOS CONOCIDOS 10 min

Familiar verbs, sorted into two families

You met all of these in Spanish 1 — today we simply reactivate them and notice each one's ending. Click the 🔊 and say each infinitive back. First, the -er family:

  • comerto eat
  • beberto drink
  • leerto read
  • aprenderto learn
  • comprenderto understand
  • correrto run

And the -ir family:

  • vivirto live
  • escribirto write
  • abrirto open
  • compartirto share
  • recibirto receive
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Sort each verb by its last two letters into its family in your head: comer, beber, leer, aprender, comprender, correr are -er; vivir, escribir, abrir, compartir, recibir are -ir. Knowing the family is the first step to conjugating it — that's exactly what Part 3 is for.
🧠 PART 3 · LAS TERMINACIONES 9 min

Two families, almost one pattern

To conjugate, drop the -er or -ir from the infinitive and add the ending that matches the subject. Look at comer and vivir side by side — the endings are identical except in the nosotros and vosotros forms (highlighted):

Four of the six forms are letter-for-letter the same. Only nosotros (-emos vs. -imos) and vosotros (-éis vs. -ís) tell the two families apart. Learn -er and you've all but learned -ir.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 9 min
✅ TRY IT · the nosotros form
“Mi familia y yo ___ en Chicago.” (My family and I live in Chicago.)
✅ TRY IT · the tú form
“¿Qué ___ (tú) en la clase de historia?” (What do you learn in history class?)
✅ TRY IT · the ellos form
“Mis amigos ___ mensajes todos los días.” (My friends write messages every day.)

Your turn: how would you say “We read and write in class”? Say it out loud first, then reveal a model.

Leemos y escribimos en clase. Both verbs take the nosotros ending — but watch the families: leer is -er → leemos, while escribir is -ir → escribimos. That's the one spot the two families part ways.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

«Hola, me llamo Valentina y vivo en Montevideo, Uruguay. Los domingos mi familia come en la casa de mi abuela. Nosotros compartimos un asado enorme y después bebemos mate juntos. Mientras los adultos conversan, mi primo y yo leemos las revistas viejas de la abuela y yo escribo en mi cuaderno. Así aprendo palabras nuevas cada semana. ¿Y tú? ¿Con quién comes los domingos?»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find every -er and -ir verb in the paragraph (there are seven). Which one is in the nosotros form, and how can you tell whether it belongs to the -er or the -ir family? (Hint: look at the ending — -emos or -imos.)
🌎 CULTURA · EL MUNDO HISPANO
La sobremesa: comer es solo el principio

Across the Spanish-speaking world — from Spain to Uruguay to Mexico — the meal doesn't end when the plates are empty. The sobremesa (literally “over the table”) is the cherished stretch of time when families stay seated and simply talk, sometimes for hours. People keep bebiendo coffee or mate, they comparten stories, and no one is in a hurry to leave. Notice how many of today's verbs power that scene: you comer, you beber, you compartir. In much of the Hispanic world, sharing a table is less about the food than about the people around it. (In the full course this pairs with a short clip of a family sobremesa and a reflection prompt.)

★ LA VIRTUD · LAS SIETE VIRTUDES

Today's endings ask for the same virtue as yesterday's: perseverancia, one of Optima's siete virtudes, shown in going back over -er and -ir verbs even after you thought you already knew the pattern. There's also a small valor in speaking a new ending out loud before you're sure it's right — language learning rewards students willing to risk the mistake. Both habits belong to las siete virtudes this course keeps coming back to.
