Lesson 0.07
★ Lo que te gusta — y cómo decirlo bien
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Gustar doesn't work like “to like.” It works backwards — and once you see the trick, it's easy.
You've said me gusta since Spanish 1. Today we look under the hood: gustar literally means to be pleasing, so the thing you like is really the subject, and a little pronoun (me, te, le, nos, les) says to whom. Master that one pattern and you unlock a whole family of verbs — encantar and interesar behave exactly the same way. No new theme vocabulary today; we reuse words you already own and aim the spotlight at the structure.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to choose gusta vs. gustan depending on whether one thing or several things is liked.
2
I will be able to attach the right indirect object pronoun (me, te, le, nos, les) to say who likes something.
3
I will be able to use encantar and interesar the same way, and clarify with a mí / a ti / a + a name.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

You already do this — let's notice how

Read these two aloud: Me gusta la música. and Me gustan los videojuegos. Same feeling, but the verb changed — gusta became gustan. Why? Because the second sentence is about several things. In English “I like” never changes; in Spanish the verb agrees with the thing being liked, not with you. That's the whole puzzle for today.

⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +4 min
Sort the list in your head into three buckets: singular things (la música, el deporte, la comida), plural things (los videojuegos, las películas, los animales), and actions (viajar, cocinar, bailar, leer). Keep this map handy — singular things and actions take gusta; plural things take gustan.
🧠 PART 3 · CÓMO FUNCIONA GUSTAR 10 min

The backwards verb, made simple

Think of gustar as “to be pleasing to.” So Me gusta la música literally says “Music is pleasing to me.” The music is the subject; me is a little pronoun that answers to whom? Here is the full set of those pronouns:

¿A quién?PronounExample
(a mí)meMe gusta el café.
(a ti)te¿Te gusta el café?
(a él / ella / usted)leA ella le gusta el café.
(a nosotros/as)nosNos gusta el café.
(a ellos / ellas / ustedes)lesA ellos les gusta el café.

(In Spain you'll also hear os for a vosotros. Across Latin America — the Spanish we model this year — les covers the whole “you all” group.)

Now the part everyone trips on — gusta or gustan? The verb agrees with the thing, not the person:

Use…When the thing liked is…Example
gustaone thing (singular)Me gusta la comida.
gustaan action / verb (infinitive)Me gusta viajar. / Me gusta cocinar y leer.
gustanseveral things (plural)Me gustan los animales.

Same exact machine for two more verbs you can start using today:

  • encantarto love / really like — Me encanta bailar. Me encantan las películas.
  • interesarto interest — Me interesa la historia. Me interesan los deportes.

Tip: to make any of these negative, just put no in front — No me gusta cocinar. A él no le interesan los videojuegos.

📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Me llamo Camila y soy de Bogotá, Colombia. A mí me encanta la música — toco la guitarra todos los días. También me gustan los animales; tengo dos perros. A mi hermano Diego le interesan los videojuegos más que nada, pero no le gusta leer. A nosotros dos nos gusta cocinar juntos los domingos: preparamos arepas para toda la familia. ¿Y a ti? ¿Qué te gusta hacer?

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find every gustar-type verb in the passage. Which ones are gusta/encanta/interesa (singular or action) and which are gustan/interesan (plural)? Notice how le switches to nos when Camila talks about herself and Diego.
🌎 CULTURA · EL MUNDO HISPANO
¿Qué le gusta al mundo hispano?

Ask across the Spanish-speaking world what people love, and one answer comes back almost everywhere: el fútbol. It's the most popular sport in nearly every Spanish-speaking country, and national teams from Argentina, España, and Uruguay have all lifted the World Cup. But taste is beautifully local too: to a family in Bogotá les encanta the accordion-driven rhythms of vallenato; in Mexico les gusta a Sunday of tacos and mariachi; in Spain les interesa the late-evening ritual of tapas. Learning to say precisely what pleases whom — the exact grammar of today's lesson — is how you start trading favorites with 500 million people.
