Lesson 7.04
★ El progresivo con -er/-ir: leyendo, durmiendo y más
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Not every verb turns into “-ando” — and a few refuse to follow the rule at all.
Yesterday you built estar + -ando for -ar verbs (hablando, jugando). Today you finish the picture: -er and -ir verbs take -iendo (comiendo, viviendo), and a handful of very common verbs bend the spelling — leer → leyendo, dormir → durmiendo. These aren't random exceptions; they follow two small, predictable patterns you'll use constantly this year.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to form the present progressive of regular -er/-ir verbs with -iendo (comiendo, viviendo, escribiendo).
2
I will be able to use the two irregular-gerund patterns correctly: vowel-stem verbs like leer → leyendo, oír → oyendo, traer → trayendo; and stem-changing -ir verbs like dormir → durmiendo, pedir → pidiendo, decir → diciendo.
3
I will be able to read a short passage describing what several people are doing right now and identify each gerund correctly.

🧠 PART 3 · -IENDO Y SUS DOS PATRONES IRREGULARES 10 min

One ending, two small exceptions

For -er and -ir verbs, drop the ending and add -iendo — the same estar frame from yesterday:

VerbGerundExample
comercomiendoEstoy comiendo un sándwich.
vivirviviendoElla está viviendo en Montevideo.
escribirescribiendoEstamos escribiendo la tarea.

Pattern 1 — vowel-stem verbs: when the stem ends in a vowel, the unstressed -i- of -iendo becomes a y so two vowels don't collide.

VerbGerundExample
leerleyendo¿Qué estás leyendo?
oíroyendoEstamos oyendo música paraguaya.
traertrayendoMi abuela está trayendo mate.
construirconstruyendoEllos están construyendo una casa.

Pattern 2 — stem-changing -ir verbs: -ir verbs that stem-change in the present (e→i, o→u) carry that same change into the gerund — even verbs like dormir that don't stem-change everywhere in the present tense.

VerbGerundExample
dormir (o→u)durmiendoEl bebé está durmiendo la siesta.
pedir (e→i)pidiendo¡Está pidiendo más tereré!
servir (e→i)sirviendoNos están sirviendo mate.
decir (e→i)diciendo¿Qué estás diciendo?

Two rules, ten verbs, no need to memorize each one separately: vowel stem → y; stem-changing -ir → carry the change into -iendo.

📖 PART 5 · A LEER 6 min

Son las cuatro de la tarde en una casa en Montevideo. Toda la familia está en el patio. El abuelo está sirviendo mate y su nieta le está pidiendo más azúcar. La madre está leyendo el periódico y, de vez en cuando, está diciendo algo sobre las noticias. El perro está durmiendo debajo de la mesa, tranquilo. En la cocina, el padre está escribiendo una lista para el supermercado mientras está oyendo la radio. Nadie está comiendo todavía — solo están tomando mate y disfrutando la tarde juntos.

🌎 CULTURA · URUGUAY Y PARAGUAY
El mate: una taza que se comparte

In both Uruguay and Paraguay, few scenes are as common as the one in today's reading: a group passing a single gourd of yerba mate from hand to hand, refilled again and again by whoever is holding the thermos. Uruguayans drink more mate per person than any other country in the world — you'll see people carrying a thermos under one arm on the street, at school, even at the beach. In Paraguay, the same herb is often drunk cold, as tereré, to fight the heat, and Guaraní, the indigenous language spoken alongside Spanish by most Paraguayans, gave mate culture many of its words. The ritual is never really about the drink itself — it's about the circle of people paying attention to one another while it's passed around.

★ LA VIRTUD · COURTESY

José Mujica (Uruguay, 1935–2025) kept a small personal library on his chacra outside Montevideo, and visitors often found him leyendo philosophy or oyendo a guest out fully before responding — two simple, courteous habits: reading closely and listening well. Those are exactly today's irregular gerunds. Real courtesy often looks like patience with a book or a person — está leyendo, está oyendo — giving your full attention is itself a form of respect.
