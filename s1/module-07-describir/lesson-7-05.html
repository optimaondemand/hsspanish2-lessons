Lesson 7.05
★ ¿Progresivo o presente simple?
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Same two verb forms, two very different jobs.
You already know how to build the presente progresivo (estar + -ando/-iendo) and you've used the presente simple all year. Today isn't about learning a new form — it's about learning to choose between the two you already have. The signal is almost always a time expression hiding in the sentence: does it point to right now, or to always / every day? Get that signal right, and the verb form follows automatically.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to recognize time-expression cues (ahora, siempre, todos los días…) that signal habit versus “happening right now.”
2
I will be able to choose correctly between the presente simple and the presente progresivo based on those cues.
3
I will be able to describe someone's routine and what they're doing at this exact moment, in the same short paragraph.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

Two sentences, one question

Read both sentences aloud: (1) Silvia juega al vóleibol los sábados. (2) Silvia está jugando al vóleibol ahora mismo. Only one of them tells you what's happening at this exact moment.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Which sentence describes what Silvia is doing right now, at this exact moment?

🧠 PART 3 · ¿CUÁNDO SE USA CADA UNO? 11 min

Same builder, two different jobs

Quick reminder, not new material: the progressive is estar (conjugated) + gerundio (-ando for -ar verbs, -iendo for -er/-ir verbs). Today's real question isn't how to build it — it's when to reach for it instead of the simple present.

 Presente simplePresente progresivo
Se usa para…hábitos, rutinas y verdades generalesuna acción en desarrollo, justo ahora
Expresiones clavesiempre, todos los días, normalmente, a veces, por lo generalahora, en este momento, ahora mismo, en este instante
EjemploLos paraguayos toman mate todos los días.¡Mira! Ese chico está tomando mate ahora mismo.

One trap: Spanish almost never uses the progressive for a plan or a habit, even when English does. “I'm playing volleyball on Saturdays” (habit, disguised as an -ing verb in English) is still juego in Spanish — not estoy jugando.

📖 PART 5 · A LEER 4 min

En Montevideo, la familia de Sofía se reúne todos los domingos en el patio. Por lo general, su abuelo prepara el mate y su madre cuenta historias de Paraguay, de donde es su familia. Pero hoy es diferente: en este momento, su abuelo está contando un chiste, y todos están riendo mientras el mate pasa de mano en mano. Sofía saca fotos cada domingo para su álbum — y ahora mismo, está sacando una del abuelo a mitad de su chiste.

🌎 CULTURA · URUGUAY Y PARAGUAY
La ronda de mate — un hábito y un momento

In Uruguay, drinking mate is such a daily habit that many people carry a termo (thermos) and gourd with them everywhere — to work, to the beach, even walking down the street. Uruguayans toman mate constantly; it's simply what they do (presente simple). But zoom in on any single afternoon and you'll find a specific ronda — a circle of friends where, right now, one person está cebando (is pouring) and passing the gourd to the next (presente progresivo). In Paraguay, the same ritual turns cold and refreshing in the heat as tereré, and conversation often mixes Spanish with guaraní, the country's co-official Indigenous language. The habit and the moment are the same drink — exactly the two verb forms you practiced today.

★ LA VIRTUD · COURTESY

José Mujica (Uruguay, 1935–2025) showed courtesy both as a lifelong habit and in single, specific moments — he lived simply and treated people with respect for decades, but he's also remembered for particular afternoons when he personally welcomed a stranger at his farm gate. Spanish needs both tenses for the same reason: some things are simply true of a person (habit), and some things are happening only right now (progressive). Sorting habit from moment today is really sorting the courtesy someone always shows from the courtesy they're showing you this instant.
