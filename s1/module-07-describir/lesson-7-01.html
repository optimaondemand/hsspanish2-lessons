📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Before you compare two people, you have to be able to describe just one, precisely.
Welcome to Módulo 7: Describir y comparar. Last module you compared clothes and prices; this module you'll compare people — but comparison only works if the description underneath it is accurate. Today is a pure vocabulary and review day: no new grammar. You'll widen your bank of physical-description adjectives (this is vocab set A — personality words return in 7.02), and you'll sharpen a rule you already know from Spanish 1: an adjective has to agree, in gender and number, with the person it describes. Getting that agreement automatic now is what will let you describe and compare fluently later this module.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to recognize and pronounce an expanded set of physical-description adjectives (height, build, hair, age).
2
I will be able to make an adjective agree correctly in gender and number with the person it describes (review + expansion).
3
I will be able to describe a real person's physical appearance in two or three accurate sentences with ser.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

Picture someone, then try to describe them

Close your eyes for a second and picture a grandparent, a coach, or a close friend. Now try to describe them out loud in Spanish — height, hair, build — using only what you already know from Spanish 1. Notice where you run out of words. That gap is exactly what today's vocabulary fills.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
When you tried to describe that person just now, what happened?
📚 PART 2 · VOCABULARIO A · EL ASPECTO FÍSICO 13 min

Twelve words for describing how someone looks

Some of these are a refresh from Spanish 1; several are new additions that let you be more precise. Click the 🔊 and say each one back — and notice the /a ending on most of them, which is exactly what Part 3 is about.

  • alto/atall
  • bajo/ashort (height)
  • de estatura medianaof average height
  • delgado/athin, slim
  • fuertestrong, sturdy
  • rubio/ablond(e)
  • moreno/adark-haired, dark-skinned
  • pelirrojo/ared-haired
  • calvo/abald
  • guapo/agood-looking, attractive
  • jovenyoung
  • mayorolder, elderly
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +4 min
For each word, say a sentence about a real person you know: Mi abuelo es mayor y fuerte. Mi prima es rubia y delgada. Saying the -o/-a ending out loud, attached to a real face, is what makes it stick.
🧠 PART 3 · REPASO · LA CONCORDANCIA DE ADJETIVOS 8 min

The rule you already know — now applied to a bigger list

No new grammar today — just the agreement rule from Spanish 1, reviewed so it's automatic before Module 7 asks you to compare people. Adjectives change their ending to match the person they describe:

Adjective typeMasc. sing.Fem. sing.Plural
ends in -oaltoaltaaltos / altas
ends in -e (no change)fuertefuertefuertes
ends in a consonant (no change)jovenjovenjóvenes
ends in -dor (adds -a)trabajadortrabajadoratrabajadores / trabajadoras

One-line reminder: -o/-a words change for gender; -e and most consonant endings don't — but everything adds -s or -es for plural. Mi hermana es alta y fuerte. Mis hermanos son altos y fuertes.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 9 min
✅ TRY IT · agreement with a woman
“Mi tía es muy ___.” (My aunt is very tall.)
✅ TRY IT · agreement with plural men
“Mis primos son ___.” (My cousins [male] are short.)
✅ TRY IT · pick the right word
“Mi abuelo no tiene pelo. Es ___.” (My grandfather has no hair.)

Your turn: describe one real person — a family member or friend — in two sentences using ser and at least two words from today's list, with correct agreement. Say it out loud, then reveal a model.

Mi hermano es alto y delgado. Mi abuela es baja, pero es fuerte y todavía muy activa para su edad. Yours will use your own family — the frame is [persona] es [adjetivo] y [adjetivo], with every adjective agreeing with that person.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

«Me llamo Rocío y vivo en Montevideo, Uruguay, con mi familia. Mi papá es alto y fuerte; trabaja en el puerto. Mi mamá es de estatura mediana y morena, con el pelo largo. Mi hermano menor, Tomás, todavía es muy joven, pero ya es tan alto como yo. Y mi abuelo, que es mayor y calvo, siempre tiene una historia nueva para contar. Los domingos tomamos mate juntos en el patio y escuchamos sus cuentos.»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find every adjective describing a person and check its agreement: why alto for Papá but morena for Mamá? Why does joven not change even though it describes Tomás, a boy? (It ends in a consonant.)
🌎 CULTURA · URUGUAY
El mate: una taza que pasa de mano en mano

In Uruguay, sharing mate (a hot, bitter infusion drunk from a shared gourd through a metal straw, the bombilla) is one of the most common daily rituals — on patios, on buses, at the beach, at every family gathering like Rocío's Sunday afternoon. One person, the cebador, prepares the mate and passes it around a circle; everyone drinks from the same straw, then hands it back to be refilled and passed to the next person. It's slow on purpose: nobody rushes a round of mate. Grandparents like Rocío's, who are mayores and full of stories, are often the ones holding the circle together, and the ritual only works if everyone pays close attention — whose turn is next, when the gourd is empty, when someone is telling a story worth hearing. (In the full course this pairs with a short video of a mate round and a map locating Uruguay and Paraguay, both mate-drinking countries.)

★ LA VIRTUD · COURTESY

José Mujica (Uruguay, 1935–2025), the former president the world came to know as “el presidente más pobre,” described friend and stranger alike with the same plain, respectful attention — never mocking how tall, short, or simply someone was dressed. That's the courtesy behind today's vocabulary: words like alta, bajo, and pelirrojo are just honest description, offered kindly, never as insults. Courtesy — la cortesía — means noticing someone's traits accurately and naming them with respect, exactly the balance Mujica modeled toward everyone he met.
