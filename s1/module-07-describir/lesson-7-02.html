★ Describir lugares y cosas
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A good description does two jobs at once: what a place is like, and what's happening in it right now.
Yesterday you picked up a first set of describing words. Today you add a second set — words for how a place or thing feels: comfortable or not, crowded or empty, cheap or expensive, calm or noisy. You'll pair every new word with something you already own from Spanish 1: the present progressive (estar + -ando/-iendo), which lets you say exactly what's going on in a place at this moment.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use a new set of adjectives — cómodo, ruidoso, lleno, caro, and more — to describe how a place or thing feels.
2
I will be able to say what's happening in a place right now using the present progressive (estar + -ando/-iendo).
3
I will be able to combine an adjective and a present-progressive verb into one description of a place.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Two ways to describe the same place

Think about your school cafeteria at lunchtime. You could describe it with an adjective — ruidosa (noisy) — or with an action — los estudiantes están hablando y comiendo (the students are talking and eating). Both are true at the same time. Today you'll practice both moves.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Picture your favorite café or restaurant on a Saturday afternoon. Which word comes to mind first?

🧠 PART 3 · REPASO — EL PRESENTE PROGRESIVO 9 min

Saying what's happening right now

You met the present progressive in Spanish 1: estar + a gerund (-ando for -ar verbs, -iendo for -er/-ir verbs). Today's job isn't to learn something new — it's to make it automatic so you can drop it into any description.

SubjectestarGerund pattern
yoestoyhablando · comiendo
túestástú estás escribiendo
usted / él / ellaestáella está mirando
nosotros/asestamosestamos comiendo
ustedes / ellos / ellasestánestán hablando

Put a describing word and the progressive in the same sentence and you get a full picture: El café está lleno y la gente está conversando. (The café is full and people are talking.)

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 8 min
✅ TRY IT · choose the adjective

✅ TRY IT · pick the present-progressive form

✅ TRY IT · match the scene

Your turn: describe a real place near you right now — one adjective + one present-progressive verb. Say it out loud, then reveal a model.

El parque está tranquilo. Unos perros están caminando y unos niños están jugando cerca del lago. Yours will use your own place — the frame is [lugar] está [adjetivo]. [Sujeto] está [verbo-ando/iendo]…
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Son las doce del mediodía en el Mercado del Puerto de Montevideo, Uruguay. El edificio es antiguo — tiene más de 150 años — pero está lleno de vida. Los cocineros están preparando parrilladas en las parrillas de hierro, y el olor a carne asada llena el aire. El ambiente es ruidoso y alegre: la gente está conversando, riendo y comiendo junta en mesas largas. Algunos turistas están sacando fotos de la estructura de hierro. No es un lugar tranquilo, ni barato — pero es auténtico e inolvidable.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find all four describing words and all three present-progressive verbs in the paragraph. Notice how they work together — the adjectives paint the mood, the progressive verbs show the action inside it.
🌎 CULTURA · URUGUAY Y PARAGUAY
Un lugar de hierro, una cosa de hilo

The Mercado del Puerto in Montevideo, Uruguay, opened in 1868 as a cast-iron market building; today it's famous across South America for its parrilladas (grilled-meat restaurants) packed under the original iron beams. Just across the river in Paraguay, the town of Itauguá is known for ñanduti lace — its name means “spiderweb” in Guaraní — a delicate, hand-stitched circular pattern traditionally made by women and worn for festivals. One is a place, antiguo but full of life; the other is a cosa, small but intricate. Both are exactly the kind of thing today's vocabulary lets you describe.

★ LA VIRTUD · COURTESY

José Mujica (Uruguay, 1935–2025) is remembered less for any single policy than for a personality trait: he greeted senators and farmers, journalists and strangers, with the exact same warmth and humility. Personality words like amable and generoso aren't just vocabulary — they describe the kind of courtesy Mujica practiced daily, never treating anyone as less important than himself. As you build sentences with the present progressive today, notice how many of them could describe a person siendo or actuando con cortesía — being courteous, right now, in some small visible way.
