★ Describe una escena
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Stop narrating your own life for a second — narrate someone else's, frame by frame.
You already know estar + gerundio and you already know how to compare things. Today you put both to work on a single task: describing a photo or a scene as if you were standing right inside it, narrating what everyone is doing in real time. This is a production day — no new grammar, no new vocabulary. You're drafting and rehearsing the description that becomes your recorded Tarea oral.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to narrate a photo or scene aloud, using estar + gerundio to describe what people are doing right now.
2
I will be able to layer in a comparison (más…que, tan…como) to make my scene description richer and more precise.
3
I will be able to draft and rehearse the scene description that becomes my recorded Tarea oral.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

Freeze a moment in your own house

Close your eyes for five seconds and picture a photo taken right now, somewhere in your house. Don't describe the room — describe what someone is doing in it. That's the skill for today: pinning an action mid-motion with estar + -ando/-iendo.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Picture that photo of your kitchen right now. What's the first thing happening in it?

🧠 PART 3 · REPASO — ESTAR + GERUNDIO Y LA COMPARACIÓN 8 min

The two tools working together

To catch an action mid-motion, conjugate estar and attach a gerundio (-ando for -ar verbs, -iendo for -er/-ir verbs):

SubjectestarExample
yoestoyYo estoy describiendo la foto.
túestás¿Qué estás mirando?
usted / él / ellaestáEl vendedor está vendiendo fruta.
nosotros/asestamosNosotros estamos caminando por la plaza.
ustedes / ellos / ellasestánEllas están tomando mate.

Watch the irregular gerundios you already met: leer → leyendo, oír → oyendo, dormir → durmiendo, pedir → pidiendo, decir → diciendo.

And since this module is describir y comparar, fold in a comparison to sharpen the picture: más + adjective + que (more…than) or tan + adjective + como (as…as). El mercado está más lleno que la plaza. / La plaza está tan tranquila como mi calle.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 9 min
✅ TRY IT · complete the description
“Mira la foto: dos amigos ___ mate en la plaza.” (two friends are drinking mate)
✅ TRY IT · narrate yourself narrating
“Yo ___ la escena para mi Tarea oral.” (I am describing the scene)
✅ TRY IT · add the comparison
“El mercado de Asunción es ___ la plaza pequeña de mi pueblo.” (bigger than)

Your turn: draft your Tarea oral. Pick a real or imagined photo and say, out loud, four sentences — where it is, who's in it, what two people are doing (estar + gerundio), and one comparison. Then reveal a model.

Esta es una foto de mi patio. Mi hermano está jugando al fútbol y mi madre está leyendo un libro en la silla. El perro está durmiendo cerca de ella. Mi patio está más tranquilo que el parque del barrio. Yours will use your own photo and people — the frame is lugar → personas → estar + gerundio → comparación.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Es una mañana de domingo en la Feria de Tristán Narvaja, en Montevideo. La plaza y las calles cercanas están llenas de gente. Un vendedor está vendiendo libros viejos debajo de una carpa azul. Dos amigas están caminando entre los puestos, charlando sobre sus planes para la semana. Una de ellas está tomando mate de un termo verde mientras mira las frutas de un puesto — las manzanas de la feria son más frescas que las del supermercado del barrio. Cerca de allí, un abuelo está leyendo el periódico y sonriendo a los niños que juegan cerca. Todos están disfrutando de la mañana, cada uno a su manera.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Count how many -ando/-iendo verbs you can find in bold. Each one freezes a different person mid-action — that's exactly the effect your own Tarea oral is going for.
🌎 CULTURA · URUGUAY
La Feria de Tristán Narvaja

Every Sunday since 1919, Montevideo's Feria de Tristán Narvaja turns dozens of blocks into an open-air market — books, antiques, plants, food, and thousands of people moving through it at once. It's one of the largest street fairs in South America, and it's also a perfect real-world scene to practice describing: nothing in it holds still. Every vendor is selling, every visitor is walking, chatting, or drinking mate from a thermos tucked under one arm — the same mate culture shared across Uruguay and neighboring Paraguay. Learning to narrate a scene like this one, in the moment, is exactly the skill Module 7 is building toward.

★ LA VIRTUD · COURTESY

José Mujica (Uruguay, 1935–2025) always described other people generously, even political rivals, choosing words that respected their dignity rather than words that scored a point. Before you record your Tarea oral today, remember that describing a real person — even one in a photo — is a small act of courtesy: choose accurate words, but choose kind ones. Mujica's example is a good one to sit with while you draft your description.
