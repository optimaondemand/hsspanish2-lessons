Lesson 7.03
★ El presente progresivo: estar + -ando
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Right now, someone in your house is doing something specific — not in general, but in this exact instant. Spanish has a form built exactly for that moment.
In Módulo 6 you paired estar with conditions and locations. Today estar takes on a new job: paired with a gerund (the -ando form of an -ar verb), it builds the present progressive — the tense for “is/are ___-ing,” used only for what's happening right now. Spanish still prefers the plain present for habits and general truths, so today is also about learning when not to reach for this form. We're starting with -ar verbs only; -er and -ir verbs get their own turn in Lesson 7.04.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to form the gerund of a regular -ar verb by dropping -ar and adding -ando.
2
I will be able to combine a conjugated form of estar with an -ando gerund to say what someone is doing at this exact moment.
3
I will be able to tell the difference between a habitual action (plain present) and an in-progress action (present progressive), and choose the right one.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

Right now vs. always

Compare these two true sentences about the same person: Ana estudia mucho (Ana studies a lot — true in general, every week) and Ana está estudiando ahora mismo (Ana is studying right now — true at this exact second, and maybe not true an hour from now). English marks that difference with “-ing.” Today you'll build the Spanish equivalent.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Your friend texts “¿Qué haces?” and you look up from your homework spread across the table. Which sentence best captures THIS exact moment?
📚 PART 2 · VERBOS EN ACCIÓN (REPASO) 11 min

Verbs you already know — now in motion

Every infinitive below is one you've already met. Click 🔊, listen to its -ando form, and repeat it aloud before moving to the next one.

  • hablar → hablandoto speak → speaking
  • cocinar → cocinandoto cook → cooking
  • bailar → bailandoto dance → dancing
  • cantar → cantandoto sing → singing
  • nadar → nadandoto swim → swimming
  • caminar → caminandoto walk → walking
  • escuchar → escuchandoto listen → listening
  • mirar → mirandoto watch → watching
  • trabajar → trabajandoto work → working
  • estudiar → estudiandoto study → studying
  • tomar → tomandoto drink/take → drinking
  • descansar → descansandoto rest → resting
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Cover the right-hand column. Look only at the infinitive and say its -ando form out loud before you check yourself. All twelve follow the exact same pattern — drop -ar, add -ando — with zero exceptions.
🧠 PART 3 · ESTAR + -ANDO 8 min

Two known pieces, one new shape

The present progressive has exactly two parts: a conjugated form of estar (from Módulo 6 — nothing new there) plus a gerund. For -ar verbs, the gerund is always stem + -ando.

Subjectestar (repaso)+ -ando · ahora mismo
yoestoyYo estoy hablando.
túestás¿Tú estás cocinando?
usted / él / ellaestáElla está bailando.
nosotros/asestamosNosotros estamos estudiando.
ustedes / ellos / ellasestánEllos están trabajando.

Formation rule: drop -ar, add -ando. hablar → habl- → hablando. This is completely regular for every -ar verb — even ones that change their stem in the plain present, like jugar (juego) and pensar (pienso). Their gerunds stay perfectly regular: jugando, pensando.

✅ TRY IT · match subject and form

✅ TRY IT · pick the correct gerund
What is the correct -ando form of trabajar?

Your turn: say out loud what you and someone in your family are doing right now, this exact moment — use at least two different -ar verbs in the present progressive. Then reveal a model.

Ahora mismo, yo estoy escribiendo… — wait, that's an -ir verb, save it for 7.04! Stay with -ar: Ahora mismo, yo estoy estudiando y mi mamá está cocinando la cena. Yours will use your own subjects and verbs — the frame is estar (conjugated) + -ando.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Es domingo por la tarde en Montevideo, Uruguay, y toda la familia Fernández está en el patio.
Sofía está escuchando música mientras su hermano Tomás está tomando mate con su abuelo.
En el jardín, los primos están jugando y cantando una canción vieja.
—Mamá, ¿qué estás cocinando? — pregunta Sofía.
—Estoy cocinando un asado para todos — contesta su mamá, sonriendo.
Cerca de la puerta, la abuela está mirando a su familia y está pensando: “¡Qué tarde tan perfecta!”
A todos les gusta este ritual de los domingos: estar juntos, hablando y descansando, sin prisa.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find all the -ando forms in the reading. One comes from pensar and one from jugar — both change their stem in the plain present (pienso, juego) but stay perfectly regular here. That's the pattern from Part 3.
🌎 CULTURA · URUGUAY Y PARAGUAY
El mate: una costumbre que une

When Tomás in the reading is tomando mate, he's part of a ritual shared across Uruguay, Paraguay, Argentina, and southern Brazil. The word mate itself comes from mati, a word in Guaraní — the indigenous language still spoken alongside Spanish by the majority of Paraguayans, and one of Paraguay's two official languages. Uruguay, meanwhile, drinks more mate per person than any other country in the world; it's common to see people carrying a thermos under one arm all day, refilling the same gourd (la calabaza) again and again. The gourd and metal straw (la bombilla) are passed from person to person in a circle — you don't sip your own; you share one. (In the full course this pairs with a short listening clip of a family sharing mate.)

★ LA VIRTUD · COURTESY

José Mujica (Uruguay, 1935–2025) was famous for small acts of courtesy happening in the present moment — opening his own gate for visitors, pouring the mate himself, answering a reporter's question without a hint of superiority. Those are exactly the moments the present progressive exists to capture: not what someone always does, but what they are doing right now. Today, as you consolidate the -ando forms, picture a sentence like está saludando a todos con cortesía — he is greeting everyone courteously — the tense and the virtue fit together naturally.
