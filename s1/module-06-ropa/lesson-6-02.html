★ Ir de compras — la tienda, el mercado y la ganga
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you named the clothes. Today you learn to go get them.
Day 1 gave you the ropa — the shirts, shoes, and jackets. Today we add the words you need to actually go shopping for them: where you shop (la tienda, el mercado), how to ask a price (¿cuánto cuesta?), how to say something fits (me queda bien), and how you'll pay. No new grammar today — just a fresh set of words layered onto the clothing you already own, dropped straight into real shopping conversations.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…

I will be able to name where and how I shop — la tienda, el mercado, el probador, la talla, el número.
2
I will be able to ask a price and comment on fit — ¿cuánto cuesta? and me queda bien / grande.
3

🧠 PART 3 · FRASES PARA LA TIENDA 8 min

Dropping the words into ready-made frames

No new grammar today — every frame below uses verbs you already own. Cuesta and cuestan come from costar (the o→ue stem-changer from Módulo 2). Me queda works just like me gusta — the thing does the "fitting," so it's queda for one item, quedan for more. Me lo pruebo is probarse, a reflexive like the ones from Módulo 4. 📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

En un mercado de La Paz, Bolivia. Marisol quiere comprar un suéter de lana de alpaca.

—Buenas tardes. ¿Cuánto cuesta este suéter?
—Cuesta 120 bolivianos, señorita.
—¿Hay un descuento hoy?
—Sí, hoy cuesta 100. ¡Es una ganga!
—¿Puedo probarmelo? ¿Dónde está el probador?
—Allí, a la derecha… ¿Cómo le queda?
—Me queda grande. ¿Tiene una talla más pequeña?
—Aquí tiene. ¿Y ahora?
—¡Ahora me queda bien! ¿Puedo pagar con tarjeta?
—En el mercado solo aceptamos efectivo.
—Está bien, pago en efectivo. Muchas gracias.

🌎 CULTURA · BOLIVIA
El aguayo: comprar una historia tejida

In a Bolivian mercado, one of the most striking things for sale is the aguayo (also spelled awayu) — a rectangular handwoven cloth the Aymara and Quechua have made for centuries, used to carry babies, goods, and market purchases on the back. Its bold stripes and figures aren't just decoration: in the weaving of the Jalq'a and Tarabuco communities near Sucre, the patterns encode animals, spirits, and stories, and a single fine piece can take weeks on a backstrap loom. When those traditional designs were fading, the ASUR textile project and its Museo de Arte Indígena in Sucre helped revive them, and today weavers sell their work directly to visitors. So when you ask ¿cuánto cuesta? at a Bolivian stall, remember the precio reflects not machine output but many careful hours of a person's hands. (In the full course this pairs with a short gallery of Jalq'a and Tarabuco textiles.)

★ LA VIRTUD · HONESTY

Jaime Escalante (La Paz, Bolivia, 1930–2010), the Bolivian-born math teacher whose honest, hard-nosed teaching turned Garfield High School into a calculus powerhouse (Stand and Deliver, 1988), refused to let a student fake understanding just to move a lesson along. He'd rather hear an honest «No entiendo» than a nod that wasn't true. Today, as you learn the verbs for ir de compras, carry that same honesty into the market: ask clearly for the talla and precio you actually need, instead of guessing to avoid an awkward question.
