🧠 PART 3 · LA GRAMÁTICA DE UN VISTAZO 10 min

The whole module on two tables

First, comparisons. The frame never changes — you just slot the adjective in the middle, and the adjective still agrees with its noun. The bottom row is the four irregulars you never say más in front of:

IdeaFormulaExample
more … thanmás + adj + queLa chaqueta es más cara que la camisa.
less … thanmenos + adj + queLos zapatos son menos caros que las botas.
as … astan + adj + comoLa falda es tan bonita como el vestido.
the most / least (superlative)el/la/los/las (+ noun) + más / menos + adj + deEs el vestido más elegante de la tienda.
irregulars (no más!)mejor / peor / mayor / menorEsta camisa es mejor que esa. Soy menor que mi hermano.

Then the split you polished all module: ser vs. estar. Both mean “to be,” but they answer different questions. Keep this as your quick-reference:

VerbUse it for…Example
ser (soy · eres · es)identity, material, color, origin, and what the price is — the stable traitsLa blusa es de algodón. El suéter es azul. ¿Cuánto es?
estar (estoy · estás · está)condition & location right now — a state that can changeLa tienda está cerrada. El vestido está sucio. El probador está ahí.

The one-line reminder: ser tells us what something is (a lasting trait), estar tells us how or where it is (a state or place that can change).

🧠 PART 4 · AUTOEVALUACIÓN 12 min

Four checks — comparison, superlative, ser/estar, and error-spotting

Answer each one before you check it. A miss isn't a problem — it's a signpost pointing you to what to review tonight.

✅ CHECK 1 · comparison (más / menos / tan)
Esta camiseta cuesta $10. Esa camiseta cuesta $25. → “Esta camiseta es ___ esa.” (This t-shirt is cheaper than that one.)
✅ CHECK 2 · superlative
“It's the most elegant dress in the store.” → “Es el vestido ___ de la tienda.”
✅ CHECK 3 · ser vs. estar
“No puedes entrar — el probador ___ ocupado ahora.” (The fitting room is occupied right now.)
✅ CHECK 4 · spot the correct sentence
One of these is written correctly. Which one?

Now put it all together out loud. Compare two pieces of clothing you own: name each one, say what it is (ser: color or material), how it is right now (estar: clean, new, old), and which one is better or cheaper. Say it first, then reveal a model.

Mi chaqueta azul es de algodón y está nueva. Mis zapatos negros son más viejos, pero la chaqueta es más cara que los zapatos — y para mí, ¡es la mejor de las dos! Yours uses your own clothes — the frame is es… (ser) + está… (estar) + más/menos… que + la mejor/peor. If you produced all of that cleanly, you're ready for the quiz.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

«Me llamo Camila y hoy voy de compras en un mercado de La Paz, Bolivia. Busco un suéter de lana de alpaca. Hay dos: uno rojo y uno gris. El rojo es más bonito, pero también es más caro que el gris. El gris es tan suave como el rojo y cuesta menos — para mí, es la mejor opción. La vendedora está muy contenta y me dice el precio final. 🌎 CULTURA · BOLIVIA
El tejido que cuenta una historia

In the highlands of Bolivia, a piece of clothing is never just clothing. Andean weavers — often working on a backstrap loom exactly as their great-grandmothers did — spin wool from alpaca, llama, and vicuña and weave patterns that identify a person's home community and mark births, harvests, and festivals. A fine handwoven aguayo (the bright carrying-cloth women wear on their backs) can take months and is one of a family's most treasured possessions — genuinely la mejor and más cara item they own, though its real value isn't the price. When you compared a red sweater to a gray one in the reading, you were doing in Spanish exactly what a shopper does in a La Paz market: weighing quality, warmth, and worth. (In the full course this pairs with a short gallery of Andean textile patterns and the stories they carry.)
