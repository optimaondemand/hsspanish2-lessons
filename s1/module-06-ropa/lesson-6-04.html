Lesson 6.04
★ Comparatives in Use — Shopping and Comparing
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you built the comparison. Today you take it shopping.
No new rules and no new words today — just putting the comparison to work. You already know how to say something is más caro que, menos caro que, or tan caro como something else. Now we use those exact frames in a real store: comparing two garments, weighing two prices, choosing between two sizes. At the end you'll read a short market conversation from Bolivia. Watch for one thing that's coming, not being taught: a few superlatives (la más fina, el precio más bajo — "the finest," "the lowest price"). Just recognize what they mean today; you'll build them yourself tomorrow.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to compare two garments by price, size, and quality using más/menos + adjective + que and tan + adjective + como.
2
I will be able to rank three prices from most to least expensive by chaining comparatives in a real shopping situation.
3
I will be able to read a short shopping conversation and understand which item is cheaper, better, or the best — recognizing comparatives and superlatives.

🧠 PART 3 · LAS COMPARACIONES, EN LA TIENDA 9 min

The three frames you already own

Every comparison of two things fits one of three frames. Nothing here is new — this is the toolkit from Lesson 6.03, lined up next to real clothing examples so you can grab the right one fast.

MeaningFrameExample (clothing)
more … thanmás + adj + queLa chaqueta es más cara que la camiseta.
less … thanmenos + adj + queLos zapatos son menos caros que las botas.
as … astan + adj + comoEsta falda es tan bonita como aquella.
as much / as many … astanto/a/os/as + noun + comoTengo tanta ropa como mi hermana.

The adjective still agrees with the first noun: la chaqueta es más cara but los zapatos son más caros. With tan… como, the adjective agrees too; with tanto… como, it's tanto/tanta/tantos/tantas to match the noun that follows.

Two irregulars worth remembering — they replace "más bueno / más malo": use mejor que (better than) and peor que (worse than). So a jacket of finer quality is mejor que the other one — never más bueno que.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 12 min

Compare it out loud, then check

✅ TRY IT · more … than

✅ TRY IT · as … as

✅ TRY IT · the irregular one
This jacket is better quality than that one. Which is correct Spanish?

Your turn: you see three items — unos zapatos ($60), unos pantalones ($40), and una gorra (a cap, $12). Rank them out loud from most to least expensive by chaining two comparatives. Say it first, then reveal a model.

Los zapatos son más caros que los pantalones, y los pantalones son más caros que la gorra. Two comparatives chained together give a full ranking — no new grammar needed. (Tomorrow you'll compress this into one superlative: la gorra es la más barata.)
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

En el mercado de La Paz

—Buenas tardes. Busco una chompa de alpaca. ¿Cuánto cuesta esta?
—Esa chompa cuesta 180 bolivianos. Es más cara que la de lana, pero la alpaca es más suave y más caliente.
—¿Y aquella roja?
—Aquella es más barata: 120 bolivianos. Pero no es tan fina como esta. Esta es la chompa más fina del puesto — la mejor calidad.
—Quiero una talla más grande. ¿Tiene una mediana?
—Claro. Y mire este chal tejido a mano: es tan bonito como la chompa y cuesta menos. Es el regalo más popular para los turistas.
—¡Perfecto! Y es el precio más bajo del mercado. Me llevo la chompa y el chal. Muchas gracias.

🌎 CULTURA · BOLIVIA
El aguayo y los tejidos andinos

The chompa de alpaca the shopper compares is part of one of the world's oldest living weaving traditions. In the Bolivian highlands, Aymara and Quechua weavers still make the aguayo — a rectangular cloth of brilliant stripes used to carry babies, food, and goods on the back — on backstrap looms, using wool from llamas and alpacas. Every region has its own patterns and colors, so a trained eye can tell where a textile was woven; the town of Tarabuco, near Sucre, is famous for its intricate designs and holds the yearly Pujllay festival. When a vendor calls one piece la más fina, she's judging the tightness of the weave and the softness of the fiber — skills passed down for centuries. Prices are quoted in bolivianos (Bs), Bolivia's currency. (In the full course this pairs with a short photo tour of aguayo patterns and a market listening clip.)

★ LA VIRTUD · HONESTY

Jaime Escalante (La Paz, Bolivia, 1930–2010) taught his students that honest self-assessment — knowing exactly where you stand, not where you wish you stood — was the first real step toward progress. Today you take your comparative sentences into the market itself, deciding cuál chompa es más barata or cuál tienda es mejor. Shop with Escalante's honesty: compare what's actually true about price and quality, and say so plainly instead of talking yourself into a bad deal.
