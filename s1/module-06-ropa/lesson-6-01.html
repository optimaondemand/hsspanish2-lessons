★ La ropa — What We Wear, and Why
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
In Module 5 you learned to describe people. Now we dress them — and step into the market.
Welcome to Módulo 6: La ropa y las compras (clothing & shopping). Last module you talked about who people are and what they know (saber / conocer, object pronouns). The very next thing we notice about people is what they're wearing — and that's the doorway to the whole world of ir de compras (going shopping). Today is a pure vocabulary day: no new grammar, just the words for clothing (las prendas), colors, and patterns, each with a button so you can hear a native voice. By the end you'll be able to name what's in your own closet.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name at least eight articles of clothing (las prendas) with the correct article (el / la / los).
2
I will be able to describe clothing by color and pattern, making the color agree in gender and number (una camisa roja, unos zapatos negros).
3
I will be able to say what I and others wear, using llevar (review) + a garment + a color.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

From describing people to dressing them

In Module 5 you could already say Conozco a Ana; es alta y simpática. The next detail you'd notice is her clothes. You know the verb llevar (to wear) from Spanish 1 — a regular -ar verb: yo llevo, tú llevas… Today we're only adding the nouns that go with it. Read this aloud: Hoy llevo una camiseta y unos zapatos cómodos. You already understand more than half of it.

🧠 PART 3 · REPASO RÁPIDO · GÉNERO Y CONCORDANCIA 8 min

Nothing new — just old rules on new words

There's no new grammar today. You already know these two rules from Spanish 1; we're just applying them to the clothing words you just met. First, the article matches the noun's gender and number:

ArticleUse it with…Example
lafeminine, singularla camisa, la falda, la blusa
elmasculine, singularel vestido, el sombrero
losmasculine, plural (incl. pairs)los pantalones, los zapatos
un / unaa / an (one item)un vestido, una chaqueta

Second, a color goes after the noun and agrees with it. Colors ending in -o change (rojo → roja → rojos → rojas); colors like azul, verde, gris, marrón don't change for gender, only add -s / -es for plural.

Garment+ color (agrees)Why
la faldala falda rojafeminine singular → roja
los zapatoslos zapatos negrosmasculine plural → negros
la blusala blusa azulazul doesn't change for gender
los pantaloneslos pantalones verdesverde just adds -s for plural

Patterns join with de and never change: una camisa de rayas, un vestido de lunares.

🌎 CULTURA · BOLIVIA
El aguayo: una prenda que cuenta historias

In the Bolivian Andes, clothing is woven memory. The aguayo — a rectangular cloth of brilliant stripes carried on the back to hold a baby, food, or goods to market — is hand-woven from alpaca and llama wool and dyed in bold colores and rayas. Traditional weaving communities such as the Jalq'a and Tarabuco near Sucre encode symbols, animals, and family identity into every pattern, so a person's textiles can signal where they are from. And the cholitas — Aymara and Quechua women — are known worldwide for the layered pollera skirt, the fringed shawl, and the small bombín (bowler hat) worn tilted on the head. When you learn la falda, el sombrero, de rayas, you're learning to describe a living art form. (In the full course this pairs with a short video of a Tarabuco weaver at work.)

★ LA VIRTUD · HONESTY

Jaime Escalante (La Paz, Bolivia, 1930–2010) built his legendary Garfield High classroom — the true story behind the film Stand and Deliver (1988) — on one honest rule: tell students the plain truth about how hard the work will be, and never inflate what they already know. That same honesty starts today's lesson: as you learn las prendas, los colores, and los patrones, describe what's actually hanging in your own closet, not a fancier wardrobe you wish you had. La honestidad begins with naming things exactly as they are.
