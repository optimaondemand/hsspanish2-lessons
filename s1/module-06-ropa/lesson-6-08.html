📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Everything you've practiced this module — the clothes, the comparisons, ser and estar — comes out of your mouth today, out loud and recorded.
No new grammar and no new words today — this is a production day. You already have the clothing and shopping vocabulary, the comparatives & superlatives (más… que, menos… que, tan… como, el/la más… de), and the ser / estar contrast. Now you'll assemble them into a smooth spoken description that compares an outfit or a market purchase, rehearse the pronunciation, and record your Tarea oral (20 points, presentational rubric). Think of today as the dress rehearsal and the performance.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to describe and compare clothing or purchases out loud, chaining comparatives (más/menos… que, tan… como) and at least one superlative (el/la más… de).
2
I will be able to choose ser or estar correctly — ser for what a thing is (color, material, price), estar for how it looks or fits right now (está de moda, me queda bien).
3
I will be able to draft, rehearse, and record my Tarea oral so it meets all three rubric criteria: task completion, accuracy, and pronunciation/fluency.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

Warm up your voice before you record

Athletes stretch; speakers warm up their mouth. Say this comparison aloud three times, a little smoother each time: Esta camisa es más bonita que esa, pero esa es la más barata de la tienda. Feel how the soft d in bonita… wait — listen for it in vestido and moda in Part 3. Comprehensible and steady beats fast and choppy every time.

🧠 PART 3 · FONÉTICA: LA D SUAVE + LA ESTRUCTURA 7 min

Soften the d between vowels

When a d sits between two vowels (or at the end of a word), Spanish speakers don't hit it hard like the English d in “dog.” It softens almost to the th in English “this.” This matters all over your clothing vocabulary. Click each 🔊 and copy the soft join exactly:

  • el vesti·dosoft d — like “ves-thee-tho”
  • de mo·dathe d in moda barely touches
  • más bara·toglide más into barato, one unit

Tip: don't over-pronounce the d in vestido, moda, verde, usado. A light, soft d is the single fastest way to sound more natural — and it helps your Pronunciation score on the rubric.

The recipe for your comparison

You're not inventing anything new — you're following a frame. Each sentence stacks the same pieces you already know. Notice how ser and estar split the work:

OpenerFrameExample
Esta camisaes más … queEsta camisa es más bonita que esa.
Estos zapatosson menos … queEstos zapatos son menos caros que esos.
La camisetaes tan … comoLa camiseta es tan cómoda como el suéter.
Este ponchoes el más … deEste poncho es el más bonito del mercado.
La chaquetaestá (right now)La chaqueta está de moda y me queda bien.

ser = what it is: color, material, origin, price (es azul, es de algodón, es caro). estar = how it looks / fits / where it is right now (está de moda, está sucio, me queda bien). Pick the right one and your accuracy score takes care of itself.

Notice the frame: opener → ser (what it is) or comparison → estar (how it looks / fits now). Yours will use your own clothes — that's exactly what “Task Completion” on the rubric rewards.

🎤 TAREA ORAL · MÓDULO 6 · 20 PUNTOS · graba en Canvas
«Mi estilo y mis compras»

Record a 45–60 second spoken description, using Canvas media recording, in which you describe and compare either your favorite outfit or two items you'd buy at a market. Stay in the present tense. To earn full marks, include all of these:

  • at least five clothing or shopping words (la camisa, los zapatos, el mercado, el precio, la ganga…),
  • at least two comparisons (más… que, menos… que, tan… como),
  • at least one superlative (el/la más… de),
  • ser for what things are (color, material, price) and estar for how they look or fit (está de moda, me queda bien) — at least one of each.

Speak, don't read word-for-word — glance at your notes, then look up and talk. You may re-record as many times as you like; submit your best take.

🌎 CULTURA · BOLIVIA
El aguayo y los tejidos andinos

When you talk about clothes in Bolivia, you're talking about art. The aguayo (or awayu) is a rectangular hand-woven cloth of brilliant stripes that Aymara and Quechua women use to carry babies, food, or goods to the mercado. The weaving communities of Tarabuco and Jalq'a are so renowned that Bolivia recognizes their textile art as national heritage — each pattern encodes a village, a family, even a story. And no market outfit is complete without the pollera, the layered skirt worn proudly by cholitas, whose fashion has become a symbol of indigenous pride. So when you compare a suéter de alpaca to a factory shirt, remember: in the Andes, a garment can carry centuries of skill on its threads. ★ LA VIRTUD · HONESTY

Jaime Escalante (La Paz, Bolivia, 1930–2010) proved his students' honesty the hard way: when testing officials accused his 1982 Garfield High calculus class of cheating, most of the students retook the exam under supervision — and passed again, honestly earning what they'd already earned once. Today, when you record yourself speaking Spanish about clothing and comparisons, that same honesty is on the line: it has to be your own voice, your own words, your own work, not something borrowed. Speak with honestidad, and let your recording prove what you can really do.
